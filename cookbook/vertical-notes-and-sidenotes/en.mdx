## What you'll build

Four pages of a 四六判 edition of Higuchi Ichiyō's *Child's Play* (たけくらべ, 1895–96), its first chapter, with seven notes on the old Yoshiwara quarter it describes. The notes are 傍注 (bōchū), the sidenotes of Japanese classics in paperback: a small （1） stands at the right of the line after the word it explains, and the notes cited on both pages of a spread are set at the fore-edge of the left-hand page, under a rule a third of the line long, in type a fifth smaller. Readers find each note without turning a page. The text, 42 characters down 17 lines in Shippori Mincho at 9.25 pt, keeps Ichiyō's single long paragraph and her sesame marks over ぎつちよんちよん. Notes at the column foot in a European novel are <RecipeLink slug="novel-footnotes-column-foot">Nº 071</RecipeLink>.

## Method

### 1 · Gather the spread's notes at the left page's fore-edge

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `footnotes.placement: 'spread'` sets the notes cited on pages 2 and 3 at the end of page 3's text, which in a book bound on the right is its fore-edge, and `numbering: 'spread'` starts each spread's notes at （1） ([Footnotes](/en/docs/configuration#footnotes)). A note cited on the right-hand page waits for the left one; if the notes leave the left page less than one line of text, the rest stay at the foot of the right page, as JLReq allows. A document tagged `ja` already sets a vertical book's markers as `（{n}）` at the right of the line, 0.7 em, with digits upright: the two lines written out show it. `numberGap: 'em'` puts a full note-character after the number and `hangingIndent: em(2)` lines the turnovers up past it.

### 2 · Write the markers where Japanese puts them

The Markdown writes `見返り柳[^1]` after the word the note explains and the notes as `[^1]: …` paragraphs at the end of the chapter. The marker follows the word the note explains; one that ends a sentence goes before its 。 (`…先生[^1]。`), and the engine never lets 。 or a marker start a line. The seven notes were written for this recipe: what the 見返り柳 and the お齒ぐろ溝 were, where 大音寺前 lay, what was sold at the 酉の市, in plain modern Japanese.

### 3 · Keep the source's sesame marks

`cjk.emphasisMark` decides what `*…*` and `:dots[…]` print in this book: sesame dots, at the right of a vertical line. A `ja` document already uses them; written out, they turn on the kenten in the feature list. The Aozora text marks ぎつちよんちよん with 傍点, and `aozora.py` wrote it as `:dots[ぎつちよんちよん]{style=sesame}`; Chinese books put dots under the line instead ([Marks, ruby and warichu](/en/docs/configuration#marks-ruby-and-warichu)).

### 4 · Open on a spread

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The chapter's first sentence cites four notes in its first three lines. Set under a title on page 1, they would leave room for three lines of text: the left-hand page holds its own notes and nobody else's. A title page alone on page 1 moves the chapter to page 2, a right-hand page, so the first spread holds the text and all its notes. The title page is a level-1 heading whose design fills the page, anchored to the trim so the title stands on the page's axis.

### 5 · Put the hashira and folios down the fore-edge

<Excerpt region="furniture" />

The title runs down the outer margin of the left-hand pages, outside the notes, and the kanji folios at the foot of every page but the title page.

## Variations

### Gather the notes at the chapter's end

Without `placement`, a vertical `ja` book sets its notes as 後注 (kōchū): after the chapter's text, under the same rule, numbered through the chapter. Here they fill the left half of page 4.

```diff
-  placement: 'spread', // 傍注; 'chapterEnd' sets 後注 after the chapter instead
-  numbering: 'spread',
+  placement: 'chapterEnd', // 後注, the default for a vertical ja book
+  numbering: 'chapter',
```

### Set the marker beside the word

`markerPosition: 'side'` sets a smaller marker in the gap beside the line, as interlinear ruby is set, so it takes no room in the line at all.

```diff
-  markerPosition: 'right', markerTemplate: '（{n}）', // written out: 'ja' sets them so
+  markerPosition: 'side', markerTemplate: '{n}',
```
