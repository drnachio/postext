## What you'll build

A page from a yonkoma anthology, ねこ喫茶まどろみ (*Café Doze*), written for the recipe and drawn in a soft chibi style: Mugi runs a cat café with a white, a black and a calico cat. Yonkoma, four-panel strips, are the newspaper gag strip turned on its side: four equal panels stacked top to bottom, set up, develop, turn, punchline. A magazine page carries two of them side by side, and because the page opens from the right, the right-hand strip comes first. The page is A5, 148 × 210 mm, with the Japanese original on one side of the spread and the edition in this page's language on the other (English on the Japanese page).

## Method

### 1 · Split the page into two columns of four

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. The whole page is one `:::page` with `split="* [* / * / * / *] | * [* / * / * / *]"`: two equal columns, each cut into four equal tiers. The eight `::panel` lines fill the cells in reading order, so the first four belong to the first strip, and with right-to-left reading the first column is laid out on the right (<Feature id="comic-panel-split">page split</Feature>). The gutter between the strips is 9 mm and the gutter between panels 3 mm, so the eye goes down a strip before it crosses to the next.

### 2 · Title each strip in its first panel

<Excerpt region="balloons" />

A yonkoma has a title, and an anthology prints it in a box at the head of the strip. Here the title is a script line, `caption{title}: 朝ごはん`, and `title` is a balloon style of its own: white on black, flush against the panel border in the top start corner, which is the top right in this book. It is lettered in each edition like any other line, so the English page reads *Breakfast* and *Therapy* in the same boxes.

### 3 · Mark the cats as speakers too

<Excerpt region="art" />

The cats speak, `kuro: にゃ`, so each picture marks their mouths and faces as it marks Mugi's. Two small helpers keep the list short: `cat()` for a cat that faces the reader and `nose()` for one whose face is buried in a bowl or a cushion. In the third panel Mugi is out of the picture, and her line `mugi{tail=top}` gets a tail to the top edge.

### 4 · Load each edition's faces with its words

<Excerpt region="fonts" />

Each face gets the words it sets: Zen Antique the balloons, Dela Gothic One the sound effects (ガツガツ, じーっ, すやぁ, ずしっ). A yonkoma panel is wider than it is tall, so the Japanese columns stop at six characters (`maxColumnChars: 6`) and the lettering is half a point smaller than in a story page.

### 5 · Set the original beside each edition

<Excerpt region="editions" />

Each language of the site letters the strips in its own file, facing the Japanese original across the spread; the Japanese page faces the English edition. The order stays right strip first in every language.

## Pitfalls

- In a small panel a vertical balloon runs out of height before it runs out of width: the engine sets it in more, shorter columns, breaking between words. The office worker's last shout is still too tall for the bottom panel in Japanese, so it is marked `break` (`kyaku{shout break}`): it bursts up through the frame into the panel above instead of covering the cats, and stays out of the page margin.
- A large sound effect is kept off faces like a balloon, and its pinned point lies inside the panel's safe area, so every crop keeps it: THUMP sits at `70% 80%`, on the cats' paws. The Japanese ずしっ is set vertically and is taller; at that point it covered the calico's face and the build warned `comicBalloonOverflow`, so there it sits at `86% 84%`, beside her.
