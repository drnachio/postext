## What you'll build

Seven pages of a machine-learning preprint on US letter: Wei et al.'s *Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models*, abridged from its arXiv version under CC BY 4.0. The first page carries the paper's best-known figure, set as boxes rather than pasted as a picture: standard prompting on the left, chain-of-thought prompting on the right, each prompt in a box tabbed *Model Input* and each answer in a box tabbed *Model Output*, the exemplars in IBM Plex Mono and the chain of thought in bold colour, with a cross on the wrong answer and a tick on the right one. The text cites the way NeurIPS papers do, *(Brown et al., 2020)*, from the paper's own reference list rebuilt as BibTeX. Table 2 and two charts are redrawn from the numbers the paper publishes.

## Method

### 1 · Figure 1 is three box styles

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. The figure is one page-wide box with no frame of its own; inside it, `:::columns{count=2 breaks="4"}` opens the right column at the fourth block, and every nested box counts as one block. The prompt and answer boxes share one style factory, so they differ only in fill, tab colour and the badge on the corner: a callout `icon` with `position: 'corner'` takes an SVG resource, which is how the ✓ and ✗ reach the page in a document whose fonts have no such glyphs. In your own paper, write each exemplar as plain paragraphs inside a box and mark the reasoning with `**…**`; the box style's `boldColor` turns that into the highlight.

### 2 · Author–year citations, natbib style

<Excerpt region="citations" />

ML venues use natbib's author–year look: a comma between the names and the year, *et al.* from three authors on. Chicago author-date is the bundled style closest to it, so the recipe edits one group of its CSL, the one that joins author and year, from a space to a comma. Bring your own `.bib` file as it is: paste it into a `:::references{format=bibtex}` block, cite with `[@key]` for *(Brown et al., 2020)* and `@key` for *Brown et al. (2020)*. Works named only in a caption go into `nocite`, because captions are not scanned for citations.

### 3 · The title block

<Excerpt region="title" />

The title is the document's only level-1 heading, with a style that spans both columns and draws a designed block: venue line, title, authors in two rows, affiliation and a note on the abridgement. The authors and the note are heading attributes, and `\n` inside an attribute starts a new row, as `\\` does in a LaTeX `\author`. For your own paper, change the attributes; the design stays.

### 4 · Figure numbers that match the original

<Excerpt region="figures" />

The engine numbers figures in the order they are first cited, but Figure 1 here is a box, which the counter never sees. So the redrawn charts get a resource type each, with the number written into the label and an empty `numberingTemplate`, and keep the numbers of the arXiv version: Figure 2, Figure 4 and Table 2. In a paper of your own, where every figure is a resource, one `figure` type with `numberingTemplate: '{n}'` does the counting.

### 5 · Table 2 from the paper's data

<Excerpt region="table" />

The results are typed once, as the LaTeX source has them, and feed both Table 2 and Figure 4, so the chart cannot drift from the table. The benchmark names span two columns each; a merged cell needs a placeholder marked `hiddenBy` where it spreads. Bold marks the cells where chain-of-thought prompting beats standard prompting, as the green cells of the original do.

## Variations

### Cite in APA

APA writes *(Roy & Roth, 2015)* and lists up to twenty authors, which the bibliography of a large-model paper rarely wants.

```diff
-const citations = { style: 'custom', customStyle: natbib, link: true,
+const citations = { style: 'apa', link: true,
```

### Set the paper in one column

A NeurIPS-style single column needs wider side margins to keep a readable measure; the page-wide boxes then take the single column.

```diff
-const [TRIM_W, TRIM_H, TOP, BOTTOM, SIDE, GUTTER] = [215.9, 279.4, 22, 22, 19, 7];
+const [TRIM_W, TRIM_H, TOP, BOTTOM, SIDE, GUTTER] = [215.9, 279.4, 24, 24, 38, 7];
-  layout: { layoutType: 'double', gutterWidth: mm(GUTTER) },
+  layout: { layoutType: 'single' },
```

## Pitfalls

- A citation whose suffix has no locator, `[@brown2020language, inter alia]`, lost its comma in postext 1.18.0 and printed *2020inter alia*; inline marks in the suffix printed as asterisks. The recipe writes `[@brown2020language, , inter alia]`, which prints *2020, inter alia* in roman.
- A page-wide box in a two-column layout goes on the current page only when it fits with at least a couple of body lines below it. Figure 1 sits on page 1 because the abstract is set in 9.3 pt with tight padding; a longer abstract sends the figure to page 2.
