## What you'll build

Four pages of a diwan, the collected poems of al-Mutanabbī, as Cairo printers set them a century ago: a half-title with ديوان in red Ruqʿa, then the qaṣīda he recited before Sayf al-Dawla in 954, under a red-ruled headpiece. Each of the forty-six bayts is one line of two hemistichs, the ṣadr on the right and the ʿajuz on the left, both stretched with kashidas to one width, so the rhymes in ‑mu run down the left edge in a straight line. The book is bound on the right and the folios count in Arabic-Indic digits. Only the note on the text, at the end, changes with the edition. <RecipeLink slug="tang-poems-facing-english">Tang poems facing their English verse</RecipeLink> sets classical Chinese verse on its own grid.

## Method

### 1 · One bayt a line, both halves to one width

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. Inside `:::verse` each line is a bayt, and `||` parts the two hemistichs, so the Markdown reads like the Wikisource text it came from ([:::verse](/en/docs/document-format#verse)). The poem measures every hemistich, takes the widest half as the width of both columns (up to half the measure less the gap) and fills each half to that width, with kashidas first and the word spaces after ([Classical verse](/en/docs/arabic-layout#classical-verse)). The `bayt` style is set at 14 pt on a 26.6 pt pitch, 1.9 × the size, so the shaddas and kasras of adjacent lines do not meet ([Vowel marks](/en/docs/arabic-layout#vowel-marks)). The trim is 17 × 24 cm: at 14 × 21 cm a third of the bayts did not fit their half of the 104 mm measure and fell back to a staggered layout, ṣadr and ʿajuz on lines of their own.

### 2 · A headpiece band in Ruqʿa

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The level-1 heading is drawn by its design: a tinted box ruled in red above and below, the title centred over it in Aref Ruqaa, and the metre under it from a heading attribute, `{metre="من البحر الطويل"}`. Display type in Ruqʿa takes no kashida, so the title keeps its natural width ([Justification and kashida](/en/docs/arabic-layout#justification-and-kashida)).

### 3 · Running heads on the sheet’s sides

<Excerpt region="heads" />

The body text runs right to left, and its left and right mean start and end. Running heads stay physical. In a book bound on the right the verso, an even page, lies on the right of the spread, so its folio goes to the top right and the recto’s to the top left: both sit on the outer edge ([Page order and right binding](/en/docs/arabic-layout#page-order-and-right-binding)). The `locale: 'ar'` tag alone sets the direction, the binding and the Arabic-Indic digits.

### 4 · A half-title with nothing in the flow

<Excerpt region="title" />

The half-title is a heading style with `span: 'page'` and its own empty header and footer. Its heading text is the poet’s name, so the word ديوان is design text, set at 64 pt above it, and the Latin line under the rhyme comes from the `latin` attribute, which each edition writes in its language.

## Variations

### Mark the gap with an ornament

Some diwans print a small star between the hemistichs. `ornament` puts a mark in the middle of the gap without adding it to the text.

```diff
-:::verse{style="bayt"}
+:::verse{style="bayt" ornament="٭"}
```

### Give every hemistich the same width

A fixed `width` sets both columns to one measure on every page of the diwan, whatever the widest half of a given poem is.

```diff
-:::verse{style="bayt"}
+:::verse{style="bayt" width=58mm}
```
