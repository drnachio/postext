# The valley in March

The snow goes from the south slopes first, and for a week or two the orchards above the river stand on bare brown ground while the valley floor is still white. That is the time to walk the rows with a pair of secateurs and a notebook, before the buds break.

Most of the trees in this valley were planted by people who never ate from them at their best. The old standards on M25 and on seedling roots took ten years to crop well and then cropped for sixty. The small trees on M9 that now stand in rows below them will be in full bearing by their fifth summer and finished by their twenty-fifth. Neither is the right choice for every grower. A family that wants apples soon and has a small plot will plant dwarf trees and replant them; a farm that grazes sheep and thinks in generations will plant standards and leave them to the next owner.

What matters in either case is the ground under the tree and the air around it. A tree on the right slope, in soil that drains, on a rootstock that suits the place, will forgive a great deal of bad pruning.
