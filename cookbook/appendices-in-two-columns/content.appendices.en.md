# Appendix A · Rootstocks at a glance

Heights are those of a mature tree on average soil, kept by ordinary pruning. Spacing is between trees in the row; rows need at least a metre more than the height of the trees.

**M27.** The most dwarfing of the common stocks. A tree of a metre and a half to two metres, often in fruit in its second year. It needs a stake for life, rich soil and regular watering. Suited to pots and to a few trees in a garden, not to an orchard on poor ground. Spacing: one metre.

**M9.** Dwarfing, two to three metres. The stock of most modern orchards: early and heavy cropping, fruit of good size. Brittle roots that need a stake for life; sensitive to drought, woolly aphid and fireblight. Its roots can be killed in the hardest winters. Spacing: one and a half metres.

**Bud 9.** Raised at Michurinsk, in Russia. A tree of the size of one on M9 that stands much harder winters. Needs a stake and good soil, like M9. Spacing: one and a half metres.

**M26.** Semi-dwarfing, about three metres. Hardier than M9 and more anchored, though it needs a stake while young. Does badly in wet soil, where the collar rots. Spacing: two and a half metres.

**MM106.** Semi-vigorous, three and a half to four and a half metres. Resists woolly aphid, crops in its third or fourth year and stands on its own after the first years. Prone to collar rot on wet clay, and it grows late into autumn, which leaves the wood soft when the first hard frost arrives. Spacing: four metres.

**MM111.** Vigorous, four and a half to five and a half metres. Deep roots that tolerate drought and light soils; resists woolly aphid. Slow to crop but long-lived. Spacing: five metres.

**M25.** Very vigorous, six metres and more: a full standard tree for a grazed orchard. Crops after five or six years and lives for generations. Spacing: eight metres.

**Antonovka seedling.** Full size and the hardiest of all, grown from the seed of the Russian apple. Trees vary, as seedlings do. Spacing: eight metres.
