## What you'll build

Three pages of a paediatric journal, 170 × 240 mm, set in two justified columns of 66 mm in STIX Two Text: an original study of lung ultrasound for pneumonia with a numbered Vancouver reference list. A column that narrow gives the line breaker few choices, and in three places the English text breaks badly. A citation, *[2, 3]*, starts a line on its own, away from the word it supports, and twice the likelihood ratio *LR− = 0.09* splits after the equals sign. The first proof is set with `citationBreaks` and `operatorBreaks` at `'warn'`. It changes no break, marks each one with a pale amber band and lists them on a slip after the references. The second proof is set with `'avoid'`, and one of the three is left. The Spanish edition sets the same study with a decimal comma.

## Method

### 1 · A price on two kinds of break

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. A citation break is a line that starts with a numbered citation, or with the second half of one, such as *5]* after *[4,*. An operator break is a line that ends or starts on a sign set between spaces: `=`, `<`, `±`, `×`, the minus. `'warn'` reports both and leaves the lines as they are. `'avoid'` adds a penalty to the demerits of those breaks, and the Knuth–Plass breaker looks for another way to set the paragraph. It needs `optimalLineBreaking`, since the greedy breaker has no demerits to add to.

The default penalty, 10000, is what a line stretched to the word-spacing limit costs, so the breaker never opens a line past that limit to keep a citation with its word. In this column 10000 left two breaks in each edition. At 30000 the breaker takes looser lines, still within `maxWordSpacing`, and one break is left in each. A higher value changed nothing more: the last break has no alternative within the limit.

### 2 · Mark the breaks in the text

<Excerpt region="marks" />

Each warning carries its kind, the citation or the sign, the text of the two lines, the source position of the first one and the page. The script finds those two lines in the built document and wraps the break in `:mark[…]`: the word before a citation and the citation, or the sign and the number on its other side. A mark paints a band under the text and leaves the breaks where they were. The band does not reach the number the citation style generates, so on the page only the word before it is marked.

### 3 · Two proofs and a query slip

<Excerpt region="proofs" />

Each proof is built twice. The first build finds the breaks; the second sets the article with the marks and a slip after the references, so the slip falls on the last page. A typesetter returns proofs with queries in the same way. The slip's lines join their words with no-break spaces, so they add no breaks of their own. `capture.doc` publishes the two marked builds, `[1, 3]`.

<PageShot page={6} crop="0,0.62,1,0.78" caption="Second proof, page 213: the slip lists one break left in the article." />

The break left in English is *first* over *[2, 3]* on page 211. In Spanish the first proof finds four, among them *CP+* over *= 19,19* and *[4,* over *5]* where the citation itself splits, and the second proof keeps one, *anteriores [4,* over *5]*.

### 4 · Numbered citations in brackets

<Excerpt region="citations" />

The citation processor sets each `[@key]` as a bracketed number after a word space and collapses runs such as *[4–6]*. The space before the bracket and the space after a comma inside it are both places a line can break, which is why a narrow column with many citations needs the rule.

The heading style draws the journal band, the article type, the title, the authors with their affiliation numbers and a note, all from attributes on the H1. The two editions share the style, and the text stays in the Markdown.

## Variations

### Report the breaks and change nothing

A journal that checks its proofs by hand can keep the rule at `'warn'`: the Sandbox lists each break in Review, and the lines stay as the breaker set them.

```diff
-  citationBreaks: 'avoid', operatorBreaks: 'avoid',
+  citationBreaks: 'warn', operatorBreaks: 'warn',
```

### Keep the default penalty

At 10000 the spacing stays a little tighter and two breaks are left in each edition instead of one.

```diff
-  citationBreakPenalty: 30000, operatorBreakPenalty: 30000,
```

## Pitfalls

- The breaks are found in the lines as broken. A change of trim, size or text moves them, so read the warnings of each build rather than copying positions from an earlier one.
- To keep one citation with its word whatever the column, type a no-break space before it in the Markdown. The rule does the rest of the paper.
