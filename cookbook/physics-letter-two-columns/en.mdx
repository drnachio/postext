## What you'll build

Five pages of the letter that announced the first gravitational waves, *Observation of Gravitational Waves from a Binary Black Hole Merger* (Abbott et al., 2016), abridged and re-set on US Letter in two columns. Page 1 opens with the evidence: the strain recorded at Hanford and Livingston, redrawn from the open data files of the Gravitational Wave Open Science Center, runs across both columns under the title. Below it come the collaboration byline with its footnote, the abstract across the page and the text, with citations numbered [1,2] and [25–27] as the APS journals print them. The chirp mass is equation (1). Table I sets its asymmetric errors as stacked super- and subscripts, written as maths in the cells, and the captions print the letter's own formulas. Bring your own BibTeX and data files, and the same script sets your letter.

## Method

### 1 · Numbered physics citations from the IEEE file

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. postext-citeproc bundles no APS style, and the bundled `ieee` style is the nearest: citation order, numbers in brackets, initials before the family name. Four string replacements on `STYLES.ieee` close the gap. `collapse="citation-number"` joins runs (*[25–27]*), one pair of brackets on the layout prints *[1,2]*, `et-al-min` rises to eleven so a list of ten authors stays whole, and a new `<bibliography>` block prints the reference the way Physical Review does, with the journal, the volume in bold, the page and the year: *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **74**, 3515 (1995). The 43 works come in a `:::references{format=bibtex}` block of their own; a name in double braces such as `{{J. Aasi et al.}}` is printed as written, which is how the letter cites its own collaboration's long author lists. See [Citations and bibliography](/en/docs/document-format#citations-and-bibliography).

### 2 · Figure 1 under the title, across both columns

<Excerpt region="title" />

A float never stands above the line that cites it, and the text first cites Figure 1 in Section II, so as a float it could not reach page 1. The letter puts it there directly: `::resource{id="fig1"}` comes right after the title, and its placement, `{ position: 'here', span: 'page' }`, sets it across both columns at that point, with the columns going on under it. The byline and the abstract follow in a page-wide box, `front`, whose `:::paragraphs` blocks centre the byline and set the dates small. The footnote marker after *et al.* sends its note to the foot of the first column, as the APS journals print the author note, and `footnotes.numberFormat: 'lower-alpha'` letters the notes so they never read as references. See [Block embed](/en/docs/document-format#block-embed-optional-explicit-inline-placement) and [Footnotes](/en/docs/configuration#footnotes).

### 3 · Table I, with maths in its cells

<Excerpt region="table" />

A table cell sets `$…$` as the text does (since postext 1.19), so each value is written as TeX, `$36^{+5}_{-4}\,M_\odot$`, and its two errors stack over and under the number, scaled to the cell's line; Markdown's `^+5^~−4~` would print them one after the other. Captions and notes take the same maths: Figure 2 writes `$R_S = 2GM/c^2$` and Figure 4 `$5.1\sigma$`, set in MathJax's glyphs, which have the Greek that neither text face carries, and the PDF keeps them as vector paths. The resource types print *FIG. 1* and *TABLE I* in the captions and *Fig. 1* and *Table I* in the text: a `captionPrefix`, and `counterFormat: 'upper-roman'` for tables. See [Mathematical formulas](/en/docs/document-format#mathematical-formulas).

### 4 · Figures drawn from the open data

<Excerpt region="palette" />

The four figures are drawn in code from the data files GWOSC publishes with the letter, under CC BY 4.0. Each trace keeps every eighth sample, 430 points for the 0.21 s of Figure 1, and stored as one base-64 character per point for its change from the previous one, which keeps the five traces under 3 KB inside the script. The residual row is the data less the waveform, as published. Hanford is orange and Livingston blue in every figure, and the text keeps the page's ink and one blue. The labels are MathJax paths in its sans-serif face (`\textsf`), so an exponent or a symbol on an axis is set like the formulas of the text, and the PDF keeps every line and label as vectors.

## Variations

### Use the bundled IEEE style as it ships

Drop the edits and the references read *[2], [3], [4]* in the text and *A. Einstein, “title”, journal…* in the list.

```diff
-const citations = { style: 'custom', customStyle: aps, link: true,
+const citations = { style: 'ieee', link: true,
```

### Raise the numbers as Nature does

Nature's style sets superscript numbers and lists titles; it is bundled.

```diff
-const citations = { style: 'custom', customStyle: aps, link: true,
+const citations = { style: 'nature', link: true,
```

### Set it on A4

Change the trim; the measure, the columns and every figure follow from it.

```diff
-const [TRIM_W, TRIM_H, TOP, BOTTOM, SIDE, GUTTER] = [215.9, 279.4, 21, 21, 16, 7];
+const [TRIM_W, TRIM_H, TOP, BOTTOM, SIDE, GUTTER] = [210, 297, 22, 22, 15, 7];
```

## Pitfalls

- Up to postext 1.18 a `'here'` resource with `span: 'page'` was set one column wide in a two-column flow, and a `$` in a caption or a cell printed as typed. Pin 1.19 or later.
- A Greek letter, ⊙ or ≃ typed as a character in a caption or a cell falls back to a system face on screen and is missing from the PDF, which embeds the Latin files of each face. Write it as maths, `$\sigma$` or `$M_\odot$`, and draw it in figures.
- A list of hundreds of authors does not belong in a BibTeX file. Write the name as the journal prints it, in double braces: `author={{B. Abbott et al.}}`.
- Keep the licence line of the paper and of the data. Here a footnote and the colophon say what was cut, and each caption's note names the data and what the redrawn figure leaves out.
