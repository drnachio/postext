The opening pages of a trade paperback: a chapter that starts on a recto a third of the way down, under a small tracked kicker and a short rule in the book's one accent colour, then page after page of justified text with the book's title on the left and the chapter on the right. Nothing on the page shouts. The craft is in the geometry: margins that mirror across the spread, running heads that know which side they are on, and a folio that drops to the foot on the opener, where a running head would crowd the title.

### 1 · The opener is a design, not a heading

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. The first-level heading keeps its place in the text and the contents, but it draws itself from a slot of elements: the kicker comes from a heading attribute, the title wraps at a fixed measure, and the rule anchors below whatever height the title takes. `minHeight` sinks the body text to the same line on every opener, however long the title is.

### 2 · Running heads know which page they are on

<Excerpt region="running-heads" />

Each element is anchored to the physical page and filtered by parity, so the folio always sits on the outer edge and the book title on the verso. `pages: 'body'` keeps them off the opener, which gets a centred drop folio from the footer instead.

### 3 · One accent, linked everywhere

<Excerpt region="palette" />

Every colour in the config names its palette entry, so a reader who changes one swatch retints the whole book, and a part or a heading style can recolour a section by id. Pointing `main-color` at the accent catches every default the config does not restate.

A wider trim takes two columns; the opener spans both and the running heads stay where they are.
