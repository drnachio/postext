## What you'll build

Issue 12 of *Galley*, a type lab's journal, set on A5 in two columns of about forty characters, where a justified line has only five or six word spaces to take up the slack. The opener draws the Knuth–Plass model on a graphite band: a line of word boxes whose yellow springs of glue stretch to fill the measure. The essay explains why narrow columns go loose and prints the settings behind each rule in mono between its paragraphs. At the foot of page 3 a bench test sets one paragraph ragged, justified without hyphens and justified with them. The pen also sets the issue with first-fit breaking and the widow and orphan guards off, and shows both page 2s side by side above the pages, as on this recipe's card, with loose lines washed in highlighter yellow and lone lines and runts tagged in the margin.

## Method

### 1 · Tighten the fences, keep the guards

The code for this step is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. [Knuth–Plass](/en/docs/justification#knuth-plass-seeing-the-whole-paragraph), hyphenation, the widow, orphan and runt penalties and keep-with-next headings are on by default, so the published edition only narrows the [word-spacing bounds](/en/docs/justification#word-spacing-bounds) to 0.8 and 1.6 times a normal space (the defaults are 0.6 and 2). A line past the upper bound costs more than any hyphen or runt, so the breaker tries other breaks first; delete the two bounds and this essay sets two lines past 1.6. Hyphenation follows `locale`: `'es'`, `'fr'` and `'de'` get their own TeX patterns, a regional tag such as `'es-ES'` takes its language's, and a language with no patterns is hyphenated as American English, with a warning on the console.

### 2 · Mark loose lines from the layout tree

<Excerpt region="marks" />

`findLooseLines` returns the justified lines whose word spaces stretch past a threshold, here the upper bound, each with the band a highlight covers ([Loose lines in your own canvas](/en/docs/configuration#loose-lines-in-your-own-canvas)). `marks()` adds what that list leaves out, read from the [VDT](/en/docs/justification#loose-line-debugging): a line past 3× has no ratio, because the engine [sets it ragged](/en/docs/justification#lines-the-breaker-cannot-fill) and flags it `line.ragged` instead, and a paragraph split between two columns comes back as one block per fragment, which is how the lone lines are found. At forty characters the bounds alone do not keep every line under 1.6; while the essay was being copy-fitted, the marks showed which sentences to reword.

### 3 · Lay the control beside the published page

<Excerpt region="compare" />

Both editions share one design, so every difference between the two page 2s comes from the line breaker and its guards. In English the greedy control leaves 47 of its 110 justified lines past 1.6 and 30 past 2, three of them past 3× and set ragged by the engine, while the Knuth–Plass edition keeps all 105 between 0.80 and 1.60. On page 2 the control also carries a paragraph's last line alone to the head of a column, which `avoidOrphans: false` allows, and ends another paragraph on a runt.

### 4 · Give each setting a box of its own

<Excerpt region="bench" />

A box sets all the columns of a [`:::columns`](/en/docs/document-format#columns) group in one body style, so each slip is a nested box with a `body` of its own, and `breaks="3"` stacks the ragged and the unhyphenated slips in the first column. The ragged slip needs no `hyphenation: false`: ragged text keeps its words whole unless `bodyText.hyphenation.ragged` is switched on. The bench floats to the foot of page 3 with `placement: 'bottom'`, and its bottom padding repeats the 4 mm frame because the last slip's `marginBottom` is dropped at the end of the box.

### 5 · Draw the model into the opener

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The diagram is an [image element](/en/docs/configuration#image-elements) of the H1's design. As a figure it would be a page-wide `top` float, and a `top` float cited on page 1 opens page 2. The band reaches below the diagram, and an opener reserves height down to its lowest element, so the text starts under the band without a `minHeight`, a line further down because of the H1's default `marginBottom`. The labels are design text laid on the drawing's own coordinates, set and tracked like the opener's other labels.

## Variations

### Hyphenate a German text

The locale picks the patterns, so a German text takes `'de'`, or a regional tag such as `'de-AT'`.

```diff
-const locale = t({ en: 'en-us', es: 'es' }); // config().locale: each edition's patterns
+const locale = 'de'; // German patterns; 'de-AT' picks them too
```

### Mark only the worst lines

Raise the threshold from 1.6 to 2 and the highlighter marks only the lines whose spaces have more than doubled.

```diff
-  const loose = findLooseLines(doc, { threshold: FENCES.maxWordSpacing, pageIndex: page.index });
+  const loose = findLooseLines(doc, { threshold: 2, pageIndex: page.index });
```

## Pitfalls

- A box left at the end of a column drops to the column's foot when the columns are balanced, away from the paragraph it belongs to. Fit the copy so the column is full, as the settings box under “Fences for the glue” needed, or set `headings.balancing.closingBox` to `'off'`, which never moves the box.
- The unhyphenated slip does not show the widest gaps it would need. A justified line whose spaces would stretch past three times a normal space is set ragged at its natural spacing, in a box as in the body text, so the slip's first two lines end short of the measure. The lines under that cap stay justified, and theirs are the wide gaps the slip shows.
