## What you'll build

Two pages of a research note, 号数活字の三つの系列 (“Three series of gōsū type sizes”), from an invented journal of type and typesetting, set as Japanese academic societies set their journals: A4, two columns of 25 characters by 45 lines of Noto Serif JP at 9 pt, the title in Shippori Mincho B1 on the journal’s green band. The citations follow SIST 02, the guide to references that Japanese science and technology journals use: each work is numbered in the order it is first cited, (1), (2, 3), and the list under 参考文献 gives Japanese names family first, the place before the publisher and the year last. A table of the nine type sizes, a figure drawing them to scale and two page notes come with Japanese labels. <RecipeLink slug="gbt7714-chinese-paper">The Chinese paper cited to GB/T 7714</RecipeLink> is its Chinese counterpart.

## Method

### 1 · The style, the locale and the notes

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `postext-citeproc` ships SIST 02 as `'sist02'` and the CSL locale `ja-JP`, which writes the list’s terms in Japanese and joins two Japanese authors with と and more with ほか in a narrative citation ([Citations](/en/docs/configuration#citations)). In the text, `[@kawada1949; @suzuki2015]` prints (2, 3), and `高橋[@takahashi2012]が` puts the number after the name, 高橋(8)が. The style differs from the Chinese GB/T 7714 in the marker: SIST 02 sets the number in parentheses on the line, GB/T 7714 in square brackets raised. The notes need nothing: `locale: 'ja'` gives a horizontal document notes at the foot of the column, numbered from 1 on each page, with a superscript marker and a rule a third of the column long.

### 2 · The references in a CSL-YAML block

The works are listed at the end of the Markdown in `:::references{format=csl-yaml}`, the format Zotero exports as CSL YAML ([Citing](/en/docs/document-format#citing)). Japanese names go in as `family` and `given`, `{family: 鈴木, given: 広光}`, and SIST 02 prints them solid, 鈴木広光, while Kornicki keeps his comma: Kornicki, Peter. A book series goes in `collection-title` and prints in parentheses after the year; the two articles from 日本印刷学会誌 give volume, issue and pages as vol. 49, no. 2, p. 76–80. Every work is real; their entries can be checked in CiNii.

### 3 · The title on the journal’s band

<Excerpt region="masthead" />

The title block is the design of the H1, a heading style across both columns. The band is a box anchored to the trim; the label, the title and its English translation sit on it, each placed under the one before, so a three-line title pushes the English line down. The author and her affiliation come from heading attributes and sit under the band.

### 4 · The abstract and its labels

<Excerpt region="abstract" />

The abstract is a callout across the page under a green top stripe, its labels chips in bold gothic in the journal’s colour. An ideographic space after each label, typed in the Markdown, keeps 要　旨 apart from the text.

### 5 · 表1 and 図1 where they are cited

<Excerpt region="resources" />

`defaultResourceTypes('ja')` gives 図 and 表; the paper numbers them through, 表1 and 図1, not by chapter, so the template is `'{n}'`. Both are placed `'here'`, under the paragraph that cites them, inside the column. The squares of the figure are drawn at the size of the type they stand for, 42 pt for 初号 down to 4 pt for 八号, so the halving can be seen on the page.

## Variations

### Superscript citation numbers

Many Japanese journals raise the number above the line. `marker: 'superscript'` prints it raised, with no parentheses, and leaves the list alone.

```diff
   locale: 'ja-JP',
+  marker: 'superscript',
```

### Notes numbered through the paper

A paper of a few pages may count its notes from 1 to the end instead of per page.

```diff
-const footnotes = { fontSize: pt(7.5), lineHeight: pt(11.5), color: col('ink'),
+const footnotes = { numbering: 'document', fontSize: pt(7.5), lineHeight: pt(11.5),
+  color: col('ink'),
```

## Pitfalls

- Write a Japanese name as `family` and `given`, or as one `literal` with no space. A name typed as one string with a space between family and given name, as BibTeX often carries it (`author = {夏目 漱石}`), is read family first, but a romanised one, `Natsume Soseki`, is read as given name then family name, as English names are.
- The bundled SIST 02 prints no DOI; an article whose DOI matters needs it in the text or a note.
