:::frame{slot="blurb"}
:::paragraphs{style="cover-blurb-first"}
While a plague empties his country, Prince Prospero shuts himself up with a thousand of his courtiers in a fortified abbey, welds the gates behind them and gives a masked ball in a suite of seven rooms, each hung in one colour and lit through stained glass by a brazier. In the last room, black with windows the colour of blood, stands a clock of ebony that stops the music every hour.
:::

:::paragraphs{style="cover-blurb"}
Poe published the tale in *Graham’s Magazine* in May 1842, a month after “Life in Death”, the first version of “The Oval Portrait”. In that one a wounded traveller spends the night in an abandoned château in the Apennines and finds, by candlelight, the portrait of a young woman painted by her husband. The two tales run to some twenty pages together, and each takes place in a single night.
