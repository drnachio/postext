## What you'll build

Worksheet 7 of a Year 4 science and maths course, *Water on the move*, in ten editions. The questions are the same in every language: choose an answer, tick true or false, fill the gaps from a word bank, join words to their meanings, circle a word, divide the water in a tank, explain why to save it. Each edition prints them as its schools do. The Spanish and Catalan sheets put the number in a disc, tick V or F and divide with the divisor in an L to the right of the dividend. The French one uses squares, *cases à cocher*, dotted blanks and Seyès paper for the answer. The German one writes *R* and *F*, *Lückentext* and `864 : 4 =` on one line, the multiplication with a dot. The Brazilian one asks for V or F in brackets before the statement. The English one divides under the ⟌ bracket. The Chinese sheet numbers its sections 一、二、, puts （　） after each stem with A. B. C. in a row, asks for √ or × and ends with 看拼音，写词语 on two 田字格 squares for each word. The Japanese one offers ア イ ウ and ○ ×, the Arabic one runs right to left with أ ب ج and صح or خطأ.

## Method

### 1 · Put each tradition in a table

<Excerpt region="answer" />

Every edition has the same Markdown: seven `:::exercise` fences whose answers are in the text (`- [x]`, `[[vapour]]`, `- Evaporation -> Water turns into vapour`, `- 864 ÷ 4`). What a country's books do differently is form, so it lives in this table and nowhere else: the number's shape and numerals, the option labels and whether the pupil ticks a box or rings a letter, the true/false look, the blank's look, the writing area and the layout of long division. Catalan takes the Spanish row as it is.

### 2 · Lay the tradition over the house style

<Excerpt region="style" />

One exercise style, `ficha`, carries what all ten sheets share (the blue of the numbers, the grey rules, the room between exercises), and the edition's row is spread over it. What the row leaves out comes from the language: T F, V F, R F, √ ×, ○ × and صح خطأ are the engine's own words, as are a) b) c), A. B. C., ア イ ウ and أ ب ج, so no edition writes them.

### 3 · Give each script its faces

<Excerpt region="faces" />

The Latin editions share Nunito and Baloo 2. The Chinese sheet sets its text in Song and its instructions in Hei; the Japanese one sets the text in Klee One, a textbook face, and the instructions in a gothic; the Arabic one uses Noto Naskh Arabic. Klee One's heaviest cut is 600, so the Japanese edition sets its bold at 600 (`boldFontWeight`): a 700 would be drawn by the browser and fail the capture.

### 4 · Head the sheet with lines for a name

<Excerpt region="head" />

The level-1 heading draws the band, the course from the frontmatter and the title, and two ruled fields whose words come from the heading's attributes (`nombre="Name" fecha="Date"`), so the German sheet says *Datum* and the Japanese one 名前 without a string in the code.

## Variations

### Print the teacher's copy

Every answer goes into its slot in magenta: the brackets, the boxes, the blanks and the quotient.

```diff
   exerciseStyles: [ficha],
+  exercises: { edition: 'teacher' },
```

### Collect the answers at the foot of the sheet

`placement: 'after'` sets each exercise's key under it; `'inverted'` turns it upside down, as puzzle pages do.

```diff
+  exercises: { solutions: { placement: 'inverted', layout: 'key' } },
```

## Pitfalls

- A face without a 700 cut makes the bold of the stems' numbers a synthesized one. Give such a text face `boldFontWeight` with a weight it ships.
- The ○ of the Japanese instructions and the √ of the Chinese ones are only in the CJK faces: set them in a sentence of the CJK text, not in a Latin label.
