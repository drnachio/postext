## What you'll build

A chapter of an invented handbook of Japanese typesetting, 組版の言葉 (The Words of Typesetting), and its index. The chapter explains the names of the parts of a page, of type sizes and of punctuation rules, on an A5 page of 36 characters by 28 lines; each term is marked for the index where it is explained. The index is the subject: Japanese has no alphabetical order for kanji, so an index files each entry by its reading in kana, in the order of the gojūon (五十音) table, under row heads あ行, か行, さ行. A term with furigana gives its reading by itself; any other term with a kanji says it in a `yomi` attribute. <RecipeLink slug="chinese-name-index">The Chinese name index</RecipeLink> sorts Chinese names by pinyin and by strokes instead.

## Method

### 1 · A reading for every term with a kanji

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. In the text, `:index[{版面|はん|めん}]{main}` prints 版面 with はんめん over it and files the entry as はんめん; `:index[明朝体]{yomi="みんちょうたい" main}` prints no reading and files it as みんちょうたい. Terms in kana, ノンブル or のど, and Latin terms, DTP or JIS X 4051, need nothing ([Index marks](/en/docs/document-format#index-marks)). The reading decides only the order: the entry prints as written. Kanji readings cannot be guessed, since 天 is てん here and あめ elsewhere, so the engine never tries.

### 2 · JIS order and row heads

`groupBy: 'gojuon'` is what `'auto'` picks for `locale: 'ja'`. The entries sort in the order of JIS X 4061: katakana as hiragana, small kana as large, a voiced kana right after its plain one, ー as the vowel before it. So 級 (きゅう) comes before 行取り (ぎょうどり), and ゴシック体 (ごしっくたい) after 小口 (こぐち). Latin terms come first, under their letters, as JIS orders them; symbols and digits would come before them ([Back-of-book index](/en/docs/configuration#back-of-book-index)).

### 3 · → and も見よ

A see reference is a mark with `see`, `:index{term="振り仮名" yomi="ふりがな" see="ルビ"}`, and prints 振り仮名　→ルビ. A see-also reference uses `seealso` and prints the targets before も見よ, 級　10　→歯も見よ, as Japanese indexes word it. `see: { italic: false }` keeps the labels upright, since a Japanese face has no italic. The ideographic space after each term and the 、 between pages come from `separator` and `locatorSeparator`.

### 4 · The index across two columns

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The index heading is a heading style with `span: 'page'` and a two-column `layout`: its design, a pale band with the title, spans the page, and `:::index` flows in two columns under it. The row heads are set in the vermilion gothic, the entries in the text’s mincho at 8.5 pt on a 14 pt line.

## Variations

### Heads by the first kana

`'kana'` heads each group with the entry’s first kana, as dictionaries and long name indexes do: か, き, く instead of one か行.

```diff
-  groupBy: 'gojuon', // what 'auto' picks in a 'ja' document: heads あ行 か行 さ行 …
+  groupBy: 'kana', // heads か き く け こ…
```

### A reading without furigana in the text

To keep a term’s first mention bare and still give its reading, move the reading to `yomi`.

```diff
-ページのうち、本文が組まれる範囲を:index[{版面|はん|めん}]{main}という。
+ページのうち、本文が組まれる範囲を:index[版面]{yomi="はんめん" main}という。
```

## Pitfalls

- A term with a kanji and no reading raises `indexReadingMissing`. Without its `yomi`, 明朝体 dropped to the end of the index, after わ行, with no head, and the build warned that the entry “has a kanji and no reading”.
- `:ruby[…]` inside `:index[…]` needs its brackets escaped, because the mark ends at the first `]`; write the compact form, `{版面|はん|めん}`.
- A ruby reading counts only when it covers every kanji of the term: `:index[行{取|ど}り]` leaves 行 unread and warns. `{行取|ぎょう|ど}り` gives ぎょうどり.
