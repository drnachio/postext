## What you'll build

The puzzle page of *Splash!*, a children's magazine about the sea, on two A4-sized pages in two columns. A reef painting fills the top of the first page with the title over its open water. Below it, each puzzle sits on a cream card with a coral number: a crossword built from six sea creatures and their clues, a word search that hides eight words across, down, diagonally and backwards, a riddle with three options to ring, a turtle's life to put in order, a mixed-up fact to unscramble and baby animals to join with their names. Under every puzzle, inside its card, the answers are printed upside down in small grey type, the way puzzle magazines have always kept them out of sight.

## Method

### 1 · Every puzzle on a card, its answers turned

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. The exercise style's `box` names a box style, so every puzzle is set on a card, its number in a disc and its title as the first line of its instruction. `placement: 'inverted'` sets the key after each puzzle in a box turned 180°, inside the card, so the two never fall on different pages; the grids and the writing line stay inside the card's padding.

### 2 · Let the engine build the grids

The Markdown holds only the words: `- OCTOPUS: Eight arms and three hearts` for the crossword, `- SHELL` for the word search. The engine lays the crossword with every word crossing another and numbers the starts in reading order, and hides the search words in the directions the fence names (`directions="across down diagonal backwards"`), filling the rest with the words' own letters. Both are seeded, so the grid is the same on every run, in every output, and the Spanish edition builds its own grid from its own words.

### 3 · A reef across both columns

The opener spans both columns (`span: 'page'`), so the puzzles start under the painting in each column. The picture is cut to the band's proportions and anchored to the page, so it bleeds at the top and the sides; the standfirst sits in a dark box so it reads over the water.

## Variations

### Hide the word list

A harder word search prints only the grid: add `hidden` to the fence.

```diff
-:::exercise{type=wordsearch size=9 directions="across down diagonal backwards"}
+:::exercise{type=wordsearch size=9 directions="across down diagonal backwards" hidden}
```

### Print the answers at the end of the magazine

`chapterEnd` collects every key at the end instead of turning each one.

```diff
-  solutions: { placement: 'inverted', box: 'turned' },
+  solutions: { placement: 'chapterEnd' },
```

## Pitfalls

- A crossword word that crosses no other is left out and reported as `exerciseWordUnplaced`. Choose words that share letters, or add one that links two groups.
- A puzzle card is kept together, so a tall crossword moves to the next column when it does not fit: check the Spanish edition, whose words are longer.
- Balancing is on (`headings.balancing.enabled`), so the closing page is cut level: the last cards share its two columns instead of filling the first one and leaving the second almost empty. Keep the cards of the last page close in height, or one tall card still ends its column alone.
