## What you'll build

A slim reader of 紅樓夢 (*Dream of the Red Chamber*) that reprints, from chapters 1 to 3, the passages where the cast first appears, and ends with an index of its 47 names, plus four cross-references from other names. It comes in two editions from the same marks. The mainland one, in Simplified characters, files the names under their pinyin initial, F, H, J…; the Taiwan one, in Traditional characters, under the stroke count of the first character, 三畫, 四畫… Both are 大32开 paperbacks, 140 × 203 mm, 26 characters to the line in Noto Serif, with the chapter titles in LXGW WenKai and the heads in Noto Sans. The card lays the two index pages side by side. <RecipeLink slug="names-and-subjects-indexes">An index of names and an index of subjects</RecipeLink> builds the same kind of index for an English monograph.

## Method

### 1 · Mark each name where the reader meets it

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above; the rest is in the Markdown. A mark files the full name whatever the text prints: `字:index[士隐]{term="甄士隐" main}` prints 士隐 and files a page under 甄士隐. `main` makes the page bold, and the note under the index title says what bold means here: the page where the character enters in person. Jia Baoyu is born in chapter 2 without being named (又生了一位公子), so the mark goes in with nothing to print, `:index{term="贾宝玉"}`, and his entry reads 4, **6**. Other names and nicknames get a cross-reference of their own, `:index[凤辣子]{term="凤辣子" see="王熙凤"}`, which Postext prints the Chinese way, after a full stop and with no space: 凤辣子。见王熙凤 ([Index marks](/en/docs/document-format#index-marks)).

### 2 · Let the locale choose the order

<Excerpt region="editions" />

The two editions share the configuration, and `locale` is the only switch the index needs: `groupBy: 'auto'` groups a `zh-Hans` index by pinyin and a `zh-Hant` one by strokes, and the short answer writes the two values out so you can see them. The entries sort in the order the heads come from, so the same names change places: 甄费 comes before 甄士隐 under Z (fèi before shì), and after it under 十四畫 (士 has three strokes, 費 twelve); 宁国公 and 荣国公 part for N and R, while 寧國公 and 榮國公 stay together among the fourteen-stroke names ([Back-of-book index](/en/docs/configuration#back-of-book-index)). The readings and stroke counts are the browser's Chinese collation; a surname it reads wrong (曾, 单, 解) takes a `sort` key in characters with the right reading.

### 3 · Load each face with the characters it sets

<Excerpt region="voices" />

Fontsource cuts each Chinese weight into about a hundred files, and `loadCjkFonts` fetches the ones its text touches. Each voice gets only its own text: the Song face the whole book, its bold the digits of the principal pages, the Hei face the headings and the index heads, the Kai face the three couplets and 人名索引. LXGW WenKai TC holds the Simplified characters too, but it centres its commas and full stops as Taiwan books do, so the Simplified edition gives it titles only, which carry no punctuation ([Chinese, Japanese and Korean fonts](/en/docs/configuration#chinese-japanese-and-korean-fonts)).

### 4 · Put the note in the index opener

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The note is an attribute of the heading, `# 人名索引 {style="index" kicker="按汉语拼音排列" note="…"}`, set across the whole measure above the two columns. As a paragraph inside the columns it was a lever for column balancing: to even out the Traditional edition's last page, the layout loosened it to twelve characters a line in a column of fourteen. Design text has a line breaker of its own and sets every mark full width, so keep such a note short, and check that none of its lines starts with a mark. The chapter openers use the same design, with `第{numberHan}回` as the kicker, which prints 第一回 in the script of the document.

### 5 · Show both index pages side by side

<Excerpt region="side-by-side" />

The pen builds both editions and shows the Simplified one on the desk; the 简体版 and 繁體版 buttons switch the desk, and the PDF is the edition on it. The capture records the Simplified build, so the pages above are that edition's, and the card is this strip with the first index page of each.

## Variations

### Group a Traditional index by pinyin

A Traditional edition for readers who look names up by their reading can file them by pinyin. Set `groupBy: 'pinyin'` in both editions: the heads are A–Z and the entries keep their Traditional forms.

```diff
-  groupBy: e.locale === 'zh-Hans' ? 'pinyin' : 'stroke', // A B C… or 三畫 四畫…
+  groupBy: 'pinyin', // A B C… in both scripts
```

### Print the names without heads

A short list of names reads well with a blank line between groups and no head over them.

```diff
-  groupBy: e.locale === 'zh-Hans' ? 'pinyin' : 'stroke', // A B C… or 三畫 四畫…
+  groupBy: 'none', // one list, groups set apart by groups.marginTop
```

## Pitfalls

- The `index` configuration belongs to the document, so one document prints one order. A book that wants both a pinyin index and a stroke finding list builds twice, as this pen does.
- Chinese paragraphs do not yet pull a lone character back from their last line: `avoidRunts` acts on Latin text only, and 哀。 on page 1 and 人。” on page 3 stand alone.
