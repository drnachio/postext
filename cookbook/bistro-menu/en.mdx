## What you'll build

The autumn card of Les Tanneurs, an imaginary Paris bistro: one sheet, 230 × 310 mm, printed on both sides. On the front, a striped awning hangs over the name, set in Limelight. Each course head stands between two brass rules that run out to the margins, with four dishes below it. A dish is its French name in bold with the garnish, an English line in italic beneath it and the price in bold, flush right. A dark green V disc marks the vegetarian dishes and an outlined SG pill the gluten-free ones. Each dish is one paragraph, and a tab sends its price to a stop at the right margin. On the back, the wine list sits under a shelf of bottles, with the glass and bottle prices in columns headed 12 cl and 75 cl, in a table with no rules and no caption.

## Method

### 1 · A tab stop where the price goes

The code is in [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. Each course is a `:::paragraphs{style="dish"}` group, one paragraph to a dish ([Paragraph styles](/en/docs/configuration#paragraph-styles)). The dish style has one tab stop, at `'end'`, the end of the measure, with `align: 'end'`: the text after a tab ends on the stop. So `:tab **7**` after the garnish and the badges sends the price flush right, however long the dish ([Tabs and tab stops](/en/docs/document-format#tabs-and-tab-stops)). `:tab` takes the spaces around it, and the backslash at the end of the line is a forced line break, so the English line in italics starts a second line of the same paragraph and the price stays on the first. `spaceBetween` puts half a line between two dishes, so each dish takes two and a half lines of the 14.5 pt leading. `indent` and `endIndent` of a quarter line keep the dishes in line with the text of the wine list's cells on the back. A tabbed paragraph is set line by line; here every line is set ragged anyway.

### 2 · One resource type, in place and never numbered

<Excerpt region="type" />

The built-in figure and table types print a caption line and float each resource to the first free slot after its first reference. The `menu` type has no caption prefix and its pieces have no caption, so nothing prints under the drawings or the wine list. Give it `captionPrefix: 'Menu'` and a numbered line prints under every piece, starting with “Menu 1.” under the awning, which takes the card to four pages. `defaultPlacement` sets the wine list and each drawing at its `::resource` line ([Block embed](/en/docs/document-format#block-embed-optional-explicit-inline-placement)). Without it the wine list floats to the foot of the back, under Le Comptoir and the colophon. The awning and the bottles are SVG resources of the same type, drawn in code and registered with the kit's `loadSvg`.

### 3 · Merge the region rows

<Excerpt region="wines" />

The wine list stays a table: its two prices stand in columns under their heads, 12 cl and 75 cl, which a row of tab stops would leave without a head. `parseTSV(tsv, { headerRows: 1 })` reads the first line as the head row, so Appellation · cépage, 12 cl and 75 cl print over their columns in the header face, Josefin Sans. A line with a single field names a region. Its cell becomes a centred header cell, and `mergeCells` spreads it across the three columns and marks the two cells it covers `hiddenBy` ([Building table models](/en/docs/configuration#building-table-models)). Centred in the first column alone, BLANCS would stand 22.6 mm left of the axis that La Cave and Le Comptoir are centred on. A blank line in the TSV becomes a row of empty cells, one and a half lines tall, and that row is the gap above Blancs and Rouges.

### 4 · Hang a rule on each side of the course title

<Excerpt region="heads" />

A course head is a design in the column. The `{titleText}` element has no width, so its box is as wide as its capitals, and the `'top'` anchor centres it on the column. Each rule hangs off one edge of the title with `'left-of'` or `'right-of'` and fills to the column's edge ([Element placement](/en/docs/configuration#element-placement)), so the rules beside PLATS are 66.7 mm long and those beside LE COMPTOIR 56.2 mm, each 4 mm from the letters. The title's `lineHeight` is a multiple, 0.96, which makes its box 14.4 pt and keeps it inside one 14.5 pt grid line. At 1 the box is 15 pt, each head takes two lines and the card runs to four pages.

### 5 · Badges as chips, small print as paragraph styles

<Excerpt region="badges" />

A chip draws a box round a run of text inside the line ([Chip styles](/en/docs/configuration#chip-styles)), so `:chip[V]{style="veg"}` typed after the garnish puts the disc on the dish's French line, before the tab. The chip styles set `bold: true` because a chip takes the weight of the text around it, 400 after a garnish, and `FONTS` loads Josefin Sans in 700 only. The notes under the desserts and the colophon on the back are `:::paragraphs` blocks at 9 and 7.5 pt, and both styles carry a `marginTop` of one line ([Paragraph styles](/en/docs/configuration#paragraph-styles)). Without that margin the notes start on the line after the cheese plate's English line, as if they belonged to the dish.

### 6 · Centre the card on one axis

<Excerpt region="centred" />

`headings.textAlign` and `bodyText.textAlign` put everything but the dishes and the wine list on the card's centre line. `lineHeight: pt(3 * LEAD)` gives the 48 pt name three grid lines. Without it the name takes the default leading and four lines, the second line of notes moves onto a page of its own and the card runs to four pages. Level 1 turns the H1 break off, `breakBefore: { enabled: false }`, because the name and La Cave belong on the page of the drawing above them. Leave the default, `{ enabled: true, parity: 'always-odd' }`, and each drawing stands alone on a page, seven pages in all. The config sets no `locale`: nothing on this card is justified, and ragged text is hyphenated only with `bodyText.hyphenation.ragged` on.

## Variations

### Stop the rules at a fixed length

A fixed width in place of `'fill'` keeps each rule 20 mm long and 4 mm from the title, so the pair sits closer to the centre beside PLATS than beside LE COMPTOIR.

```diff
-    size: { width: 'fill' }, // to the column's edge
+    size: { width: mm(20) }, // 20 mm, wherever the title ends
```

### Run dots to the price

A leader fills the room before the stop. `'. '` repeats a full stop and a space, from half an em after the dish's last badge or word to half an em before its price.

```diff
-  tabStops: [{ position: 'end', align: 'end' }],
+  tabStops: [{ position: 'end', align: 'end', leader: '. ' }],
```

### Rule the wine list like a ledger

`rules: 'horizontal'` draws a 0.5 pt brass hairline over and under every row of the wine list. Rules take no room, so both sides keep their layout, and the blank rows above Blancs and Rouges get a pair of hairlines of their own.

```diff
-const tableStyle = { rules: 'none', cellPadding: pt(LEAD / 4), // a row: 1½ lines of LEAD
+const tableStyle = { rules: 'horizontal', borderColor: col('brass'),
+  borderWidth: pt(0.5), cellPadding: pt(LEAD / 4),
```

## Pitfalls

Leave room for the price on the dish's first line. When the dish and the garnish run past the place the price needs, the stop takes the dish's last word down to the next line with the price, and the English line comes after them: the dish then takes three lines and the course ends a line lower.
