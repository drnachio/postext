# The Postext Cookbook: contributor guide

The Cookbook (*Recetario* in Spanish) is the gallery of complete Postext examples at
[postext.dev/en/cookbook](https://postext.dev/en/cookbook). Each entry is a **recipe**: a real
publication set with Postext, with its pages, the code to copy, a CodePen, a `.html` download,
often a PDF, and a short write-up that explains the method and the pitfalls.

This guide is for the people and AI agents who write recipes. The lint and the capture
enforce most of its rules. When a rule here and a tool disagree, the tool is right and this
guide needs a fix.

**Contents:** 1 What a recipe is · 2 Folder anatomy · 3 Commands · 4 `recipe.json` ·
5 The pen · 6 The design bar · 7 Never the default skin · 8 Content and licences ·
9 The write-up · 10 Registries · 11 Capture and checks · 12 Review · 13 For AI agents

## 1. What a recipe is

- **A publication.** A recipe sets real pages: a magazine opener, a textbook
  page with margin notes, a poem sequence, a print-ready PDF. The pages are judged before
  anyone reads the code.
- **One subject.** It teaches one to three features (`features.primary`) and
  answers at least one reader question (`answers`). Everything else on the page stays quiet
  and correct.
- **Runs on its own.** The composed `script.js` runs in CodePen, in a downloaded `.html`
  file or pasted into any `<script type="module">`, with no build step.
- **Honest about gaps.** When Postext cannot do something (line numbers, pipe tables, three
  body columns), the recipe shows the workaround and says so; it never fakes the feature.
- **Permanent.** A recipe has a catalogue number (Nº) that is never reused and an English
  slug that never changes (renames go through `formerSlugs`).
- **Bilingual where it can be.** The site is in English and Spanish; both write-ups are
  required, and most samples come in both languages.

A recipe is **not** a feature demo on placeholder text, a page in the engine's default look,
or a copy of a third-party publication.

**Chapters.** Every recipe belongs to exactly one chapter, the one of *what it teaches*
(`cookbook/_registry/taxonomy.json`). Genre is a separate facet: a magazine *opener* belongs
in Headings; a whole magazine *issue* belongs in Complete publications.

| Part | Nº | Chapter id | English | Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I · The page | 1 | `page` | Page & grid | Página y retícula |
| | 2 | `type` | Type & text | Texto y tipografía |
| | 3 | `headings` | Headings & openers | Títulos y aperturas |
| II · The book | 4 | `running-heads` | Running heads & folios | Cabeceras y folios |
| | 5 | `structure` | Book structure | Estructura del libro |
| | 6 | `boxes` | Boxes & notes | Recuadros y notas |
| | 7 | `figures` | Figures & images | Figuras e imágenes |
| | 8 | `tables` | Tables | Tablas |
| III · In practice | 9 | `publications` | Complete publications | Publicaciones completas |
| | 10 | `output` | Output & integration | Salida e integración |

A recipe in `publications` composes at least eight features over at least four pages.

## 2. Folder anatomy

```
cookbook/
├── README.md                 this guide
├── recipe.schema.json        JSON Schema of recipe.json (pnpm cookbook schema)
├── _roadmap.json             the planned line-up: Nº, chapter, order and coverage of each
│                             planned recipe (read by `new` and `coverage`)
├── _registry/*.json          bilingual vocabularies: taxonomy, features, apis, config,
│                             questions, gaps, warnings, gotchas, collections (§10)
├── _kit/*.js                 helpers every pen shares, inlined by composition (§5.4)
├── _template/                what `pnpm cookbook new` copies
└── <slug>/                   one folder per recipe; the folder name is the slug
    ├── recipe.json           metadata (§4)
    ├── en.mdx  es.mdx        the write-ups (§9); both are required
    ├── script.js             the pen, with markers (§5)
    ├── content.en.md         the sample document, one file per sample language
    ├── [content.es.md]
    ├── [content.<slot>.<lang>.md]   named slots, e.g. the chapters of a book
    ├── [index.html] [style.css]     only for recipes with live controls
    ├── [pen.json]            extra CodePen tags or scripts (never fonts)
    └── [assets/]             pictures the pen fetches; file names are immutable

apps/web/public/cookbook/<slug>/     GENERATED by `pnpm cookbook capture`, committed
├── capture.json                     what the capture measured and verified
└── <lang>/p01.webp p01.s.webp …     pages (1000 px and 240 px wide)
    card.webp card.480.webp og.jpg   gallery card and social image
    [<slug>.pdf]                     when downloads.pdf is set
    <slug>.postext                   the document, for Open in Sandbox
```

Slugs are English kebab-case outcome phrases of two to five words, 3–48 characters, with no
number, level or version in them: `magazine-photo-opener`, not `recipe-9` or `openers-v2`.

## 3. Commands

Run them from the repository root. They print one line per recipe and edition, then the
findings; they exit 0 when green, 1 on any failure, 2 on a usage error.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `pnpm cookbook new <slug> --chapter <id> [--from <slug>]` | Copies `_template/` (or another recipe) to `cookbook/<slug>/` as a **draft**: a planned slug keeps its Nº, chapter and order from `_roadmap.json`, any other takes the next free Nº and the last place in the chapter; today's dates, and the slug and Nº written into `script.js`. |
| `pnpm cookbook dev <slug> [--lang es] [--port 4400]` | Serves exactly the page the capture runs, and reloads it whenever you save a file of the recipe or the kit. Open it next to your editor. |
| `pnpm cookbook lint [slug…] [--engine local]` | The static checks: `recipe.json`, both write-ups, every composed edition of the pen, assets, credits. Run it until it is silent. With `--engine local`, a draft may pin the next release (see below). |
| `pnpm cookbook capture [slug…]` | Runs the pen in Chrome against the released engine, verifies it (§11) and writes the pages, card, social image, PDF and `capture.json`. With no slug: every recipe whose capture is missing or stale. |
| `pnpm cookbook capture <slug> --check` | Runs and verifies, writes nothing (a regression run). |
| `pnpm cookbook capture <slug> --preview-dir <dir>` | Also writes PNG copies of every page, the card and the social image to `<dir>/<slug>/<lang>/`, with the `capture.json` entry the edition would get. |
| `pnpm cookbook dev <slug> --engine local`, `pnpm cookbook capture <slug> --engine local --preview-dir <dir>` | Run the pen on the workspace engine (`packages/postext/dist` and `packages/postext-pdf/dist`; run `npx tsc` in both first) instead of the released one, to preview a recipe that needs an unreleased feature. The capture only checks: a published recipe is captured from npm once the engine is released. |
| `pnpm cookbook capture [slug…] --sandbox-only` | Writes only each edition's `<slug>.postext` and its `sandbox` entry in `capture.json`, running the pen on the engine version `capture.json` records. Pages, card, social image and PDF are left alone. With no slug: every captured recipe. |
| `pnpm cookbook capture --all --sheet <out.webp>` | A contact sheet of every card and first spread, for the design review. |
| `pnpm cookbook schema` | Regenerates `cookbook/recipe.schema.json` (editors use it through `$schema`). |
| `pnpm cookbook coverage` | Questions, features, warnings and gaps that no recipe covers yet, and the planned recipes that have no folder: the contributors' roadmap. |

The capture skips rewriting images when the layout and the card inputs did not change; pass
`--force` to rewrite them anyway. Drafts show on the site in development
(`next dev`) or with `COOKBOOK_DRAFTS=1`; production lists published recipes only.

**Recipes for the next release.** A recipe that shows a feature not yet on npm is written as a
draft that pins the version the feature will ship in (`engine.postext` newer than
`packages/postext/package.json`, at most the next major). Such a *preview draft* passes
`pnpm cookbook lint <slug> --engine local`, and runs with `dev` and `capture`
`--engine local`; a capture from npm refuses it. It stays on its feature branch: the
repository tests take it only with `COOKBOOK_PREVIEW=1` (`COOKBOOK_PREVIEW=1 pnpm test` in
`apps/web`), and without it they fail its engine pin and ask for its capture, as plain
`pnpm cookbook lint` does. A draft in `develop` pins a released engine and has its capture, so
the recipe lands in a PR after the release: pin nothing new, run
`pnpm cookbook capture <slug>` and publish it.

## 4. `recipe.json`

The TypeScript source of truth is `RecipeMeta` in `apps/web/src/lib/cookbook/types.ts`;
`validate.ts` checks it. The fields you edit most:

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| `number` | Permanent Nº, assigned by `new`. Never change or reuse it. |
| `status` | `draft` → `published` (by the reviewer) → `retired` (needs `replacedBy`). |
| `chapter`, `order` | One chapter; sparse order inside it (10, 20, 30…). |
| `level` | 1 Basic, 2 Intermediate, 3 Advanced (rubric below). The lint warns when the code reads two levels away. |
| `genres` | 1–3 of `novel poetry textbook workbook manual paper report magazine newsletter catalogue photobook ephemera any`. |
| `outputs` | 1–4 of `canvas html pdf bundle live`. `pdf` ⇔ the `pdf` kit block ⇔ an import from `https://esm.sh/postext-pdf` ⇔ `engine.postextPdf`. |
| `features` | `primary`: 1–3 features the recipe **teaches**; `also`: up to 17 others it uses. Ids from `_registry/features.json`. |
| `answers` | Question ids (`Q01`…`Q97`); `answers[0]` is the question the recipe page leads with. |
| `gaps` | Unsupported features the recipe works around (gives the Workaround badge). |
| `gotchas`, `explainsWarnings` | Shared pitfalls and warning kinds shown under Pitfalls. |
| `related` | Up to four hand-picked sibling slugs; the rest are computed. |
| `workarounds` | Engine bugs the recipe routes around: `{ followup?, issue?, package, note }`, revisited when fixed. |
| `engine` | `postext` minimum version (≤ the released one, or the next release in a preview draft, §3); `postextPdf` for PDF recipes; `math`, `worker` flags. |
| `kit` | Blocks to inline: always `core`, `fonts`, `viewer`; plus `pdf`, `images` and `cjk` when used. |
| `sample.locales` | Languages with a `content.<lang>.md`; `[0]` is the fallback edition. |
| `capture` | `hero` (a page, or a spread `[verso, recto]`; page 1 is a recto on its own), `card` (`spread`, `page`, `loupe`, `crop`, `screenshot`), `focus` for loupe and crop, `pages`, `expect`. Page numbers count from 1 at the document's first page, whatever its printed folio (`continuation.pageNumbering.startAt`) or its place in the book (`continuation.pageIndexOffset`). Versos and rectos follow the physical book page (`pageIndexOffset` + index + 1), not the folio: the capture checks that a spread's verso is an even book page. A spread is named in reading order, `[verso, recto]`, also in a book bound on the right, which the light table and the card lay out mirrored (`[3 \| 2]`). |
| `downloads.pdf` | Keep and serve the PDF the pen builds. |
| `credits` | Authors; text, image and font credits (§8). |
| `license` | `{ "code": "MIT", "content": "MIT" \| "CC-BY-4.0" }` for your own code and prose. |
| `created`, `updated` | ISO dates; bump `updated` on any change a reader can see. |

**Level rubric.** *Basic*: Markdown plus at most four design sections in the config (the
`colorPalette` and the empty `header` and `footer` every recipe carries do not count), no
design slots, one resource at most, a single `buildDocument`. *Intermediate*: design slots (openers, running
heads), placed resources, named styles or a PDF export. *Advanced*: any of `buildBundle`,
part palettes, rotated or split tables, a column-and-a-half float channel with side captions,
maths, workers or interactive rebuilds.

## 5. The pen: `script.js`

### 5.1 Principles

1. **The composed file is self-sufficient.** It loads its own fonts from Fontsource (the same
   files the PDF embeds), builds its own viewer and needs nothing else. `index.html`,
   `style.css` and `pen.json` are optional.
2. **Top-level `await` runs the whole pipeline.** The module settling is the completion
   signal the capture waits for. Interactive recipes paint their first state at top level and
   attach listeners afterwards.
3. **One engine entry.** Named imports come from `https://esm.sh/postext` (plus
   `https://esm.sh/postext-pdf` for PDFs), never pinned: the capture pins the released version
   for you. Maths recipes import **every** symbol from `https://esm.sh/postext?bundle`.
4. **Design first.** The file reads top to bottom: design, content, fonts, build. The answer
   sits at the top of CodePen's JS panel; the long sample and the kit are folded away.
5. **Never the default skin** (§7).

### 5.2 Skeleton and markers

Start from `_template/script.js`; it runs as it is. The fixed parts, in order:

```js
// ═══ Postext Cookbook · Nº 009 · Magazine opener on a full-bleed photo ═══════════   line 1
// https://postext.dev/en/cookbook/magazine-photo-opener                              line 2
// Code: MIT · Text: ESO eso2315 (CC BY 4.0) · Photo: ESO/VPHAS+ (CC BY 4.0)           line 3
// Fonts: Newsreader, Archivo, Chivo (SIL OFL 1.1) · Needs postext ≥ 1.4.1            line 4
import { buildDocument, renderPageToCanvas, clearMeasurementCache } from 'https://esm.sh/postext';

const LANG = 'en'; // @lang: the language of the sample document ('en' | 'es')
const RECIPE = 'magazine-photo-opener';

// ─── 1 · Design ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
// #region answer: <what it shows>      exactly one; 10–40 lines; the technique itself
// #endregion
const config = () => ({ /* … */ });     // a factory, never a shared object

// ─── 2 · Content ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
const markdown = /* @content */ '';     // content.<lang>.md, inlined by composition

// ─── 3 · Fonts ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
const FONTS = { Newsreader: ['400', '400i', '700', '700i'], Archivo: ['700'] };

// ─── 4 · Build & show ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
await loadFonts(FONTS, markdown);
const doc = await buildWithFonts(() => buildDocument({ markdown }, config()), markdown);
showPages(doc, { title: t({ en: 'Magazine opener', es: 'Apertura de revista' }) });

// @kit                                  the last line
```

Composition (`apps/web/src/lib/cookbook/compose.ts`) turns this source into the file that
CodePen, Copy, the `.html` download and the capture all run, so they are identical:

| Marker (exact form) | Replaced by |
|---|---|
| `const LANG = 'en'; // @lang` | the edition's language |
| `/* @content */ ''` | a literal of `content.<lang>.md` (falling back to `sample.locales[0]`) |
| `/* @content:<slot> */ ''` | a literal of `content.<slot>.<lang>.md` |
| `// @kit` (last line) | the kit blocks listed in `recipe.json` `kit`, in the order core, fonts, viewer, pdf, images, cjk |
| `// #region <id>: <title>` … `// #endregion` | kept as is; the write-up excerpts regions by id |

The banner's four lines, the section banners `1 · Design`, `2 · Content`, `3 · Fonts`,
`4 · Build & show`, the `RECIPE` constant (equal to the folder name) and the `FONTS` object
are all linted. Regions: exactly one `answer`, at most six others, never nested.

### 5.3 Variants

- **PDF.** Add `"pdf"` to `outputs` and `kit`, `"postextPdf": "1.4.1"` to `engine`, and
  `"downloads": { "pdf": true }` to serve the file:
  ```js
  import { renderToPdf, decompressWoff2 } from 'https://esm.sh/postext-pdf';
  offerPdf(() => renderToPdf(doc, { fontProvider: fontsourceProvider, resourceBytes: imageBytes }),
    `${RECIPE}.pdf`);
  ```
- **Pictures.** Add the `images` kit block and `registerResourceImage` to the import. Put the
  files in `assets/` (see the limits in §5.6), load them before the build and declare bitmaps
  at print size: `await loadImage('lagoon.jpg', asset('lagoon-2400.jpg'))`, then a resource
  `{ id, typeId: 'figure', kind: 'bitmap', createdAt: 0, updatedAt: 0, bitmap: { fileId: 'lagoon.jpg', format: 'jpeg', width: 2400, height: 1520 }, caption, note, altText }`.
  Draw generated SVG with `loadSvg(fileId, markup)`.
- **Books.** `buildWithFonts(() => buildBundle({ chapters: [{ markdown }, { markdown: chapter2 }], config: config(), resources }))`
  with `const chapter2 = /* @content:chapter-2 */ '';` and `content.chapter-2.en.md`.
  `showPages` pairs pages across chapters and `renderToPdf` accepts the array of documents.
- **HTML.** `document.getElementById('pages').innerHTML = renderToHtml(doc, { mode: 'single', background: '#fff', resourceImageUrl: imageUrl })`.
  Always pass a background (HTML pages are transparent) and use `capture.card: "screenshot"`.
- **Maths.** Import everything from `https://esm.sh/postext?bundle`, `await initMathEngine()`
  before the build, set `engine.math: true` and accept the known console noise:
  `"expect": { "console": ["module \"buffer\" not found", "module \"esprima\" not found"] }`.
- **Live controls.** `index.html` holds `<form id="controls">…</form>`; a `render()` rebuilds
  with `config()` and calls `showPages`. Add `"live"` to `outputs`.
- **Workers.** Follow the docs' "Running layout in a Web Worker" and set `engine.worker: true`.

### 5.4 The kit

The kit (`cookbook/_kit/*.js`) is inlined, never copied, so a recipe cannot drift from it.
Its functions are hoisted declarations you can call from anywhere in the script:

| Function | Block | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `mm(n)`, `pt(n)`, `em(n)` | core | Dimensions: `mm(18)` is `{ value: 18, unit: 'mm' }`. |
| `t({ en, es })` | core | The string for the sample's language. |
| `asset(file)` | core | The jsDelivr URL of `cookbook/<slug>/assets/<file>`. |
| `loadFonts(FONTS, text)` | fonts | Loads every face from Fontsource before the first build (latin-ext too when the text needs it). |
| `buildWithFonts(build, text)` | fonts | Runs the build, loads any face the pages use that `FONTS` forgot (with a warning the capture fails on), and rebuilds. |
| `showPages(doc \| docs, { title })` | viewer | The dark desk with facing spreads; sets `data-postext="ready"`. |
| `offerPdf(makePdf, filename)` | pdf | A "Build the PDF" button, then open and download links. |
| `fontsourceProvider` | pdf | The PDF font provider: snaps to shipped weights, falls back from missing italics. |
| `loadImage(id, url)`, `loadSvg(id, svg)` | images | Register pictures for the canvas and keep their bytes. |
| `imageBytes`, `imageUrl` | images | The `resourceBytes` of `renderToPdf` and the `resourceImageUrl` of `renderToHtml`. |
| `loadCjkFonts(faces, text, { vertical })` | cjk | Chinese, Japanese and Korean faces (the other families of `faces` are left to `loadFonts`): one `FontFace` per Fontsource unicode-range file, loading the files `text` touches; fails on a character no file has, or when api.fontsource.org does not answer. One face: `loadCjkFonts(FONTS, markdown)`. Several voices: one call per voice with the text it sets, `loadCjkFonts({ 'LXGW WenKai TC': ['400'] }, quotes)`, so the Kai and Hei faces do not fetch a file for every character of the book (C12 fails a character set from a file that was not loaded). `vertical: true` also loads each family's vertical punctuation for the canvas (import `loadVerticalAlternates`). |
| `cjkPdfProvider` | cjk | The PDF font provider for such faces: the files that hold each face's characters (other families go to `fontsourceProvider`, so list `pdf` too). |
| `showBook(doc \| docs, { title, binding })` | cjk | `showPages` for a book bound on either edge: a right-bound document (`doc.binding`, set by `page.binding: 'right'` or vertical text) lies mirrored, page 1 alone on the left of the spine, then `[3 \| 2]`. |

A change to the kit changes every recipe's source hash, so every capture goes stale and the
whole Cookbook is verified again.

### 5.5 Style and engine traps (linted)

- ES2022, two-space indent, single quotes, semicolons, lines of 100 characters or fewer.
  No classes, no frameworks, no dependencies beyond `postext` and `postext-pdf`.
- Comments in English (the Spanish explanation lives in `es.mdx`). They say *why*, and cite
  shared pitfalls by id: `// gotcha: headings-drop-h1-break`.
- `const config = () => ({ … })`: the engine caches resolved configs by object identity.
- Any `headings` object drops the H1 page break: restate
  `levels: [{ level: 1, breakBefore: { enabled: true, parity } }]`.
- `fontFamily` holds one family, never a CSS stack.
- Ordered lists use `numberFormat: 'arabic'` (`'decimal'` prints "undefined").
- `::resource{id="…"}` takes double quotes; every frontmatter value is quoted.
- Bitmaps declare `width` and `height` in pixels at print size.
- No `Math.random()` (use a seeded Mulberry32 for generated art), no clock, no dates from
  `new Date()`: captures must be deterministic.
- SVG without `<marker>`, filters or masks (they fall back to raster in the PDF); draw
  arrowheads as paths.

### 5.6 Limits and network

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Recipe code (lines outside the content, the kit and `#region art…` artwork) | ≤ 300; aim for ≤ 120 (level 1), ≤ 180 (level 2), ≤ 250 (level 3) |
| Composed script / CodePen prefill | ≤ 60 KB / ≤ 96 KB |
| Each content file | ≤ 2,500 words; Chinese and Japanese characters count 1.7 to the word (about 4,250 characters of Chinese); Korean counts its spaced words, fullwidth Ａ１ counts as A1 |
| Captured pages | 2–12 |
| Each asset / all assets | ≤ 400 KB / ≤ 2 MB; images ≤ 2400 px on the long side, JPEG q80 |
| Captured media per edition (PDF excluded) | warning at 0.9 MB (0.1 MB per published page past 9), failure at 1.4 MB |
| PDF text | inside Fontsource's `latin` range, or set in a CJK face loaded by the `cjk` block (the lint and C25 warn otherwise) |

A pen may fetch from `esm.sh`, `cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@fontsource/*`,
`cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/drnachio/postext@main/cookbook/*`, `api.fontsource.org` and
`postext.dev`. Nothing else, and no hotlinking. Assets are immutable: a changed file gets a
new name (`lagoon-2400-v2.jpg`), because jsDelivr caches `@main` for hours.

## 6. The design bar

A recipe is published only when its pages would pass for a professional publication. Check
every box before you ask for a review. The ranges come from the showcase books.

**Page and geometry**
- [ ] A trim chosen for the genre, never the 17 × 24 cm default: magazine 225 × 297 or
      168 × 237 mm, trade book 155 × 235, catalogue 210 × 270, textbook 210 × 275–280.
- [ ] `mirror: true`; top margin 22–24 mm, bottom 20–24, sides 14–22 (a wide outer margin
      when one column needs a readable measure).
- [ ] The layout the genre needs: `double` with a 6–9 mm gutter for magazines; `oneAndHalf`
      with a 26–30 % side column (`sideColumnRole: 'floats'`, `sideColumnSide: 'outer'`) for
      glosses and textbooks; `single` for catalogues and literary books.
- [ ] A measure of 45–75 characters.

**Typography**
- [ ] Three voices: text, display and label faces, either a superfamily (Source Serif 4 +
      Source Sans 3) or a deliberate contrast (Newsreader + Archivo + Chivo; EB Garamond +
      Bodoni Moda + IBM Plex Sans Condensed; Alegreya + Playfair Display + Alegreya SC).
- [ ] Body 9.3–12.4 pt with leading ×1.33–1.47, never the 8 pt default.
- [ ] One texture: *book* (justified serif, 4–5 mm indent, `indentAfterHeading: false`) or
      *report/magazine* (ragged sans, no indent, `paragraphSpacing: true`), never mixed.
- [ ] Hyphenation on, in the text's language; `optimalLineBreaking`; widows, orphans and
      runts avoided.
- [ ] Display scale: cover titles 46–88 pt, opener titles 23–35 pt (about 2.5–3.5× the body),
      H2 11.5–17 pt, H3 10–15.6 pt.
- [ ] Kickers and labels 7.5–11.7 pt, capitals tracked 0.15–0.25 em, weight 600–700, in the
      accent.
- [ ] Bold, italic and references in ink (or references deliberately styled), never blue.

**Colour**
- [ ] A 5–8 entry palette with semantic ids (`ink`, `band`/`accent`, `tint`, `rule`, `muted`,
      `paper`), and **every** colour in the config linked with `paletteId`.
- [ ] Ink a tinted near-black, one accent (at most a second), pale tints, 0.5 pt
      hairlines in `rule`.
- [ ] Contrast: body and captions ≥ 4.5:1, display ≥ 3:1, small bold kickers ≥ 4.5:1.

**Furniture and rhythm**
- [ ] Running heads: verso folio + book title, recto chapter + folio, 7.5–9.75 pt, anchored
      to the page with explicit mm offsets; `parity` and `pages: 'body'`; a drop folio on
      openers.
- [ ] No running heads on covers and front matter (their heading styles set
      `header`/`footer: { elements: [] }`).
- [ ] Openers are `advancedDesign` with a `minHeight`, fed by heading attributes; a
      `breakBefore.parity` chosen on purpose (`'any'` for short chapters).
- [ ] Each callout uses one device: a 2.5 pt top stripe, a 3 pt side stripe or a tint; never
      the grey default box.
- [ ] Captions in the label face, 7.4–9.75 pt, label bold in the accent, credit notes 6.3–8.2 pt
      muted.
- [ ] Tables with horizontal 0.5 pt rules, header filled in ink or accent with white bold
      labels, 1–1.8 mm padding.
- [ ] Chapters end on a paragraph, not a box; no forced breaks that leave near-empty pages.

**Content**
- [ ] Real, coherent long-form text for a named publication, real numbers in tables and
      panels, captions with credits, a one-line colophon. No lorem ipsum, no repeated
      paragraphs.
- [ ] Spanish conventions in Spanish text: « », a dash (—) for dialogue, a decimal comma.
- [ ] Invented facts read as fiction, never as claims about real organisations.

**The card**
- [ ] The hero page or spread shows what the recipe teaches: a picture or colour field
      over half the card, a title of 45 pt or more with high contrast. Text-only pages are
      grey mush at card size.
- [ ] No 1 px hairline patterns or ruled baseline grids on the hero: they alias into noise.

### Chinese, Japanese and Korean pages

A recipe set in Chinese meets the bar above with these changes, taken from clreq and the
showcase's 红楼梦. The Latin rules on hyphenation, italics and Spanish conventions do not apply
to the Chinese text.

**Page and grid**
- [ ] The type area in characters (`cjk.grid`): a measure of 17–45 characters per line, 25–40
      in a single-column horizontal book, 30–45 in a vertical one, 17–25 in a column of a
      magazine or a newspaper, and a whole number of ems in every case. Lines per page stated,
      not left to the margins.
- [ ] A trim from Chinese practice: 大32开 140 × 203 mm, 32开 130 × 184 mm, 16开 184 × 260 mm;
      天头 (head margin) larger than 地脚 (foot).
- [ ] Vertical text bound on the right (`layout.writingMode: 'vertical-rl'`, which sets
      `page.binding` to `'right'`), shown with `showBook`; its `capture.hero` in reading order.

**Typography**
- [ ] `config.locale` written out: `'zh-Hans'` or `'zh-Hant'` (`'zh-HK'`), never `LANG`.
- [ ] Body 9–12 pt (五号 10.5 pt in books, 小五 9 pt in two columns), leading 1.5–2 × the
      size; a line gap large enough for any ruby or marks the page sets.
- [ ] Three voices from the Chinese styles: 宋/明 (Song or Ming) for the text, 黑 (Hei) for
      heads and labels, 楷 (Kai) for quotations, prefaces and notes. Noto Serif SC/TC,
      Noto Sans SC/TC and LXGW WenKai TC are on Fontsource. Each voice loads with the text
      it sets (`loadCjkFonts`, one call per voice).
- [ ] Justified, with a 2-character first-line indent (`em(2)`) and no space between
      paragraphs; no hyphenation.
- [ ] One punctuation style chosen for the region: Kaiming on the mainland (the `'auto'`
      default for `zh-Hans`), full width and centred in Taiwan and Hong Kong; quotation marks
      “ ” ‘ ’ in horizontal mainland text, 「」『』 in Taiwan and in vertical text.
- [ ] No italics in Chinese: emphasis dots (`*…*` in a document tagged Chinese, or
      `:dots[…]`), bold or the Kai face; italics only for Latin words.
- [ ] No `cjkLooseLine` in the capture (C24): a long Latin word or web address may not leave
      a justified line short.

**Content**
- [ ] The text from a public-domain Chinese source (zh.wikisource, Project Gutenberg), cited
      with its edition; both editions (`en`, `es`) carry the Chinese text, and the chrome,
      captions and any translation change with the edition.
- [ ] The recipe's write-up names the Latin-script recipe it pairs with (the same technique
      in a European book), so a reader can compare.

## 7. Never the default skin

The engine's defaults exist so that a first `buildDocument` shows something. Nobody chose
them for print, and readers copy what a recipe shows. The default look is: a
17 × 24 cm page with unmirrored margins, EB Garamond at 8 pt, Open Sans headings in
`#295AA3` blue, bold and italic in the same blue, a blue running head over a 1 pt blue rule,
a grey `#f4f4f4` box for callouts and a spreadsheet grid for tables.

**Every recipe sets at least:** page geometry (mirrored for books); body family, size and
leading; bold, italic and reference colours back to ink; `headings`, with the H1
`breakBefore` restated; `header` and `footer`; a semantic `colorPalette` with every colour
linked; and callout, table and caption styles whenever the recipe uses them.

The lint fails a config without `bodyText`, `header`, `footer` or `colorPalette`. The capture
(check C17) fails on any of: body text in EB Garamond 8 pt; a heading level in Open Sans
`#295AA3`; no `header` or `footer` in the config; bold, italic or reference colour left at
`#295AA3`; a callout in the grey default box without a stripe; a table with the default grid
and grey header; no palette.

Two habits make it hard to get wrong:
- **Point `main-color` at your accent.** The engine's defaults are linked to the palette id
  `main-color`; adding `{ id: 'main-color', value: <your accent> }` to the palette recolours
  every default you forgot to restate (the template does this).
- **Style what you use as soon as you use it.** The template already carries a callout, a
  table and a caption style in the house palette.

The only exception is a recipe *about* the defaults, which declares what it shows on
purpose in `capture.expect.defaultSkin`.

## 8. Content and licences

**Code** is MIT. **Your own prose** is MIT or CC BY 4.0 (`license.content`).

**Sample text**, in order of preference:
1. Original prose written for the recipe, bilingual, in a realistic genre voice.
2. Public-domain literature (Project Gutenberg: credit the eBook number, strip the licence
   boilerplate).
3. CC BY texts (ESO and NOIRLab releases, OpenStax Spanish and first editions) with an
   in-page source line.
4. CC BY-SA text (Wikipedia) only with the article and revision cited; `CC-BY-SA-4.0` is
   allowed in `credits.text` only.

**Never NC or ND** licences, and never "all rights reserved" sources. The validator rejects
them. OpenStax English 2e books are NC-SA: unusable.

**Pictures**, in order of preference: SVG generated in code (seeded, paths instead of
markers, the page's palette), then CC0 or public-domain images (Met, NGA and Cleveland open
access; Wikimedia Commons with a checked licence), then CC BY with the full credit line.
**The hero page and the card use only original, CC0 or public-domain imagery**, because
thumbnails travel without their credits. Host downscaled copies in `assets/`; never hotlink.

**Fonts**: SIL OFL or Apache families served by Fontsource. Every family in `FONTS` needs a
`credits.fonts` entry, and families differ in what they ship (Outfit and Bricolage Grotesque
have no italic; Alegreya SC has no 600). Chinese, Japanese and Korean families are the
exception to "the same static files": Fontsource splits each weight into about a hundred
unicode-range files, so a recipe lists them in `FONTS` as usual and loads them through the
`cjk` kit block (`loadCjkFonts`, `cjkPdfProvider`), which fetch only the files the text
needs. They ship no italic.

**Credits in three places:** `recipe.json` `credits` (text, every asset with its `file`,
fonts), a `note` under every picture whose licence requires attribution, and a one-line
colophon inside the sample document:
*"Set in Newsreader, Archivo and Chivo (SIL OFL) · Text: ESO, CC BY 4.0"*.

## 9. The write-up

`en.mdx` and `es.mdx` hold YAML frontmatter and only the **authored** sections. The page
generates the others and interleaves them, so the order cannot drift:

| # | English | Spanish | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What you'll build | Lo que vas a componer | MDX, required: 60–150 words in an art director's voice. The page appends "This recipe answers". |
| 2 | The short answer | La respuesta corta | Generated from `#region answer`. |
| 3 | Ingredients | Ingredientes | Generated: features, fonts, assets, APIs, with docs links. |
| 4 | Method | Elaboración | MDX, required: 3–7 steps (`### 1 · Title`), each opening with `<Excerpt region="…" />`, then 1–3 sentences on *why*. |
| 5 | The whole recipe | La receta completa | Generated: the composed files, content and kit folded. |
| 6 | Variations | Variantes | MDX, optional: 1–4 × (`### Imperative title`, one sentence, a `diff` or a sibling link). |
| 7 | Pitfalls | Errores frecuentes | Generated from `gotchas` and `explainsWarnings`, plus optional MDX lines. |
| 8 | Credits | Créditos | Generated from `credits`. |

Write the H2 headings exactly as in the table; nothing may come before the first H2.

**Frontmatter** (every value quoted):

```yaml
---
title: "Magazine opener on a full-bleed photo"      # ≤ 60 characters: an outcome
summary: "A heading style whose opener is a bleed photo plus kicker, headline and standfirst taken from heading attributes."   # 60–160
description: "…"          # optional, 120–160: the meta description
question: "…"             # optional, ≤ 110: rephrases answers[0] for this page
aliases: ["article opener", "hero image", "entradilla"]   # search-only synonyms
pageNotes: { "3": "…" }   # optional extra alt text per captured page
---
```

**Components.** `<Excerpt region="photo" highlight="4-7" />` (a region of the composed script
with its real line numbers; `highlight` counts from the excerpt's first line), `<PageRef page={3}>…</PageRef>`, `<PageShot page={3} crop="0,.55,1,.45" caption="…" />`,
`<Gotcha id="…" />`, `<Feature id="…">…</Feature>`, `<RecipeLink slug="…">…</RecipeLink>`
and `<Note>…</Note>`. Every id and slug must exist, and `en.mdx` and `es.mdx` show the same
excerpt regions in the same order. Links into the docs use the page's own locale prefix
(`/es/docs/…` in `es.mdx`) and must resolve.

**Voice.** Short sentences, present tense, the reader as "you" (*tú* in Spanish). Explain
decisions, not syntax: the code is right there. No marketing adjectives.

**Write like an editor, not like a chatbot.** The owner does not want the stock phrases and
turns that make text read as machine-written, in the write-ups *or* in the sample documents
you write yourself (quoted public-domain text stays as its author wrote it). `pnpm cookbook
lint` enforces the clearest ones (lint FAIL) and flags the rest (WARN; fix them unless the
word is literal, like a real journey on foot). The rules live in
`apps/web/src/lib/cookbook/style.ts`. Chinese prose you write yourself (a caption, a
colophon, an editorial note) is checked against a Chinese list in either write-up: 值得一提的是,
众所周知, 不言而喻, 总而言之, 综上所述, 在当今…时代 and 赋能 fail; 至关重要, 随着…的发展 and 打造
warn. Quoted classical text is left as its author wrote it.

- **Stock phrases (FAIL).** English: *delve, tapestry, a testament to, in today's … world,
  ever-evolving, seamless(ly), unlock/unleash the power/potential, game-changer, look no
  further, let's dive in, dive into, embark on, harness the power, a symphony/treasure
  trove/masterclass of, whether you're a … or …, picture this, in the realm of, navigate the
  complexities, plays a crucial role, in conclusion, empower, effortless(ly), nestled, stands
  as a, it's not just…, elevate your*. Spanish: *sumérgete, adentrémonos, en el vertiginoso
  mundo, en el mundo actual, en constante evolución, sin fisuras, desbloquear, un testimonio
  de, juega un papel crucial/clave, cabe destacar, es importante señalar, en resumen, en
  conclusión, en definitiva, toda una experiencia, una auténtica joya, llevar al siguiente
  nivel, un sinfín de, un abanico de posibilidades, aprovechar al máximo*.
- **Tells (WARN).** English: *vibrant, bustling, breathtaking, stunning, captivating,
  intricate, meticulous, crucial, pivotal, robust, leverage, cutting-edge, Moreover,
  Furthermore, Additionally, Notably, in essence, at its core, it's worth noting, not only …
  but also, the art/magic of, journey, realm*, and the reveal *"The result? …"*, *"Here's
  why"*. Spanish: *fascinante, apasionante, vibrante, impresionante, cautivador, meticuloso,
  crucial, robusto, de vanguardia, sin lugar a dudas, no solo … sino también, en el corazón
  de, el arte de, la magia de, un viaje, de manera fluida, descubre cómo, no es solo un,
  potenciar tu*, the reveal *"¿El resultado? …"* and a sentence opening with *Imagina…*.
- **Shapes to avoid** even without those words: three adjectives or three clauses in a row
  for rhythm (*clear, elegant and precise*); "it's not X, it's Y" and "X isn't just Y";
  rhetorical questions answered in the next sentence; a colon or dash before a punchline;
  sentences that restate the heading; a closing line that sums up what was just said;
  em dashes where a comma, colon, parenthesis or full stop would do (the lint warns above one
  per 90 words in write-ups; Spanish uses the *raya* for dialogue and incisos, sparingly);
  hedges and filler (*simply, just, basically, actually, really, very*); anthropomorphised
  engines (*Postext knows…, understands…, cares about…*).
- **Say the concrete thing.** Name the setting, the number and the consequence: *"The band is
  100 mm deep, so the text starts on the same grid line in every chapter"*, not *"a striking
  band that seamlessly anchors the page"*. In Spanish: *"La banda mide 100 mm, así que el
  texto empieza en la misma línea en todos los capítulos"*, no *"una banda impactante que
  aporta un toque de elegancia"*.
- **Sample prose** is a real publication's voice: a magazine writer, a textbook author, a
  catalogue editor. It must read as if a person with that job wrote it, with facts, names,
  places and numbers, and without the phrases above.

**Write the Spanish natively.** Do not translate the English sentence by sentence, and use
the site's vocabulary so search and the docs agree:

| English | Spanish | English | Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|
| callout, box | recuadro | running head | cabecera |
| opener | apertura | folio | folio |
| heading | título | kicker | antetítulo |
| standfirst, lead | entradilla | drop cap | capitular |
| baseline grid | rejilla base | gutter | medianil |

| caption | pie | bleed | sangre, sangrado |
| crop marks | marcas de corte | front matter | preliminares |
| part divider page | portadilla de parte | table of contents | índice |
| hyphenation | separación silábica | widow, orphan | viuda, huérfana |
| chip | chip | swatch | muestra de color |
| spread | pliego, doble página | recto, verso | página impar, par |

The docs name their sections with *encabezado* ("Estilos de encabezado"); the Cookbook says
*título*, which never clashes with *cabecera*, the running head. The `heading-*` features list
the docs' wording among their Spanish aliases, so both find them.

Spanish typography applies: `¿…?`, `¡…!`, « » quotation marks, the em dash (—) for
dialogue, the decimal comma.

## 10. Registries

`cookbook/_registry/*.json` hold the bilingual vocabularies the site, the CLI and the tests
share. Each file starts with a `"$comment"` that explains it; the loader ignores `$` keys.

| File | Holds | Keyed by |
|---|---|---|
| `taxonomy.json` | parts, chapters, genres, outputs, levels, the write-up headings | fixed ids |
| `features.json` | ~100 user-facing features: label, definition, search aliases, group, docs anchor, research ids, optional detect rules | kebab-case id |
| `apis.json` | exported engine symbols → docs section | symbol name |
| `config.json` | top-level config keys → docs section | key |
| `questions.json` | the reader questions Q01–Q80, how/why, index form, theme, gap | `Qnn` |
| `gaps.json` | what Postext does not do, with aliases and the workaround | kebab-case id |
| `warnings.json` | every engine, parse and Sandbox warning: label, cause, fix | warning kind |
| `gotchas.json` | shared pitfalls, tied to a feature and to the engine follow-up that would retire them | kebab-case id |
| `collections.json` | featured recipes and curated reading paths | kebab-case id |

**Rules.** Ids are permanent: never rename one a recipe uses. Every text field has non-empty
`en` and `es`. A `docs` anchor names a doc slug and the exact text of one of its h1–h3
headings in each language; the registry test resolves it with the docs' own slugger and
fails when a heading is renamed. A new Sandbox warning kind fails the test until
`warnings.json` explains it. Use the ids that exist before adding one; when a recipe
needs a new feature or pitfall, add it in the same pull request, in both languages.

Run `pnpm exec vitest run src/lib/cookbook` from `apps/web` after any registry change.

## 11. Capture and checks

`pnpm cookbook capture` composes each edition, serves it with an import map that pins the
released engine, opens it in Chrome (`COOKBOOK_CHROME`, else the Puppeteer cache), waits for
the module to settle and the fonts to load, verifies, then paints every page with the
engine's own renderer and writes the files atomically. A failure writes nothing to the site
(`--preview-dir` still gets its PNGs, so you can see what failed).

The capture also writes the document it pictured as a `.postext` bundle, `<slug>.postext`
in each edition's folder, which the recipe page's Open in Sandbox button loads. The pen's
own engine writes it with `createBundle`: the markdown (or the chapters of a `buildBundle`
book), the configuration, the resources and the files they name, and the card as its
thumbnail. Drawings go in as SVG; photos keep the bytes they were decoded from; font files
go in only when the pen built its faces from bytes (Fontsource families travel by name).
Some things cannot travel: a `continuation` keeps its page numbering (moved into
`page.pageNumbering`) but loses its page offset and heading counters, a print master
handed only to `renderToPdf` stays behind, and an interactive recipe gives the Sandbox its
document without its controls. The run lists each of these as a `sandbox` note. Files in the
bundle carry a fixed date, so the same input always gives the same bytes. A bundle that
cannot be written (a resource whose file the pen never registered) fails the edition.

After a tooling change, `--sandbox-only` rewrites the bundles without recapturing the pages:
it runs each pen on the engine its `capture.json` records and warns when the layout no
longer matches the published pages.

**Failures** block the write: script errors or timeouts (C1), console errors not listed in
`expect.console` (C2), failed or disallowed network requests (C3), nothing built (C4), layout
warnings not in `expect.warnings` (C5), a layout that did not converge (C6), parse issues
(C7), unknown directives (C8), unknown style ids (C9), unknown references that print "?"
(C10), unregistered images (C11), faces used but not loaded, or a CJK face setting characters
whose files were not loaded when the layout ran (C12), `FONTS` incomplete (C13),
PDF errors (C14), a tainted canvas (C15), "undefined" or "NaN" printed (C16), the default
skin (C17), empty pages (C18), missing credits (C19), over budget (C20), a warm build over
4 s (C21).

**Warnings** go into `capture.json` for the reviewer: blank-page cascades (C22), near-empty
pages not listed in `expect.nearEmptyPages` (C23), loose lines over 2 % (C24; a Chinese,
Japanese or Korean line is loose when it needed more than half an em between its characters
and ends short, `cjkLoose`, and one is enough), characters a PDF recipe's faces cannot set
(C25: outside Fontsource latin and not covered by a CJK face the `cjk` block loaded, or
reported missing by postext-pdf), a hero with too little picture or display type (C26), a
primary feature whose detect rule did not fire (C27), a level two steps from the suggested
one (C28), a page count outside `expect.pages` (C29).

A right-bound book (`doc.binding: 'right'`) records `binding: "right"` in `capture.json`:
the card, the light table, its lightbox and the contact sheet lay each spread out mirrored,
and ← turns to the next spread.

Treat warnings as questions the reviewer will ask. Fix them, or explain in the pull request
why they are right.

## 12. Review

1. **Start from a request or the roadmap.** Open a *Recipe request* issue, or pick an
   uncovered question or planned recipe from `pnpm cookbook coverage`.
2. **Scaffold and iterate.** `pnpm cookbook new <slug> --chapter <id>`, then
   `pnpm cookbook dev <slug>` beside your editor.
3. **Get to green.** `pnpm cookbook lint <slug>` silent; `pnpm cookbook capture <slug>`
   without failures; `pnpm exec vitest run src/lib/cookbook` (from `apps/web`) passing.
4. **Look.** Open the captured pages at full size and the card at card size
   (`--preview-dir`), in both editions. Read both write-ups aloud once.
5. **Open a pull request** with the recipe folder and its generated
   `apps/web/public/cookbook/<slug>/`, the card and one spread as images, the questions it
   answers, and any capture warning you kept, with the reason.
6. **Reviewers check four things:** the pages against the design bar (§6); the code (the
   answer region is the technique, comments say why, own lines within limits); both
   write-ups (native language, the template, *why* over *what*); licences and credits.
7. **Publication.** The reviewer sets `"status": "published"`; the curator adds the recipe
   to collections (`featured`, reading paths). A published recipe must be captured against
   the npm release (`capture.engine.source` is `npm`).

When the engine changes behaviour, recipes are recaptured with `--check` first; a changed
page is either a regression to report or a recipe to update (`updated` bumped).

## 13. For AI agents

Follow this procedure exactly; do not skip steps because the output "looks right".

1. **Read** this guide, `cookbook/_template/` and two published recipes of the same chapter.
   Read the docs sections your features link to (`features.json` → `docs`).
2. **Choose ids, never invent them.** Pick `chapter`, `genres`, `outputs`, `features`,
   `answers`, `gotchas` and `explainsWarnings` from the registries. If something is truly
   missing, add a registry entry (both languages, a resolving docs anchor) and say so in the
   pull request.
3. **Plan the pages before the code.** Write down each physical page (page 1 is a recto
   unless `continuation.pageIndexOffset` makes it a verso; a spread is `[verso, verso + 1]`
   with the verso on an even book page), what sits on it, and which page is the hero.
4. **Write the sample** as a real publication, within 2,500 words per file, in every sample
   language. Quote the frontmatter. Credit anything you did not write.
5. **Write the pen** from the template: keep the banner, the section order and the markers;
   put the technique in `#region answer` (10–40 lines); keep own code within the level's budget (120 / 180 / 250 lines; generated artwork goes in a `#region art` and does not count);
   cite gotchas in comments; list every face in `FONTS`.
6. **Run** `pnpm cookbook lint <slug>` and `pnpm cookbook capture <slug> --preview-dir <dir>`
   and **look at every page image**. Iterate until there are no failures and every warning
   is fixed or justified.
7. **Write `en.mdx` and `es.mdx`** last, from what the pages show; same excerpt
   regions in the same order; native Spanish with the vocabulary in §9.
8. **Self-review** against §6 box by box, and against §7.
9. **A Chinese, Japanese or Korean recipe** also lists the `cjk` kit block, loads its faces
   with `loadCjkFonts` after `loadFonts` (the text face with the sample, each other voice
   with the text it sets: headings, quotations), gives `renderToPdf`
   `cjkPdfProvider`, writes `config.locale` out, shows a right-bound book with `showBook`,
   and meets §6's CJK variant. When it needs an unreleased feature it is a preview draft
   (§3): `pnpm cookbook lint <slug> --engine local`, `pnpm cookbook dev <slug> --engine local`.

**Never:** leave the default skin; pin engine versions; fetch from hosts outside the
allowlist; use NC/ND content or unlicensed pictures; use `Math.random()` or the clock; put
a text-only page on the card; claim a feature works when the recipe shows a workaround;
change another recipe's `number` or slug; edit generated files by hand.
