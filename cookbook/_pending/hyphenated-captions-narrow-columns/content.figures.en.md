curlew
Eurasian curlew, *Numenius arquata*, on the mud of the lower bay at low water. An adult female’s bill reaches 150 millimetres, long enough to draw lugworms from their burrows; males and juveniles, with shorter bills, feed higher on the shore, where the prey lies nearer the surface.
Colour rings, left leg over right: orange over white. Plate made with diffusion models for the observatory’s identification sheets.
A curlew with a long down-curved bill walking on wet mud at low tide, hills and a village beyond.

redshank
Common redshank, *Tringa totanus*, on the bank of a saltmarsh creek among glasswort. Icelandic birds, the majority in winter, have longer wings and darker, browner upperparts than the redshank that breed on the coastal meadows inland.
Wing lengths of the 34 birds caught this winter: 152 to 171 millimetres. Plate made with diffusion models.
A redshank with bright orange-red legs standing among glasswort at the edge of a creek.

reedling
Male bearded reedling, *Panurus biarmicus*, gripping a reed stem below a seed head. In winter the species changes its diet from insects to reed seed, and its gizzard grows heavier to grind it, helped by grit the birds pick from the mud.
Caught on 14 February in the north net ride, one of 58 reedlings ringed this winter. Plate made with diffusion models.
A bearded reedling, orange-brown with a grey head and a black moustache, holding a reed stem.

warbler
Eurasian reed warbler, *Acrocephalus scirpaceus*, singing from two reed stems. The species winters south of the Sahara; the bird caught on 11 October is the latest in the observatory’s records by nine days.
Fat score 4 on the six-point scale, body mass 14.8 grams. Plate made with diffusion models.
A plain brown reed warbler singing with its bill open, one foot on each of two reed stems.
