:::paragraphs{style="cover-quote"}
Our lives are rivers, gliding free\
To that unfathomed, boundless sea,\
The silent grave!
:::

:::paragraphs{style="cover-blurb"}
In November 1476 Rodrigo Manrique, Master of the Order of Santiago, died in his house at Ocaña after forty years of war on the frontier of Granada and in the civil wars of Castile. His son Jorge, a soldier and a poet, wrote forty stanzas on his death. They begin with the shortness of life, go through the kings and favourites of his own century, and end at the old man’s bedside.
:::

:::paragraphs{style="cover-blurb"}
Longfellow translated the poem at twenty-six, after his first journey through Spain, and published it in Boston in 1833.
:::

:::paragraphs{style="cover-note"}
Translated by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow
