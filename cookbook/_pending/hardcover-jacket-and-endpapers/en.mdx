## What you'll build

The two Bécquer legends of the paperback with flaps, bound a second time as a 120 × 190 mm hardcover. The 37 pages stay as they are. The book now has two versions, each with its own binding and ISBN, and the hardcover has four sheets for the binder. The case is covered in dark green cloth, with the title and author stamped in gold on the front board and the spine. The dust jacket carries the painting, the blurb, the barcode and the two flaps. Two painted endpapers sit inside the boards: Soria above the Duero at the front, the spring of the second legend at the back.

## Method

### 1 · One book block, two versions

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. Both versions read the same `folio.paper` and the same laid-out pages: 37 pages, rounded up to three signatures of 16, 24 leaves of 240 µm each. The paperback adds the glue allowance and gets a 6.3 mm spine. The hardcover adds both 2 mm boards as well, so its spine is 10.3 mm. Each version has its own ISBN, both sample numbers from a publisher block assigned to no book. The case has a flat spine because a block under 6 mm thick is too thin to round; `case-round` would add 3 mm and round it. The case is `cloth` because the jacket already carries the picture, the texts and the barcode. Under it, a stamped title is enough.

### 2 · The case: turn-in, joints and squares

<Excerpt region="sheets" />

A paperback cover is cut to the trim and its flaps fold in at the fore-edges. A case is a covering wrapped round two boards. Each board is cut 7 mm narrower than the page at the spine and stands 3 mm proud of the pages at head, foot and fore-edge (the squares), so it measures 116 × 196 mm. Between each board and the spine runs a 7 mm joint, where the cover bends. Round the whole piece a 15 mm turn-in folds over the board edges, which is why the cloth ground runs out to a 286.3 × 226 mm sheet.

Each sheet is built from the same `config` and the same cover text. The case takes its design from `cover.sheets.case`, a bare cloth ground, and its lines from the frames marked `sheet="case"`: title, subtitle and author on the front board and the spine, in `case-` styles that are the jacket's in gold, and an empty frame on the back board, which keeps the blurb off the cloth. The barcode prints on the jacket only, the sheet a till scans.

### 3 · The jacket reuses the cover's design and text

<Excerpt region="cover-type" />

The jacket has no entry in `cover.sheets`, so it takes the book's `cover` and the frames with no `sheet`, with the paragraph styles and cover Markdown the paperback uses; its front and spine are filled from the metadata, since the case's frames are left out there. Its panels are wider: each covers a board, the joint and 3 mm more to fold round the board's edge, 126 mm in all. Its flaps grow to 80 mm, two thirds of the page width, which is the default for a jacket. The painting fills the front panel up to its fold, and the barcode sits on the back panel, where a bookseller scans it.

### 4 · Two endpapers in one document

<Excerpt region="endpapers" />

An endpaper is two pages wide: the pastedown is glued to the board and the flyleaf turns like a page. At the front the pastedown is on the left, and at the back it is on the right. Each sheet is 246 × 196 mm, with 3 mm of bleed at head, foot and the outer edges and none at the fold, where a 5 mm glue strip holds it to the block. Both paintings keep their middles quiet, since the fold runs through them. The two sheets have one size, so one document holds them, a page each. Each painting is its endpaper's design in `cover.sheets`, laid across both leaves.

## Variations

### Print the case instead of covering it in cloth

Drop `case` from `cover.sheets` and the case takes the book's design: the night ground and the painting on the front board. Its own frames still set the title in gold.

```diff
   sheets: {
-    case: { background: col('cloth') },
     endpaperFront: { image: { resourceId: 'soria' } },
```

### Round the spine

On a thicker book, `case-round` adds 3 mm to the case spine and the jacket's spine grows with it.

```diff
-    { id: 'hardcover', name: t({ en: 'Hardcover', es: 'Cartoné' }), binding: 'case-flat',
+    { id: 'hardcover', name: t({ en: 'Hardcover', es: 'Cartoné' }), binding: 'case-round',
```

## Pitfalls

- The sheets come after the book's pages in the captured list: the case at 38, the jacket at 39, the endpapers at 40 and 41. Add a page to the book and every number moves.
- On a hardcover, `sheet="cover"` names the case as well as a paperback's cover; `sheet="case"` names the case alone. A frame of a sheet's own replaces the shared frame of that zone on that sheet only, so an empty one leaves the zone bare there.
