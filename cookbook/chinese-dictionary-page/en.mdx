## What you'll build

Four pages from the middle of a reader’s edition of the Kangxi Dictionary (康熙字典, 1716), rearranged in pinyin order from 天 tiān to 庭 tíng. Each headword stands in red Song Black with its pinyin over it; the rhyme-book readings (反切) and the senses follow in one paragraph, the senses numbered ① ② in the same red. The page is 大32开, 140 × 203 mm, two columns of 17 characters of 小五 (9 pt) on a grid of 31 lines, each column full to its last line. At the outer corner of every page the running head names the first and the last headword on it, so a reader looking for 蜩 turns to the page headed 腆—髫. <RecipeLink slug="dictionary-thumb-index">The Sailor’s Word-Book</RecipeLink> is the Latin-script dictionary to compare it with.

## Method

### 1 · Guide words from the headwords

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. Every headword is a level-2 heading, and `{firstMark.h2}` and `{lastMark.h2}` print the text of the first and the last level-2 heading that starts on the page ([Text elements](/en/docs/configuration#text-elements)). The mark is the heading’s characters: `## {天|tiān}` marks 天, and the reading stays over the headword. An entry that runs on from one page to the next counts only where its headword stands: <PageRef page={2}>page 635</PageRef> opens with the end of 殄 and is headed 腆—髫. Level 1 is kept for the letters: none opens in these four pages, but a letter head would not count as a guide word.

### 2 · Pinyin over the headwords

<Excerpt region="headwords" />

`{天|tiān}` is the compact form of `:ruby[天]{rt="tiān"}`, one reading over one character ([Chinese marks, ruby and warichu](/en/docs/document-format#chinese-marks-ruby-and-warichu)). A heading with no design of its own sets its reading; a heading design would print 天 alone. The reading takes the heading’s own face, Black, unless `cjk.ruby.fontFamily` names one, so the regular Song is named. Half of 15 pt is 7.5 pt, and the heading’s 30 pt line leaves room for it over the headword, so the heading is two lines of the grid and the text under it stays on the 15 pt lines of both columns. A pinyin reading is raised by the depth of its face’s descenders and 0.04 em more, so the g of tīng ends 0.6 pt above the em box of 汀.

### 3 · One paragraph to an entry

<Excerpt region="senses" />

The Kangxi Dictionary runs an entry together: the readings of the rhyme books, then each sense introduced by 又. Here the 又 became circled numbers, typed as `**②**`, and `boldColor` prints them in the cinnabar of the headwords; there is no other bold in the text. A sense that the Kangxi reads another way keeps its own reading, as 跳 ② does with 徒刀切. The composer sets ① to ⑳ as Latin letters, so a line may end on a number whose sense opens the next line: a word joiner (U+2060) typed after each number stops that, and keeps the Han–Latin space out too. Column balancing is off (`headings.balancing.enabled: false`), so no column opens space above its headwords and every entry follows the one before it by the same distance.

### 4 · The tab of the letter T

<Excerpt region="tab" />

Pinyin syllables start with 23 of the 26 letters (none with I, U or V), so the type area’s height is cut into 23 steps of 7.13 mm, and T takes the 19th. The tab is 9 mm wide and starts 3 mm past the trim, so the page cuts it square at the fore-edge and only its inner corners show their rounding; a print file with `page.cutLines` would carry those 3 mm into its bleed. The letter is centred on the 6 mm inside the page. All four pages are in T, so the tab stays at the same height; <RecipeLink slug="dictionary-thumb-index">the Sailor’s Word-Book</RecipeLink> moves it down the edge from one letter to the next.

### 5 · Each face loads its own characters

<Excerpt region="voices" />

Fontsource cuts each Chinese face into about a hundred files by character range, and `loadCjkFonts` loads the files that hold the text it is given. The regular Song gets the whole sample, the colophon’s English included. The Black gets the thirty headwords, the bold weight the sense numbers, the Hei the headwords and the digits of the running heads, and the Kai the colophon’s line of Chinese. `cjkPdfProvider` embeds the same files in the PDF, each cut down to the characters its pages use.

## Variations

### First headword on the verso, last on the recto

Some dictionaries print one guide word to a page, the first at the top of the verso and the last at the top of the recto, so the spread reads 天 … 髫.

```diff
-  head('verso-guide', '{firstMark.h2}—{lastMark.h2}', 'even', SIDE + 8, GUIDE),
-  head('recto-guide', '{firstMark.h2}—{lastMark.h2}', 'odd', SIDE + 8, GUIDE),
+  head('verso-guide', '{firstMark.h2}', 'even', SIDE + 8, GUIDE),
+  head('recto-guide', '{lastMark.h2}', 'odd', SIDE + 8, GUIDE),
```

### Pinyin with ɑ and ɡ

Chinese dictionaries set their pinyin in a face with a one-storey a and g. Name Andika for the readings and add it to `FONTS` (`'Andika': ['400']`, with its OFL credit).

```diff
-  ruby: { fontFamily: SONG, fontSize: em(0.5), color: col('cinnabar') },
+  ruby: { fontFamily: 'Andika', fontSize: em(0.5), color: col('cinnabar') },
```

### Headwords at text size

A compact dictionary sets the headword in bold at the start of its paragraph. Put the entries in `:::paragraphs{style="entry"}`, open each with `**{天|tiān}**` and print `{firstMark.entry}`: a paragraph that opens with a bold run marks the page with it.

## Pitfalls

- The composer does not keep a paragraph from ending on a line of one character (孤字, as 添。 alone under the rest of its entry), and it may break a book title after its first character (《說|文》). The thirty entries are cut from much longer Kangxi articles, and the cuts were chosen so that no entry ends that way and no title breaks there. A word joiner inside a title (`《說⁠文》`) keeps it whole, at the cost of a line one character shorter.
- With column balancing on, a column that cannot take the next headword with two lines of its text under it (`bodyText.widowMinLines`) ends early, and the lines left over go above the headwords, one line each at most: one entry stands a line clear of the one before, the next does not. Off, such a column ends short; the cuts above also fill every column but the last.
