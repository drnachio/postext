## What you'll build

An offprint from a law journal's case-comment series: four pages on *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police*, the 2018 Supreme Court decision on when the police owe a duty of care. The page is 162 × 240 mm, mirrored, in Libre Baskerville at 9.4/13.6 pt, and the first page opens on a claret band with the case name set in 60 pt italics. The author writes each authority as a key with its pinpoint, `[@caparo1990, 617–18]`, and Postext sets 23 footnotes in OSCOLA, the citation standard of British law schools: case names in italics, statutes in roman, no full stops after AC, HL or edn, *ibid* for the authority of the note before and *Robinson (n 1) [55]* after that. The authorities are real; the author and the journal are not.

## Method

### 1 · An OSCOLA note for every authority

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. OSCOLA is a note style, so `[@robinson2018, para 21]` leaves a superscript number after the full stop and its words go to the foot of the page. Note 1 gives the case in full, *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police [2018] UKSC 4, [2018] AC 736*; note 2, which cites it again at once, reads *ibid [21]*; note 8, after other cases have come between, reads *Robinson (n 1) [26–27]*. A law-review article cites only in its notes, so `bibliography.auto: false` keeps the list of works off the last page.

### 2 · A case is data, written as the reports print it

A case is a CSL item of `type: legal_case`: its name in `title`, the court in `authority`, the report series in `container-title` and the first page in `page`. OSCOLA puts the year of a report in square brackets when the year identifies the volume, as with the Appeal Cases, so the recipe writes that year in `volume`, with the volume number after it: `volume: "[1990] 2"` gives *[1990] 2 AC 605 (HL)*. A case decided after 2001 also has a neutral citation: `authority: UKSC`, `number: "4"` and `issued: 2018` give *[2018] UKSC 4*, followed by the report. A statute is `type: legislation` with the year in its title and no `issued`, so the year is not printed twice, and `[@hra1998, § 6]` cites a section as *s 6*.

### 3 · Two patches to the bundled style

<Excerpt region="oscola" />

The bundled `oscola.csl` brackets a paragraph pinpoint only in a case's first note. Later notes printed *para 55* and *ibid 27*, and a second note to the same Act lost its *s*. The patch replaces one macro and one line of the style's XML before the build and passes the result as a custom style, so the text and the references do not change.

### 4 · The case name on the band

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The band runs 124 mm down from the trim. The case name, the parties and the citation are heading attributes, so another comment in the series changes only its Markdown. *Robinson* is set in Libre Baskerville Italic at 60 pt because OSCOLA italicises case names, and the citation under it stays roman, as it does in the notes.

## Variations

### Print the style as it ships

To compare with the unpatched output, use the bundled id. Paragraph pinpoints in later notes then read *para 55*.

```diff
-  style: 'custom', customStyle: OSCOLA, // the bundled 'oscola', patched above
+  style: 'oscola',
```

### Collect the notes at the end

Some journals print their notes after the text. The citations keep their full and short forms and their numbers.

```diff
-  placement: 'column', numbering: 'chapter',
+  placement: 'chapterEnd', numbering: 'chapter',
```

## Pitfalls

- A report whose volume is a plain number, `volume: "2"`, prints the year in round brackets, *(1990) 2 AC*, which OSCOLA keeps for series numbered across years such as the Commonwealth Law Reports (note 7). Write the bracketed year into `volume` for the Appeal Cases and the Weekly Law Reports.
- `s` before a locator means a page (German *Seite*), not a section: write `§ 6`. A subsection such as `6(1)` is not read as a locator; name it in the sentence and cite the section.
- OSCOLA has no bibliography in an article, and the style has no separate tables of cases and legislation. A thesis that needs those tables gets one sorted list from `:::bibliography`, with secondary sources first and then cases and statutes mixed.
