## What you'll build

A booklet of four A5 pages on three astrolabes in the Metropolitan Museum of Art: one made in Yemen in 1291, one in Iran in 1654 and one in Europe in 1462. The publisher prints it in English, Spanish and Arabic, and the three editions are one design. The cover photograph, the brass headings, the box with its stripe, the figures with the text beside them and the dark table header come from a single set of settings. The Arabic edition changes what belongs to its script: Amiri and IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Source Serif and Source Sans, a larger size, and labels without capitals or tracking. It reads from the right and binds on the right without one coordinate of its own.

## Method

### 1 · General settings and three small overrides

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `config` holds the general settings; `mergeConfigLayers` lays an edition's override over them field by field. English and Spanish have no override: their `locale` picks the hyphenation patterns and the words *Figure* and *Table*, *Figura* and *Tabla*. The Arabic override names only fields: `bodyText.fontFamily` changes the face and keeps the colour, the alignment and the hyphenation setting, and the cover's `kicker` element changes its face and tracking and keeps its anchor, colour and size of box. A change to the palette or the margins in `config` reaches all three editions.

### 2 · Sides named by the direction of the text

<Excerpt region="heads" />

An even page's outer edge is the start of its lines in both directions, and an odd page's is the end, so `top-start` and `top-end` put the folios outside on an English verso and on an Arabic one. The box stripe is `side: 'start'` and the cover's text is anchored to `top-start`: none of them needs an override.

### 3 · Figures wrapped at the end of the line

<Excerpt region="pictures" />

`wrap: 'end'` puts each photograph on the right of an English column and on the left of an Arabic one, and the text runs beside it. The captions and alt texts come in each edition's language; the Arabic captions give the Hijri date first.

### 4 · The table in each edition's words and digits

<Excerpt region="table" />

The table's data is content, so it changes with the edition: Arabic-Indic digits and the Arabic decimal separator in the Arabic edition, a decimal comma in the Spanish one. Its style is general: the override only sets the Arabic face and size.

## Variations

### Write the overrides into a bundle

<RecipeLink slug="bundle-round-trip">The .postext round trip</RecipeLink> writes a book in two languages to one file. Since postext 1.58 `createBundle` stores the general settings once and each language's override beside its chapters, as `mergeConfigLayers` reads them here. The Sandbox book of this recipe opens that way: switch the Design panel between *All languages* and the Arabic edition to see which fields the Arabic edition sets.

### Print the Arabic edition for the Maghreb

`ar-MA` keeps the direction and the binding and writes the generated numbers, the folios and the figure numbers, in European digits. Retype the table's digits too.

```diff
-  locale: t({ en: 'en-gb', es: 'es', ar: 'ar' }), // ar: right to left, bound on the right
+  locale: t({ en: 'en-gb', es: 'es', ar: 'ar-MA' }), // ar-MA: European digits
```

## Pitfalls

- Arabic letters join, so a label tracked in the general settings must lose its `letterSpacing` in the Arabic override, or the engine raises a `joiningScriptLetterSpacing` warning. `textTransform: 'none'` goes with it: Arabic has no capitals.
- The lines of an Arabic edition are deeper, so its pages break in other places. Each edition here was fitted on its own: the Arabic box floats to the foot of its page (`placement="bottom"`) and its closing sentences run beside the last photograph.
