## What you'll build

Three pages of a Japanese school reader of 漢文 (kanbun), classical Chinese read as Japanese, on an A5 page bound on the right: three sayings of the *Analects* and Meng Haoran's quatrain *Spring Dawn*. Each passage is set in Zen Old Mincho at 15 pt in the Chinese order, with its 訓点 (kunten) beside the characters in vermilion, half their size: the return marks (返り点) at the lower left of a character tell the reader to read on and come back to it, and the okurigana (送り仮名) at its right add the Japanese endings. Under each passage its 書き下し文 (yomikudashi), the same text written out as Japanese, runs in Klee One, a school-book hand, with furigana where a reading is hard. Chinese classics with their own reading aids are <RecipeLink slug="zhuyin-vertical-reader">Nº 080</RecipeLink>.

## Method

### 1 · Mark each character with `:kunten`

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `:kunten[習]{kaeri="レ" okuri="フ"}` gives 習 the return mark レ and the okurigana フ; the text keeps the character, so search, copy and the PDF read the passage as written, and the marks take no character of it ([Marks, ruby and warichu](/en/docs/configuration#marks-ruby-and-warichu)). Read 学ビテ而時ニ習フレ之ヲ: レ on 習 sends the reader to 之 first and back, 之を習ふ; 而 has no mark and is not read. In vertical text the return mark sits at the lower left of its character and takes half a character of the line, and the okurigana sit at the right, starting halfway down the character, as JIS X 4051 sets them. `cjk.kunten` sets their size, half the text's, and their colour, the vermilion a reader writes them in; a passage is 15 pt on two lines of the grid, which leaves 27 pt beside each line for the okurigana.

### 2 · Jump with 一 二 and with 一レ

The numbers carry the reader further back. In 不二亦タ説バシカラ一乎 the reader skips 不, which has 二, reads 亦た説ばしから, and at 一 goes back up to 不: 亦た説ばしからずや. In 為政 the combined mark 一レ on 為 does both: 可二以テ為一レ師ト矣 reads 以て, 師と (レ), 為る, then back to 可 (一 to 二), 以て師と為るべし. The kanbun code points ㆑ ㆒ ㆓ are read as the characters they stand for, so a text copied from elsewhere keeps its marks.

### 3 · Let long okurigana push the next character on

An okurigana longer than its character does not run beside the next one: in 花落ツルコト知ル多少 the four kana of ツルコト take two characters' length, and 知 moves down to start after them, as JIS X 4051 sets them (§5.6.4). The line grows; its next break moves with it.

### 4 · Give each passage a label two lines tall

<Excerpt region="headings" />

Each book of the reader opens a page with its title, 論語 or 唐詩, the first with a small vermilion note on what it holds; each passage takes a label in gothic two body lines wide (2行取り), 学而第一 or 春暁　孟浩然, so the passages under it stay on the grid. The reading is a `:::paragraphs{style="kudashi"}` fence two characters down, in the body's face and size, with jukugo furigana: `{君子|くん|し}` gives each character its own reading, so a line could break between them.

### 5 · Load each voice with its characters

<Excerpt region="voices" />

The classical text and the reading are set in different faces, so each loads the files for its own characters: Zen Old Mincho for the passages and their marks, whose kana sit inside the `:kunten` attributes, Klee One for the readings and their furigana, Noto Sans JP for the labels. The quatrain is set two verses to the line with an ideographic space between them, a common way to print one, so the poem and its reading fit the page under the title.

## Variations

### Print the marks in black

A printed reader sets the kunten in the text's ink; leave out the colour.

```diff
-  kunten: { fontSize: em(0.5), color: col('vermilion'), placement: 'inline' },
+  kunten: { fontSize: em(0.5), placement: 'inline' },
```

### Set the marks between the lines

`placement: 'interlinear'` takes the return marks out of the line, into the gap beside the character's lower half, so the passage keeps the spacing of the bare text (白文). JIS sets them inline, the default.

```diff
-  kunten: { fontSize: em(0.5), color: col('vermilion'), placement: 'inline' },
+  kunten: { fontSize: em(0.5), color: col('vermilion'), placement: 'interlinear' },
```
