## What you'll build

Three sheets of Japanese manuscript paper (原稿用紙, genkō yōshi), the 400-square kind, with the first three paragraphs of Natsume Sōseki's *Botchan* copied out on them as a writer or a pupil would: 20 lines of 20 squares, read top to bottom from the right, in Klee One, a face drawn with a pen. The title stands in the first line from the third square and the author's name in the second, one square above the foot; each paragraph starts after one empty square. Every character, small kana and mark has a square of its own, and a 、 or 。 that would head a line is written below the last square of the line before. A red-brown frame, the sheet number and the paper's printed label 20×20 make the sheet. The Chinese page counted in characters is <RecipeLink slug="chinese-official-document">Nº 082</RecipeLink>.

## Method

### 1 · Draw the squares and give every mark a whole one

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. The [character grid](/en/docs/configuration-east-asian#character-grid) makes the type area 20 characters of 20 pt (7.1 mm) down and 20 lines of 27 pt (9.5 mm) across, and `show: true` draws its squares in a light grey of its own, on screen, and in the PDF when `renderToPdf` is given `characterGrid: true` (a grid is a proofing aid in a book, so a PDF leaves it out by default); the 2.5 mm strip between two lines is where a writer adds readings and corrections. A Japanese mark is half a glyph and half an em of blank, and a book takes in blanks where two marks meet or a bracket starts a line. On manuscript paper each mark keeps its square, so `compressAdjacent: false` sets 。」 in two squares and `trimLineStart: false` leaves a bracket at the head of a line its whole square ([East Asian typography](/en/docs/configuration-east-asian#east-asian-typography)).

### 2 · Follow the manuscript's line rules

Manuscript paper has its own kinsoku. A small kana or ー may start a line, each in its own square; `ja-loose` allows it. A 、 or 。 that would head a line is written in the last square of the line before, with the character there, or just below it; `hangingPunctuation: 'force'` hangs it below the twentieth square, and no line is ever squeezed. With `'allow'`, the default for Japanese, the engine first takes the mark into the line by squeezing a 、 inside it to half a square, which a printed book does and a manuscript never does: the rest of that line would sit half a square up, across the grid's lines.

### 3 · Write the title and the name where the rules put them

<Excerpt region="heading" />

The title is a heading the size and face of the text, so it fills squares like any other line: `lineSpan: 1` keeps it on one line of the grid and `indent: em(2)` leaves two empty squares above it ([Per-level overrides](/en/docs/configuration-text#per-level-overrides)). The name is a paragraph with `textAlign: 'end'` and `endIndent: em(1)`, which ends it one square above the foot, and the Markdown writes the blank square between family and given name as an ideographic space: `夏目　漱石`. The text starts on the third line, each paragraph after one empty square from `firstLineIndent: em(1)`.

### 4 · Print the sheet around the squares

<Excerpt region="sheet" />

The header's elements are drawn on the sheet itself: on a vertical page `x` runs right and `y` down from the top-left corner, unlike a heading's design, which is laid out in the turned frame of the text. The double frame stands 1.5 mm off the squares on three sides and 8 mm below them, where the hanging marks fall. `GRID` and `MARGIN` place it from the same numbers the page's margins are made of; the margins handed to the page are half a millimetre smaller, because the grid treats them as minimums and an exact fit could round to 19 lines.

### 5 · Set it in a pen face

<Excerpt region="pen" />

Klee One is drawn with a pen and keeps the Japanese forms of the kanji, so the sheets read as handwriting without the irregularity of a scan. Its vertical forms put 、。 in the upper right of their square and turn ー and the brackets, which is where a writer puts them. Like every Japanese family on Fontsource it comes as about 120 files, and `loadCjkFonts` fetches the ones the text needs ([Chinese, Japanese and Korean fonts](/en/docs/configuration-programmatic-usage#chinese-japanese-and-korean-fonts)).

## Variations

### Check a book's grid with the same squares

`show: true` works on any character grid: on the bunko of <RecipeLink slug="bunko-novel-furigana">Nº 117</RecipeLink> it draws the 38 × 15 squares over the text, a quick check that every line keeps to the grid and every heading to its three lines.

```diff
-  grid: { enabled: true, charsPerLine: CHARS, linesPerPage: LINES },
+  grid: { enabled: true, charsPerLine: CHARS, linesPerPage: LINES, show: true },
```

### Let the marks squeeze, as a printed page does

With the Japanese default the sheets read like a book typed on squared paper: a 、 that would head a line is taken in by squeezing another one, and the rest of that line sits half a square off the grid.

```diff
-  hangingPunctuation: 'force',
+  hangingPunctuation: 'allow',
```
