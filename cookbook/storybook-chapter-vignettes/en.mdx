## What you'll build

The opening pages of an Italian children's edition of Collodi's *Le avventure di Pinocchio*, set in Averia Serif Libre at 13 on 19 pt on a 190 × 240 mm page. A title page on the verso faces chapter I: a jointed wooden puppet in a suit of flowered paper dances on a Tuscan hill above the title in Fredericka the Great. Each chapter opens under a vignette in a sky-blue oval (Maestro Ciliegia's bench and the talking log for chapter I, Geppetto's yellow wig and a pot of polenta for chapter II), then the chapter line and Collodi's summary in centred italic. Running heads print only on pages 8 and 9, the two without an opener. The folios sit in wood-coloured discs, and two cherries close chapter I with no caption or number.

## Method

### 1 · A heading style for each vignette

The code for this step is [the short answer](#the-short-answer). An image element reads its `resourceId` as written and expands no `{attr.…}` placeholder, so `opener(id)` builds one heading style per vignette from a single design, and each chapter names its picture on its heading line: `# Capitolo II {style="polenta" summary="…"}` ([image elements](/en/docs/configuration#image-elements)). The chapter line is placed from the top of the container, 6 mm below the vignette's box, rather than anchored to `#vignette`. An image reserves no height, so texts anchored below it are measured as if the picture were not there; without `minHeight`, chapter I's text would then start 62 mm from the top of the page, inside the drawing. With `minHeight` as a floor of 14 lines, chapter I's text starts on the same line as chapter II's, whose summary runs one line longer; without it, chapter I's text starts 6.7 mm higher ([span and advanced design](/en/docs/configuration#span-and-advanced-design)).

### 2 · Draw the pictures in the page's palette

<Excerpt region="pictures" />

The `art` region builds each drawing as an SVG string from the hex values in `palette`, so the vignettes' sky is the same `#cfe4ec` as the box behind the puppet. A seeded random generator puts the flowers in the meadow and on the suit, and the lines on the log's bark, in the same places on every run. The layout reads only the sizes the resources declare; the canvas paints whatever `loadSvg()` has registered under each file id when it draws the page. The puppet and the vignettes are painted by image elements in the designs, the cherries by `::resource{id="ciliegie"}` in the Markdown.

### 3 · Make the title page a heading style

<Excerpt region="frontispiece" />

The title page is the first heading of the Markdown, `# Pinocchio {style="frontespizio" kicker="Le avventure di"}`, and its style paints everything on the page: the sky box and the puppet, 150 mm deep from the top edge, then the kicker from the attribute, the title, and the subtitle and author from the frontmatter. The style sets `span: 'page'` even though the book has one column, because a design kept in the column is clipped at the top of the text block, and the upper 22 mm of the sky would print as bare paper. The empty `footer` removes the folio disc the chapter openers print, and `pages: 'body'` already keeps the running heads off the page ([heading styles](/en/docs/configuration#heading-styles)).

### 4 · Running heads on pages of text, folios in discs

<Excerpt region="folios" />

`continuation` numbers the excerpt from book page 6, a verso, so the title page faces chapter I and the first folio printed is 7. `pages: 'body'` keeps the running heads off the title page and both openers, so they print only on pages 8 and 9, the book's title on the verso and the chapter's on the recto ([text elements](/en/docs/configuration#text-elements)). The folio is a design text in a fixed 7.4 mm square whose corner radius is half its side, which makes a circle; `verticalAlign: 'middle'` and the line height worked out in the comment centre the figures in it. The paper-coloured figures have a contrast ratio of 4.98:1 against the wood. `trail()` moves every tracked label right by half its tracking, because 1.4.1 centres a tracked line as if the space after its last letter were part of it.

### 5 · Close the chapter with an unnumbered tailpiece

<Excerpt region="tailpiece" />

A resource type prints its `captionPrefix` and number before the caption. With an empty prefix and no caption, nothing prints under the cherries; with `captionPrefix: 'Fregio'`, the same resource prints *Fregio 1.* under the drawing and grows 10 mm taller ([resource types](/en/docs/configuration#resource-types)). The type's `defaultPlacement` sets each resource where its `::resource` line stands, a quarter of the measure wide and centred, so the Markdown needs only `::resource{id="ciliegie"}` after the chapter's last paragraph. The other drawings share the type but no `:ref` or `::resource` names them, so they are never placed or counted.

## Variations

### Open every chapter on a recto

With `'odd'`, chapter II moves to page 11 and page 10 is left blank; the title page turns its own break off, because it inherits the level's and would otherwise move to page 7 after a blank page 6.

```diff
@@ the frontispiece style @@
   id: 'frontespizio',
+  breakBefore: { enabled: false },
@@ headings, in config() @@
-    levels: [{ level: 1, breakBefore: { enabled: true, parity: 'any' } }],
+    levels: [{ level: 1, breakBefore: { enabled: true, parity: 'odd' } }],
```

## Pitfalls

- In the Markdown, a no-break space (U+00A0) ties each of Collodi's dialogue dashes to its word, so a closing dash never opens a line; two more keep *di legno* and *in piedi* together at the end of their paragraphs. In 1.4.1 a line can still break at a no-break space when the paragraph has bold or italic. The only tied paragraph with an italic word, the one with *ohi* on page 9, has four ties, and on the capture each falls mid-line or at the paragraph's end.
