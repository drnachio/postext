## What you'll build

The opening pages of an Italian children's edition of Collodi's *Le avventure di Pinocchio*, set in Averia Serif Libre at 13 on 19 pt on a 190 × 240 mm page. A title page on the verso faces chapter I: a watercolour of the Tuscan hills, where a donkey cart brings firewood to a carpenter's workshop, above the title in Fredericka the Great. Each chapter opens under a watercolour vignette that fades into the paper (Maestro Ciliegia with his axe raised over the talking log for chapter I, Geppetto in his yellow wig taking the log for chapter II), then the chapter line and Collodi's summary in centred italic. All four pictures were generated with diffusion models from one style prompt and the palette of the page. Running heads print only on pages 8 and 9, the two without an opener. The folios sit in wood-coloured discs, and two cherries close chapter I with no caption or number.

## Method

### 1 · One heading style, a vignette named on each heading

The code for this step is [the short answer](#the-short-answer). An image element's `resourceId` takes the same placeholders as a design text, so `'{attr.vignetta}'` draws the resource the heading names, the way `{attr.summary}` prints its summary. Both chapters share the `capitolo` style and name their picture on the heading line: `# Capitolo II {style="capitolo" vignetta="polenta" summary="…"}`. A third chapter needs one more resource and one more heading, not another style ([image elements](/en/docs/configuration-page-layout#image-elements)). The chapter line is placed from the top of the container, 6 mm below the vignette's box, and the summary hangs 5 mm under it; the opener reserves room down to the summary's last line. With `minHeight` as a floor of 14 lines, chapter I's text starts on the same line as chapter II's, whose summary runs one line longer; without it, chapter I's text starts 6.7 mm higher ([span and advanced design](/en/docs/configuration-text#span-and-advanced-design)).

### 2 · Load the paintings as bitmap resources

<Excerpt region="pictures" />

Each picture is a JPEG in the recipe's `assets/` folder, declared at its pixel size: the layout reads only those sizes, and the canvas paints whatever `loadImage()` has registered under each file id when it draws the page. The vignettes and the cherries were generated on white and then multiplied by the paper colour `#fffaf0`, so their margins print as the page and the pictures have no visible edge. The frontispiece is cut to 190:150, the shape of its field, so the fitted image fills the field without a band of paper. The landscape and the vignettes are painted by image elements in the designs, the cherries by `::resource{id="ciliegie"}` in the Markdown.

### 3 · Make the title page a heading style

<Excerpt region="frontispiece" />

The title page is the first heading of the Markdown, `# Pinocchio {style="frontespizio" kicker="Le avventure di"}`, and its style paints everything on the page: the landscape, 150 mm deep from the top edge, then the kicker from the attribute, the title, and the subtitle and author from the frontmatter. Every element hangs from the page, not from the text block, so the painting reaches the top edge and both sides of the sheet while the heading itself stays in the book's one column. The empty `footer` removes the folio disc the chapter openers print, and `pages: 'body'` already keeps the running heads off the page ([heading styles](/en/docs/configuration-styles#heading-styles)).

### 4 · Running heads on pages of text, folios in discs

<Excerpt region="folios" />

`continuation` numbers the excerpt from book page 6, a verso, so the title page faces chapter I and the first folio printed is 7. `pages: 'body'` keeps the running heads off the title page and both openers, so they print only on pages 8 and 9, the book's title on the verso and the chapter's on the recto ([text elements](/en/docs/configuration-page-layout#text-elements)). The folio is a design text in a fixed 7.4 mm square whose corner radius is half its side, which makes a circle; `verticalAlign: 'middle'` and the line height worked out in the comment centre the figures in it. The paper-coloured figures have a contrast ratio of 4.98:1 against the wood. The tracked labels take no offset: the tracking after a line's last letter is left out when the line is centred.

### 5 · Close the chapter with an unnumbered tailpiece

<Excerpt region="tailpiece" />

A resource type prints its `captionPrefix` and number before the caption. With an empty prefix and no caption, nothing prints under the cherries; with `captionPrefix: 'Fregio'`, the same resource prints *Fregio 1.* under the drawing and grows 10 mm taller ([resource types](/en/docs/configuration-resources#resource-types)). The type's `defaultPlacement` sets each resource where its `::resource` line stands, a sixth of the measure wide and centred, so the Markdown needs only `::resource{id="ciliegie"}` after the chapter's last paragraph. The other pictures share the type but no `:ref` or `::resource` names them, so they are never placed or counted.

## Variations

### Open every chapter on a recto

With `'odd'`, chapter II moves to page 11 and page 10 is left blank; the title page turns its own break off, because it inherits the level's and would otherwise move to page 7 after a blank page 6.

```diff
@@ the frontispiece style @@
   id: 'frontespizio',
+  breakBefore: { enabled: false },
@@ headings, in config() @@
-    levels: [{ level: 1, breakBefore: { enabled: true, parity: 'any' } }],
+    levels: [{ level: 1, breakBefore: { enabled: true, parity: 'odd' } }],
```

## Pitfalls

- In the Markdown, a no-break space (U+00A0) ties each of Collodi's dialogue dashes to its word, so a closing dash never opens a line; two more keep *di legno* and *in piedi* together at the end of their paragraphs. The ties hold in a paragraph with bold or italic too, such as the one with *ohi* on page 9, which has four.
