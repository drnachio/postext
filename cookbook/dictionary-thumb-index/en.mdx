## What you'll build

Four pages from a new edition of Admiral Smyth’s *Sailor’s Word-Book* (1867), letters A to C, on a 150 × 200 mm page. The entries run in two justified columns of 8 pt Alegreya divided by a hairline, each headword bold in navy and each turnover line hung 1 em. A letter opens inside its column, as a four-line navy initial over a brass rule, and the entries run on without a page break. Down the fore-edge every page prints the whole thumb index, 22 tabs in a pale tint. The current letter’s tab is navy, 3.2 mm wider than the others, and sits one step lower for each letter. The Spanish edition keeps Smyth’s English headwords and translates the entries.

## Method

### 1 · Give each letter a heading style with its own tab

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `TABS` lists 22 tabs, with the rare letters sharing one (QR, UV, XYZ). Each tab is two body lines (7.34 mm) deep, so together they fill the 44 lines of the text block. The loop gives each letter a heading style whose `header` adds that letter’s navy tab, one step lower than the last, to the running heads and the pale index ([heading styles](/en/docs/configuration-styles#heading-styles)). Every cell is written twice, once per `parity`, and anchored to the outer edge of the page rather than to the header’s own container. That container is the head margin, as wide as the text block and reaching from the trim down to the text, while the tabs have to reach the fore-edge and run down all 44 lines. A page takes the header of the last section that starts on it, so <PageRef page={2}>page 2</PageRef> begins in A and shows the tab for B.

### 2 · Keep the running heads off the first page

<Excerpt region="running-heads" />

The heads are text elements anchored to the page and filtered by `parity` and `pages: 'body'` ([text elements](/en/docs/configuration-page-layout#text-elements)). The title on page 1 spans both columns, which makes page 1 an opener. The heads skip it, and its folio comes from the footer, an element set to `pages: 'opener'` that prints 6 mm below the text block. On a recto `{chapterTitle}` prints the letter of the last level-1 heading on or before the page, *Letter C*. `{firstMark.<key>}` and `{lastMark.<key>}` print a page’s first and last headword instead, the guide words most dictionaries carry; this recipe keeps the letter, which is what its tabs index.

### 3 · Colour the headwords and nothing else

<Excerpt region="entries" />

Each entry is one paragraph of a `:::paragraphs{style="entry"}` block, its turnover lines hung 1 em ([paragraph styles](/en/docs/configuration-styles#paragraph-styles)). The body’s `boldColor` keeps **Note.** on page 1 in ink, and the entry style’s `boldColor` prints the headwords in navy. A paragraph style has no `italicColor`, so the italic sub-entries keep the body’s ink.

### 4 · Drop a four-line initial into the column

<Excerpt region="letter-heads" />

`LETTER`, 55.8 pt, is the size at which a capital of Alegreya SC 900 (0.652 em tall) stands on the fourth baseline and reaches the cap line of the first body line. The letter and the brass rule end inside the fifth line, and the heading block snaps down to the next grid line, so each head is five lines deep and the entries under it stay on the grid. On <PageRef page={3}>page 3</PageRef> the C at the head of the second column is level with the first four lines of the first column. Level 1 sets `breakBefore: { enabled: false }`, which switches off the page break a level-1 heading has by default, and the letter styles inherit it, so B and C open mid-column.

### 5 · Lay the title across both columns

<Excerpt region="title" />

The title style spans the page with a band that holds the anchor, the name, a subtitle and a byline read from heading attributes, and a double rule ([span and advanced design](/en/docs/configuration-text#span-and-advanced-design)). The thin rule ends 45.1 mm below the top of the text block, so the band snaps to 13 grid lines, or 47.7 mm. The column rule starts under the band, on the line where the text of the two columns starts.

## Variations

### Print only the current tab

Printed thumb indexes usually show only the current letter’s tab; take `ladder` out of the header and the navy tab still steps down from A to C.

```diff
-  header: { elements: [...runningHeads, ...ladder,
+  header: { elements: [...runningHeads,
```

## Pitfalls

- A 60 mm column holds about 50 characters, and Smyth’s English cannot be reworded. In the capture, 18 of the 218 justified English lines (8 %) stretch their spaces past 1.7 times the normal width, against under 2 % in the Spanish, whose translation was fitted to the column. The English stood at 17 % before the five entries that set worst (*Aground*, *Aloft*, *Belay*, *Cockpit*, *Crow’s nest*) were swapped for others. Choose entries that set well, or widen the column.
