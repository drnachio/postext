## What you'll build

A unit of a second-year Arabic workbook on a 17 × 24 cm page, about a school garden. A watercolour band opens it, with the unit's tab and title and a dotted line each for name, class and date. Then come the exercises an Arab primary book prints: choose the right answer, with أ) ب) ج) three to a row; join the two columns; fill the gaps from a word bank; mark صح or خطأ; find seven garden words in a grid of letters; put the words in order and write the sentence on the line. The solutions close the unit. The same text is set twice: the Mashriq edition numbers everything in Arabic-Indic digits, ١ ٢ ٣, and the Maghreb edition in European ones. Both read from the right and bind on the right.

## Method

### 1 · Name no side

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `locale: 'ar'` sets the text right to left, binds the book on the right and picks the digits; the exercise style takes the abjad letters for the options and صح and خطأ for true or false from the language. The matching columns, the word bank, the word search and the writing lines are drawn as box designs, which a right-to-left page mirrors: the first column stands on the right, a word read across the grid runs from right to left, and the dots of the match face each other in the middle.

### 2 · The garden opener

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The painting is anchored to the page and keeps its place; the tab and the title are laid in the flow of the page, where `left` is the start of the line, so they stand on the right without a coordinate of their own. The folios stay physical, as running heads do, and name each side.

### 3 · Two editions, two sets of digits

<Excerpt region="editions" />

The configuration is a function, so each build gets its own object, and the second one only changes the locale. `ar-MA` writes the exercise numbers, the items, the key and the folios in European digits and leaves everything else as it was: the direction, the binding, the abjad letters and the words of the key.

## Variations

### Print the teacher's book

The answers then print in their places, in the colour of `answers`: the dots joined, the words in their blanks, the right boxes filled and the words ringed in the grid.

```diff
-const exercises = { solutions: { placement: 'directive' } };
+const exercises = { edition: 'teacher', solutions: { placement: 'none' } };
```

## Pitfalls

- Most Arabic faces have no ✓ or ✗: the true or false words are صح and خطأ, and the instruction asks for a mark in the box.
- The word search fills its empty cells with the letters of its own words, in their isolated forms. Choose words whose letters vary, or the grid fills with ة.
