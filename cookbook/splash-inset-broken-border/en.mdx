## What you'll build

Two pages from the storm in *The Porthcove Light*. The first is a splash: one picture of the lighthouse in the storm runs off every edge of the page, with a small framed panel of Maya at her window laid over its lower corner. The second opens with a wave bled off three edges; under it, a gull flies out of the keeper's panel across its border, and a narrow panel with no picture holds two captions on a night-blue ground. The trim is an American comic book, 168 × 259 mm with 3 mm of bleed, and the pages come in six languages.

## Method

### 1 · Bleed the splash off every edge

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `split="*"` makes one cell of the whole frame, and `bleed` on the page runs every panel that touches the frame out to the bleed box, 3 mm past the trim, with no border on those sides ([splitting the page](/en/docs/comics#splitting-the-page)). On page 2, `bleed="top start end"` on the wave bleeds only those three sides; its foot keeps the gutter to the panels below. `start` and `end` follow the reading direction, so the Arabic edition bleeds the same sides.

### 2 · Lay an inset over the splash

`inset="56 64 38 30"` places Maya's panel at 56%, 64% of the panel before it, 38% wide and 30% deep. An inset takes no cell: it is laid over the splash, keeps its own border and is read right after it. Its picture is cropped to its own box by its safe area, here Maya's face.

### 3 · Let a gull break the border

<Excerpt region="art" />

`pop=sp-tomas-pop` names a second picture for the keeper's panel: a transparent PNG the size of his picture with only the gull on it. Postext crops and places it exactly like the panel's art, then draws it after the border and outside the panel's clip, so the part of the gull that lies beyond the cell flies over the border and the margin ([panels and art](/en/docs/comics#panels-and-art)). `focus="45% 50%"` slides the crop left inside what the safe area allows, so the gull sits across the left edge rather than out in the margin.

### 4 · Leave a panel without a picture

<Excerpt region="palette" />

A `::panel` with no `art` is an empty cell with the panel style's border; `bg=night` fills it with a palette colour. Its captions, `at=top` and `at=bottom`, are butted to its top and bottom edges, a quiet beat after the storm.

## Variations

### Bleed the whole bottom tier as well

```diff
-::panel{art=sp-tomas pop=sp-tomas-pop focus="45% 50%"}
+::panel{art=sp-tomas pop=sp-tomas-pop focus="45% 50%" bleed="bottom start"}
```

### Give the inset a white frame

A named panel style changes one panel; `style=` on the panel picks it.

```diff
   panel: { borderWidth: pt(1.2), borderColor: col('ink'), background: col('paper') },
+  panelStyles: [{ id: 'framed', borderWidth: pt(3), borderColor: col('paper') }],
```

## Pitfalls

- The pop layer must have exactly the panel picture's pixel size: Postext crops it with the same window, so a smaller cut-out would land in the wrong place.
- Bleed needs `page.bleed`; without it a bleeding panel stops at the trim.
- A sound effect beside a bleeding edge may run into the bleed, past the trim, where the guillotine cuts it. The Japanese ザッバーン is a column five characters tall, so it is pinned lower than the Latin effects, at `60% 44%`.
