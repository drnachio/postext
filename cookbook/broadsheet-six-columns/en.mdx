## What you'll build

Four pages of *The Corrington Despatch*, an invented daily, on a 375 × 597 mm broadsheet. The body runs in six columns of 55.2 mm with 4 mm gutters and a hairline in each: a measure of about 38 characters at 9 pt, the measure of a real broadsheet. The front page carries a nameplate with two ears, a strip of teasers, a lead whose headline and photograph take four columns, a second story with a two-column photograph, a column of briefs, a funding table and a fact box across two columns, and an advertisement across the foot. Page 2 opens on a jump from the front, page 3 switches to five columns for the leaders, a comment piece and the letters, and the back page carries a theatre review and the weather.

## Method

### 1 · Six columns on a broadsheet trim

The code for this step is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `page.sizePreset: 'broadsheet'` sets the 375 × 597 mm trim, and `layoutType: 'multiple'` with `columnCount: 6` cuts the 351 mm text width into six equal columns ([Layout types](/en/docs/configuration#layout-types)). Each is (351 − 5 × 4) / 6 = 55.2 mm wide, which at 9 pt Newsreader holds about 38 characters, so the text needs hyphenation, `minWordSpacing: 0.8` to keep spaces open and `maxJustifyTracking: 15` to spread a loose line through its letters. The column rule draws a 0.4 pt hairline in every gutter, cut where a float or a box crosses it. Up to postext 1.17 a body had two columns at most, and <RecipeLink slug="newspaper-front-page">Nº 027</RecipeLink> set its four columns of briefs inside a box for that reason.

### 2 · A float across k columns takes the first run of empty columns

<Excerpt region="pictures" highlight="1-6" />

`placement.columns` gives a photo or a table its width in columns, gutters included: four columns of the front page are 232.7 mm. The engine looks for its slot from the column the text is in, left to right. A `top` float takes the head of the first run of k columns that hold no text yet and start level; a `bottom` float takes the foot of the column the text is in and of the empty ones after it. So the funding table, cited in a paragraph of column 3, sits at the foot of columns 3 and 4, and on <PageRef page={2}>page 2</PageRef> the fact box, fenced first, takes columns 1 and 2, which leaves columns 3 to 6 as the first level run for the four-column photo fenced after it. Fenced the other way round, the box would stack under the photo. A k as large as the page's column count (or larger) is page-wide, the same as `span: 'page'`.

### 3 · Headline boxes, fact boxes and advertisements

<Excerpt region="boxes" />

A `:::callout` with `placement="top"` and `columns="4"` floats across four columns as a photo does. The lead's headline and deck are such a box, fenced before the lead's text, so it takes columns 1 to 4 and the photograph stacks under it. The second story's headline box comes after the lead in the text, behind a `:::columnbreak`: the text is in column 5 by then, and columns 5 and 6 are the first empty pair. Each advertisement is a box with `columns="6"`, page-wide, fenced first on its page so that its foot is reserved before any text arrives, and the jump heads on pages 2 and 4 are page-span boxes, as in Nº 027. `balancing.trailing: false` lets every page fill its columns to the foot, since every page here closes a section.

### 4 · The nameplate is the front page's H1

<Excerpt region="nameplate" />

`# The Corrington Despatch` takes the `front` heading style: the name in Playfair Display Black at 64 pt, two ears filled from the heading's attributes, the dateline between a thin and a heavy rule. The opener is nine grid lines deep. The tinted teaser strip under it is a page-span box, a front-page element of its own: it closes the opener's band, and the lead's headline box takes columns 1 to 4 of the band it opens. Without the strip the box would take the same four columns right under the nameplate, since the head of column 1 under an opener is a slot like the heads of the other columns (up to postext 1.24 it was not, and the box landed from column 2).

### 5 · The opinion page runs in five columns

<Excerpt region="sections" />

`# Opinion {style="opinion"}` opens a section whose heading style sets `layout: { layoutType: 'multiple', columnCount: 5 }`. The section keeps the document's gutter and column rule and changes only the count, so the leaders, the comment piece and the letters run on a 67 mm measure, about 47 characters. The comment's headline box is `columns="3"`, fenced once the leader has begun in column 1, so it lands on columns 2 to 4 with its photograph under it, and two `:::columnbreak` lines send the column to column 2 and the letters to column 5.

## Variations

### Set a Berliner in five columns

The same paper on a 315 × 470 mm Berliner: five columns of 55 mm, the measure of the broadsheet's six. The folio rule takes the narrower width too; then refit each page.

```diff
-const layout = { layoutType: 'multiple', columnCount: 6, gutterWidth: mm(GUTTER),
+const layout = { layoutType: 'multiple', columnCount: 5, gutterWidth: mm(GUTTER),
-      size: { width: mm(375 - 2 * MARGIN.side) } }] };
+      size: { width: mm(315 - 2 * MARGIN.side) } }] };
-  page: { sizePreset: 'broadsheet', dpi: 150, backgroundColor: col('paper'), // 375 × 597 mm
+  page: { sizePreset: 'berliner', dpi: 150, backgroundColor: col('paper'), // 315 × 470 mm
```

## Pitfalls

- A float fenced after the text has passed its columns finds no slot on the page and opens the next one. On the front page the lead must end in column 4: one paragraph more and the second story's headline box and photograph move to page 2.
- Each page here is fitted by hand, as a sub-editor fits a newspaper: the advertisements' `:::space` lines and the length of each story set where the columns end. Change a story and refit its page.
