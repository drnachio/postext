## What you'll build

Page 7 of *The Porthcove Light*, a comic about a storm night at a lighthouse, with the letterer's style sheet in front of it. A trawler loses its engine, the keeper talks it in by radio, Maya waits with the cat, and in the morning the skipper thanks them. The page uses every kind of balloon Postext draws: speech, joined speech, thought, whisper, shout, radio, narration captions, an inner voice, an editor's note, a sound effect, an off-panel voice and a pinned balloon. The trim is an American comic book, 168 × 259 mm, and the page comes in six languages.

## Method

### 1 · Name the kind in the script line

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. A line of the script is `speaker{kind}: text`, and the kind is the id of a balloon style. The nine built-in styles cover the usual kinds: an oval with a curved tail for speech, a cloud with bubbles for thought, a dashed outline for a whisper, a burst with a wedge tail for a shout, a jagged outline with a zigzag tail for a voice through a radio ([balloon styles](/en/docs/comics#balloon-styles)). A style in the config with the same id changes only what it names: here the stroke colour, the caption yellow and the burst's 16 points. `caption`, `note` and `sfx` are reserved speakers: narration boxes butted into a corner, the editor's note at 0.8 × the size, and display letters with no balloon.

### 2 · Point each tail at a speaker

<Excerpt region="art" />

Each picture carries anchors in fractions of the picture: the mouth a speech tail aims at, the head a thought aims at, the face no balloon may cover ([speakers and anchors](/en/docs/comics#speakers-and-anchors)). The skipper is never drawn: his anchor in the storm picture is the radio set, in the sea picture his boat. When the speaker is not in the panel, `tail=end` (or `start`, `top`, `bottom`) runs the tail to that edge, and `at="44% 22%"` pins a balloon's centre to a point of the picture, so it stays there whatever the crop. Two lines in a row from one speaker join into one outline with one tail.

### 3 · Give a character a voice of their own

The cast in the short answer gives every `skipper:` line the radio style, so the script never repeats `{radio}`. A line can still say otherwise: in the morning panel the skipper calls from his boat, `skipper{speech tail=bottom}:`, in an ordinary balloon whose tail runs off the foot of the panel.

### 4 · Letter each edition in its own faces

<Excerpt region="editions" />

The same page comes in six languages: `content.<lang>.md` holds only the words. The Japanese balloons are set vertically in Zen Antique and its sound effects in Dela Gothic One, the Arabic ones in Playpen Sans Arabic with Lalezar for the thunder. Italics and bold apply where the face has them: the inner voice is italic in English, upright in Japanese ([lettering](/en/docs/comics#lettering)).

## Variations

### Use a wavy balloon for a sick or weak voice

Any other id becomes a style of its own, laid over the speech balloon.

```diff
 const balloonStyles = [
+  { id: 'weak', shape: 'wavy', stroke: col('ink'), fontScale: 0.9 }, // maya{weak}: …
   { id: 'speech', stroke: col('ink') },
```

### Join two lines with a neck instead

```diff
-  lettering: { fontFamily: LETTERING, fontSize: pt(7.5), color: col('ink'), inset: mm(1) },
+  lettering: { fontFamily: LETTERING, fontSize: pt(7.5), color: col('ink'), inset: mm(1),
+    joinSameSpeaker: 'connector' },
```

## Pitfalls

- A pin is a point of the picture, kept whatever the crop. A sound effect pinned so near the edge that it would leave the panel (KRA-KOOM at `at="16% 28%"`) is slid back inside, so it may sit a little off the point written.
- An off-panel voice is lettered by the border its tail points to: the skipper's thanks, `tail=bottom`, sits at the foot of the morning panel although it is the panel's first line. Point the tail at the top border when such a line has to be read first.
