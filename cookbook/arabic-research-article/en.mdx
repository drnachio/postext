## What you'll build

Five pages from an issue of an imaginary Arabic journal, on 17 × 24 cm, starting at page 87: an article on whether kashida justification slows readers down, set in Amiri at 12 pt with section heads in Noto Kufi Arabic. Its notes sit at the foot of each page and are numbered «(١)», «(٢)» from one on every page, as Arabic journals number them; the citations, written as `[@ayalon2016]` in the Markdown, are notes too, formatted by the Chicago note style with the Arabic CSL locale. A list of references follows, Arabic works first, and a two-column index closes the piece. The index files الكشيدة under ك and الصحف اليومية under ص: the article ال does not count. <RecipeLink slug="history-essay-chicago-notes">The history essay with Chicago notes</RecipeLink> does the same in English.

## Method

### 1 · Notes, citations and the index in one place

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. The footnote settings are the three that Arabic books want: `markerTemplate: '({n})'` for the parentheses, `numbering: 'page'` to start again on every page, and `noteNumberPosition: 'inline'` so the note opens with «(١)» on its line; the digits are the document's, and the rule stands on the right ([Footnotes](/en/docs/arabic-layout#footnotes)). The citations share the notes' numbering. `citations.locale: 'ar'` gives the CSL terms in Arabic, such as عدد for an issue number. `index.ignoreArticle` sorts each entry by the word after ال and folds the hamza forms, so أميري and الأعمدة both go under ا ([Contents and index](/en/docs/arabic-layout#contents-and-index)); it is on by default for an Arabic index and is written out here to be seen.

## Variations

### Number the notes through the article

Modern Arabic journals often number notes per article instead of per page.

```diff
-  numbering: 'page', // from (١) again on every page, as Arabic journals number them
+  numbering: 'chapter',
```

### Cite in author and date

```diff
-  style: 'chicago-notes-bibliography', notes: 'footnote', locale: 'ar',
+  style: 'apa', locale: 'ar',
```

## Pitfalls

- The punctuation a CSL style writes is the style's: commas, semicolons and quotation marks between the parts of a note are Latin, as in الهاشمي, الحرف العربي. Dates and numbers come from the data, so the Arabic works carry `issued: {literal: "٢٠١٩"}` and locators such as `[@hashimi2019, ٧٧]`; the Latin works keep 0–9.
- A note or a reference to a Latin work runs in the direction of the Arabic page: its final full stop stands at the left end of the line.
