## What you'll build

Five pages of a life-science journal article on A4: Jumper et al.'s *Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold*, abridged from Nature under CC BY 4.0. A blue band opens the first page over the title, all 34 authors in 7.4 pt with raised affiliation numbers, and the summary set bold across the page. Fig. 1 spans the page under it: a bar chart of the CASP14 accuracies the text quotes, and the backbone of target T1049 traced from the PDB with the AlphaFold model drawn inside it, coloured by confidence. The text cites with Nature's raised numbers. The main text closes on references 1–35. The Methods follow in 8.2 pt, and a work they cite for the first time takes the next number, so their own list starts at 36, as in the journal.

## Method

### 1 · Two reference lists, one numbering

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. Citations take their numbers in order of first citation, so you do nothing to continue the count: jackhmmer, first cited in the Methods, gets 36, while the Amber force field, cited earlier in the main text, keeps its 30. The lists come from `:::bibliography{scope=new}`, which prints only the works no earlier list has printed. The pen puts the same block after the Discussion and after the Methods: the first sets 1–35, the second 36–55, and Amber is not printed again. The Methods heading is a level-1 heading with a style of its own; its `bodyStyle` sets the section in 8.2 pt on 10.8 pt, `breakBefore: { enabled: false }` keeps it in the column, and `runningChapter: false` keeps the running heads on the article's title. For your own paper, paste your `.bib` export into a `:::references{format=bibtex}` block, cite with `[@key]` straight after the word, and change `nature` to your journal's style.

### 2 · A title block for 34 authors

<Excerpt region="title" />

The author list is one heading attribute, `authors="John Jumper^1,4^, Richard Evans^1,4^, …"`, set in Noto Sans at 7.4 pt across the full measure, where 34 names take five lines. `inlineMarks` raises the affiliation numbers. The affiliations and the equal-contribution note go to the end, under *Author information*, as Nature prints them, so the opening page keeps room for the summary and the figure. No author is dropped: the running head on the versos shortens the list to *Jumper et al.*

### 3 · The summary across the page, Fig. 1 under it

<Excerpt region="resources" />

The summary is a box with `span: 'page'` and no frame, its text in Noto Sans semibold. Fig. 1 follows it as `::resource{id="fig1"}`, and its placement, `{ position: 'here', span: 'page' }`, sets it across both columns at that point; the two columns of text start under its caption. The caption begins with a bold sentence, as Nature's legends do; the type's `captionPrefix` and the caption style's `labelSeparator` make the label read *Fig. 1 |*. Table 1 is embedded where it stands in the Methods with `position: 'here'` and keeps to its column.

### 4 · A protein backbone drawn from open coordinates

<Excerpt region="art" />

The coordinates were read once, when the recipe was written, from PDB entry 6Y4F (CC0) and from the AlphaFold Database model of the same protein (CC BY 4.0), superposed on their Cα atoms and turned onto the principal axes of the experiment. The pen keeps them as three short arrays. Each trace is a chain of Bézier segments through the Cα positions, painted from the farthest to the nearest with a paper halo, which gives depth without shading. The model's colours are the pLDDT bands the AlphaFold Database uses, read from the B-factor column of its file.

### 5 · Greek letters and accents in the PDF

Fontsource splits each face by script. `loadFonts(FONTS, markdown)` reads the text it is given and loads each face's latin-ext file when it sets Ž or Š and its greek file when it sets α or χ; `fontsourceProvider` embeds the same files in the PDF from the characters each face sets. Cα, χ, Žídek and Šali take no code of their own: the recipe passes the markdown, which holds every content slot, and the kit's provider.

## Variations

### Keep the Methods at the text size

Some journals set the Methods in the same type as the text; drop the section's body style and keep the heading.

```diff
-  bodyStyle: { fontSize: pt(8.2), lineHeight: pt(SMALL) } };
+};
```

### Number in brackets

Some journals in the same field print [1] on the line.

```diff
-  style: 'nature', // raised 1–4, "Nature 577, 706–710 (2020)." entries
+  style: 'nature', marker: 'brackets',
```

### Print one list at the end

Journals that print every reference after the Methods, as *Science* does, keep only the second block.

```diff
-const markdown = [/* @content */ '', references, /* @content:methods */ '', references,
+const markdown = [/* @content */ '', /* @content:methods */ '', references,
```

### Use the Vancouver list

For a medical journal, start from the bundled NLM style, as the <RecipeLink slug="medical-article-vancouver">medical article in Vancouver style</RecipeLink> does.

## Pitfalls

- Nature names up to five authors and cuts a longer list to the first, followed by *et al.* Where the paper's own list does that, write `and others` in the BibTeX entry (`{Senior, Andrew W. and others}`) and the style prints *Senior, A. W. et al.*; in CSL-YAML, end the list with `"others"`.
- A heading style's `bodyStyle` changes the text's size and leading, not the baseline grid: the Methods headings use `snapToGrid: false`, or the line under each one would jump to the next line of the main text's grid.
