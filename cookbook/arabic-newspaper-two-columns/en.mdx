## What you'll build

Two pages of *الساحل*, an imaginary daily of an imaginary port city, on a 246 × 328 mm compact. The front page carries the paper's name in Aref Ruqaa at 84 pt, the lead story on the town's new tram under a headline across the page and a map drawn in code, and a strip of three briefs at the foot. Page 2 opens the local section under a blue flag, with four stories and the day's prayer times. Everything reads from the right: the first body column, the briefs, the table's columns, the ears of the nameplate. <RecipeLink slug="newspaper-front-page">The English front page</RecipeLink> sets the same furniture left to right.

## Method

### 1 · Two columns, the first on the right

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `layoutType: 'double'` and the column rule are the same settings as in an English paper; `locale: 'ar'` turns the page, so the story fills the right-hand column first and carries on at the head of the left one ([Page order and right binding](/en/docs/arabic-layout#page-order-and-right-binding)). A headline across both columns is a page-span box with no frame, and the columns under it start level. The In brief strip is a page-span box floated to the foot with `:::columns{count=3}` inside, and its columns run right to left too, as does the prayer-times table on page 2: a table set in the document's direction puts its first column on the right.

### 2 · A nameplate between two ears

<Excerpt region="nameplate" />

The front page's first-level heading is the paper's name, painted by a heading style. Its design is drawn in the mirrored page, so `top-right` is the start of the line: the issue and the date go there, the city and the price at the other end ([What left, right, start and end mean](/en/docs/arabic-layout#what-left-right-start-and-end-mean)). Ruqʿa is a display hand for a few words at a large size; it has no kashida and the body never uses it.

### 3 · Section flags and folio lines

<Excerpt region="inside" />

An inside page opens with a first-level heading drawn as a flag on a heavy rule. The folio line is a header, so it keeps its physical sides: page 2, an even page, has its number at the right-hand corner, the outer one in a paper that opens from the right. The front page clears the header in its heading style.

### 4 · A map drawn in code

<Excerpt region="art" />

The tram map is an SVG of paths and circles in the palette's colours, with no text in it: the place names stay in the caption, in the type of the page. The picture type has no counter and no label, because newspapers do not number their pictures.

## Variations

### Three columns in the briefs, four on a wider page

The strip is a box with its own columns, so it can have more than the body's two.

```diff
-:::columns{count=3 breaks="2,3"}
+:::columns{count=4 breaks="2,3,4"}
```

### European digits for a Maghreb paper

```diff
-  locale: 'ar', // written out, never LANG (gotcha: arabic-locale-tag): rtl, digits ٠–٩
+  locale: 'ar-MA', // rtl, digits 0–9
```

## Pitfalls

- The prayer times are typed in Arabic-Indic digits in the table's cells: `locale` changes only the numbers the engine writes, so a Maghreb edition retypes them.
