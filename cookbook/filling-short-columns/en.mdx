## What you'll build

Three pages of *Littoral*, a coastal magazine: a long read on the Fresnel lens, opened by a painting of a lit lens in its lantern. The text runs in two ragged columns of Public Sans with a blank grid line between paragraphs, and the widow and orphan rules keep at least two lines of a paragraph on each side of a break. The pen sets the feature twice. Without the setting, on <PageRef page={1}>the opener</PageRef> the left column ends a line above the right one: the next paragraph cannot start with a single line at the foot. With it, on <PageRef page={4}>the same page</PageRef>, the first paragraph wraps to one more line and both columns end on the last grid line. Overleaf, the setting also takes the extra air that balancing had put above two crossheads.

## Method

### 1 · Give a ragged column a lever of its own

<Excerpt region="answer" />

Column balancing fills a short column by adding grid lines above a heading, after a list, under a box or a figure, and as a last resort by setting a justified paragraph a line looser. A ragged column of running text has none of these between its crossheads, so the line the widow and orphan rules hold back stays empty. `fillShortColumns` lets a ragged paragraph of that column wrap a line longer within its own word spacing; when no paragraph can, it sets one a line shorter so that the paragraph after the break moves up and fills the line. The engine keeps the change only when fewer columns end short, so turning it on never makes a page worse. `maxLinesBeforeLoose: 0` tries the paragraphs before the headings, so every crosshead keeps one grid line of air above it.

### 2 · Set the feature twice and compare

<Excerpt region="builds" />

Both documents come from one config and one Markdown string. The second build passes the config as written; the first passes a copy with the setting off, so the bundle that opens in the Sandbox carries `fillShortColumns: true`. The two buttons over the desk switch between the builds. Only the pages that flow on are filled to the foot: page 3 closes the feature, and its columns are cut level with each other, as they are in both builds.

### 3 · Open on the painting

<Excerpt region="opener" />

The painting is an image element anchored to the bleed, 150 mm deep, so it reaches the trim on three sides, which no float does. The kicker, headline, standfirst and byline come from the attributes of the `#` line. The byline ends in a 6 mm padding that counts toward the opener's height, so the columns start on the first grid line below it.

### 4 · Running heads off the opener

<Excerpt region="heads" />

The verso names the magazine and the issue from the frontmatter, the recto the feature, and `pages: 'body'` keeps both off the opener, which has only a folio at its foot. The label sits 0.35 mm lower than the folio, so the two sizes share a baseline. The hairline under them is 197 mm long, the width of the text block.

## Variations

### Let the crossheads take the slack first

With the default order, a heading takes up to two grid lines before a paragraph is set longer, and the crossheads overleaf open up again.

```diff
-  maxLinesBeforeLoose: 0,
+  maxLinesBeforeLoose: 2,
```

### Justify the text

A justified paragraph can already run a line looser without the setting, within `maxWordSpacing`. With `fillShortColumns` it may also run a line tighter, and it is never set looser than its own loosest line.

```diff
-  textAlign: 'left', firstLineIndent: pt(0), paragraphSpacing: true, // a grid line between
+  textAlign: 'justify', firstLineIndent: pt(0), paragraphSpacing: true, // a grid line between
```

## Pitfalls

- The setting closes a gap of one line. A column two lines short, such as one that ends after a paragraph space with room for only one line of the next paragraph, may stay short: copy-fit it by a sentence.
- A paragraph whose new setting would end on a runt is not used, so a column can stay short when its only paragraphs are short ones.
