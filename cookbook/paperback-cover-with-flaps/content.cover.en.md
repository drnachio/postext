:::paragraphs{style="cover-blurb"}
On All Souls’ Night the bell of a ruined Templar chapel rings by itself above Soria, and a young noble rides out to fetch a blue sash his cousin says she lost on the mountain. A hunter follows a woman with green eyes to a spring on the slopes of the Moncayo.
:::

:::paragraphs{style="cover-blurb"}
Bécquer wrote these two legends in his twenties, for the Madrid newspaper *El Contemporáneo*, from stories he heard in the towns where they happen.
:::

:::paragraphs{style="flap-head"}
Gustavo Adolfo Bécquer
:::

:::paragraphs{style="flap-text"}
was born in Seville in 1836 and went to Madrid at eighteen to make a living as a writer. He wrote for newspapers, translated plays and worked as a government censor of novels. In 1864 he spent months in the monastery of Veruela, at the foot of the Moncayo, and sent from there the letters *Desde mi celda*. He died in Madrid in 1870, at thirty-four. His *Rimas* and *Leyendas* were collected by his friends and published the following year.

The translators
:::

:::paragraphs{style="flap-text"}
Cornelia Frances Bates (1826–1908) began to study Spanish at seventy-one and read Bécquer in two small green volumes with a red dictionary beside them. Her daughter Katharine Lee Bates (1859–1929), professor of English at Wellesley College, finished the translation they had planned together and published it in New York in 1909 as *Romantic Legends of Spain*.
