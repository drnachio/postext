## What you'll build

The reading list of a six-week seminar on the history and sociology of science, set on a 210 × 280 mm page in two columns. A deep green field takes the top half of the first page, with the course title in Spectral Light at 50 pt; the text below is the convenor's guide to each week. The references are the seminar's Zotero collection, exported as BibTeX and pasted into the document without touching a line: Zotero's citation keys, its braces around capitals, its `{\"o}` accents, `month = dec`, the `file`, `keywords` and `urldate` fields. The text cites eight of the works. The other five, background reading the seminar does not discuss, reach the list through `nocite`.

## Method

### 1 · Name the style once

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. The citation engine is registered before the first build and the style is one setting: Chicago author-date writes "(Shapin 1984)" for `[@shapin_pump_1984]` and "Shapin and Schaffer (1985)" for `@shapin_leviathan_1985` in a sentence. The keys are the ones Zotero built from the first author, the first significant word of the title and the year, so `gieryn_boundary-work_1983` keeps its hyphen; Postext reads a hyphen or an underscore inside a key as part of it.

### 2 · Paste the export as Zotero writes it

The last block of the document holds the export, one entry per work, tab-indented as Zotero writes it:

```bibtex
@article{gieryn_boundary-work_1983,
	title = {Boundary-{Work} and the {Demarcation} of {Science} from {Non}-{Science}: …},
	url = {https://www.jstor.org/stable/2095325},
	doi = {10.2307/2095325},
	urldate = {2026-08-21},
	journal = {American Sociological Review},
	author = {Gieryn, Thomas F.},
	month = dec,
	year = {1983},
	keywords = {demarcation, week 4},
	pages = {781--795},
	file = {JSTOR Full Text PDF:/Users/hps214/Zotero/storage/2HJ8M6PA/Gieryn - 1983 - ….pdf:application/pdf},
}
```

The reader drops the braces that protect capitals, composes `{\"o}` and `{\'e}` into ö and é, turns `--` into an en dash and reads `month = dec` as a bare month name. `urldate` becomes the access date and `language` the item's language; `file`, `keywords`, `abstract` and `shorttitle` are read past, so nothing from your hard disk reaches the page.

### 3 · Bring in the works the text does not cite

The front matter lists them by key:

```yaml
---
title: "Science as a Social Practice"
nocite: "@fleck_genesis_1979, @merton_sociology_1973, @latour_science_1987, @rheinberger_toward_1997, @lopez_pinero_ciencia_1979"
---
```

The list holds every cited work plus these five, sorted together by author as Chicago asks; nothing marks an entry as uncited. `nocite: "@*"` lists the whole export, and `bibliography.includeUncited: true` does the same from the configuration, for a design that always prints the whole library.

## Variations

### List the whole Zotero collection

When the export is the list, skip the keys: every entry in the block prints, cited or not.

```diff
-nocite: "@fleck_genesis_1979, @merton_sociology_1973, @latour_science_1987, …"
+nocite: "@*"
```

### Change the style

The same keys give a Harvard list (Cite Them Right) with no other change.

```diff
-  style: 'chicago-author-date', // '(Gieryn 1983)', 'Shapin and Schaffer (1985)'
+  style: 'harvard-cite-them-right',
```

## Pitfalls

- A key in `nocite` that matches no entry is dropped without a warning. Copy the keys from the export rather than typing them, and count the entries on the page.
- Zotero builds the keys from the data: correct an author's name or a title in Zotero and the next export may change the key, which breaks every citation of it. Pin the keys before the text is written: Zotero reads a line `Citation Key: …` in an item's Extra field.
- Zotero exports UTF-8 by default, with the accents as they are. The block here was exported with the Western encoding option, which writes `{\"o}`; Postext reads both.
- Chicago's 18th edition leaves out the place of publication, so the `address` field does not print, and an article with a DOI prints the DOI and not the JSTOR `url`.
