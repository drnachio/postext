# Music notation

**The music is text in the book; its look is a setting.**

A score in Postext is written as notation, not drawn. A fenced block in the chapter holds the music in one of four text formats, and the engine engraves it with [Verovio](https://www.verovio.org), the engraving library of the Music Encoding Initiative. The score is engraved at the width of its column and cut into **systems**, the lines of music a reader follows from left to right. Each system is placed as a block of its own, so a score longer than the room left on a page goes on at the head of the next column, and a score never breaks inside a system. Staff size, music font, bar numbers and spacing come from the [`scoreStyle` section of the configuration](/en/docs/configuration-resources#score-style), so a whole book changes its look by changing one setting.

Music is opt-in: a document with no notation fence lays out as before, and the engraver (about 3.7 MB) is loaded only by books that have music.

## A score in the text

````md
The perfect authentic cadence closes on the tonic in the soprano:

```humdrum {alt="Chorale phrase in G major ending on a perfect authentic cadence"}
**kern	**kern	**kern	**kern
*clefF4	*clefGv2	*clefG2	*clefG2
*k[f#]	*k[f#]	*k[f#]	*k[f#]
*M4/4	*M4/4	*M4/4	*M4/4
4GG	4B	4d	4b
4D	4d	4f#	4a
=	=	=	=
1GG	1B	1d	1g
==	==	==	==
*-	*-	*-	*-
```

The half cadence stops on the dominant instead.
````

A melody with words, in ABC:

````md
```abc
X:1
T:Evening hymn
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:F
c | A2 B | c2 A | G2 F | E2 c | A2 B | c2 A | G2 E | F2 |]
w: Now the day is o-ver, night is draw-ing nigh, sha-dows of the eve-ning steal a-cross the sky.
```
````

The fence takes two attributes:

- `alt="…"`: what the score is, for screen readers, the tagged PDF and the EPUB. Unset, the title and composer written in the notation (ABC `T:` and `C:`, MusicXML `<work-title>` and `<creator type="composer">`) are read out. The title is not printed: give it in the text or a caption.
- `style="…"`: a named score style of the book (`scoreStyles`), such as one with smaller staves for examples in a box.

A score in the text is not numbered and has no caption, like a display formula. It takes the score style's margins above and below, the system gap between two systems, and the text after it goes back on the baseline grid.

## Numbered music examples

A music example that the text cites ("see Example 3.2") is a resource of kind `score`, set like a figure: numbered, captioned, floated or placed where it stands, and cited with `:ref`.

```json
{
  "id": "ex-cadence",
  "typeId": "example",
  "kind": "score",
  "caption": "Perfect authentic cadence in G major",
  "score": { "format": "musicxml", "source": "<?xml version=\"1.0\"?>…", "styleId": "small" }
}
```

```md
The cadence in :ref{id="ex-cadence"} closes the phrase.

::resource{id="ex-cadence"}
```

A resource typed `example` is numbered with the built-in **Example** type, in the document's language: Example, Ejemplo, Exemple, Notenbeispiel, Esempio, Exemplo, 谱例, 譜例, مثال. The type is added to a book's [resource types](/en/docs/configuration-resources#resource-types) when a resource uses it; define a type of your own for another name. In a bundle (`preset.json`), the resource may point to a notation file instead of carrying its text:

```json
{ "id": "ex-cadence", "typeId": "example", "kind": "score", "file": "resources/ex-cadence.musicxml" }
```

A file ending in `.abc`, `.musicxml`, `.mei` or `.krn` is read as a score whatever the `kind` says. The [command line](/en/docs/command-line) reads the same files from a `--resources` folder as music examples, and the Sandbox's Resources panel creates one with **New › New score** or from a dropped file.

A score resource is kept whole: it floats or is set as one block. A piece longer than a page belongs in a fence, which breaks between systems.

## The look of a score

Every score follows `scoreStyle`; a named style laid over it changes only what it sets:

```ts
const config: PostextConfig = {
  scoreStyle: {
    staffSize: { value: 5.5, unit: 'mm' },
    musicFont: 'Leland',
    barNumbers: 'system',
    minSystemsAtBreak: 2,
  },
  scoreStyles: [
    { id: 'small', name: 'Small examples', staffSize: { value: 4.5, unit: 'mm' }, barNumbers: 'none' },
  ],
};
```

- **Staff size**: the height of a five-line staff. School books use 6 to 7 mm, textbooks and methods 5 to 6 mm, examples in the running text 4 to 5 mm.
- **Music font**: Bravura (the traditional engraved look), Leland (lighter), Petaluma (handwritten), Leipzig and Gootville.
- **Text font**: lyrics, tempo marks and directions, in the body font unless set. A word wider in this font than in the engraver's Times is set smaller, so syllables never run into each other.
- **Spacing**, **bar numbers**, **system gap**, **margins** and **ink**.
- **Systems kept together** (`minSystemsAtBreak`): the fewest systems a score leaves at the foot of a column or carries over. With 2, a single system is never left alone; a score with fewer than four systems moves whole.

Every field, its type and default are in [Configuration › Score style](/en/docs/configuration-resources#score-style). The Sandbox edits them in **Design › Figures & tables › Music scores** and **Score styles**.

## In every output

- **Canvas and HTML**: each system is painted as vector paths and words in the score's ink; the HTML viewer names the score with its alt text.
- **PDF**: vector drawings, the words in the book's embedded faces. In a tagged PDF each score is a `Figure` with its alt text.
- **EPUB**: a reflowable EPUB shows the systems as inline SVG as wide as the reading column, read as one image; a fixed-layout EPUB keeps the printed pages.
- **Word**: an exported document keeps the notation fence as written, like a code listing, so the score comes back when the document is imported again.

## Working with a music publisher

Postext typesets the book around the music; the notation software stays where the music is written.

1. Engrave or correct the music in Dorico, Sibelius, Finale or MuseScore, and export each example as MusicXML (`.musicxml`, uncompressed).
2. Put the files in the book's resources and type them `example`. Short one-voice examples can be written in ABC straight in the chapter.
3. Measure the staff size and match the music font of the house style in `scoreStyle`; examples in boxes or margins get a named style.
4. Lay out the book and check the page breaks. The bars per system follow the column width, the staff size and the spacing.

The [postext-port skill](/en/docs/skill) follows the same steps when it ports a music textbook, a method or a songbook.

## Limits

- Systems are filled to the width of the column: system breaks written in the notation are not kept yet.
- The ABC reader takes one voice.
- Compressed MusicXML (`.mxl`) must be unzipped first.
- A whole edition of a score (an orchestral score, parts, page turns planned bar by bar) is the work of notation software, not of a book typesetter.
