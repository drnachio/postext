# Document format: pictures, tables and videos


## Resources

Images, SVGs, tables and videos are not written inline. They are declared once as **resources** (managed in the Sandbox's [Resources panel](/en/docs/sandbox#resources-panel), which handles image and SVG upload, an interactive table editor, YouTube, Vimeo and uploaded videos, and caption and placement editing) and then connected to your prose by id. **Referencing a resource is enough to incorporate it** — you mention it once with an inline `:ref{id="…"}`, and the engine floats the figure or table into the first free slot after that reference — the bottom of the column you mention it in, the top of the next column, a band of the next page — just as a print typesetter would. You do not place it a second time.

Both forms below are new syntax that does not clash with CommonMark, so a document using them still reads as plain text in any other markdown viewer.

### Inline reference (the primary form)

Refer to a resource from within prose with `:ref{id="…"}`. The first reference both **incorporates** the resource (so it gets placed on the page) and renders its computed number, prefixed by the type's short label by default:

```md
As shown in :ref{id="lighthouse-diagram"}, the lantern room sits above the gallery.
```

renders as: *As shown in Fig. 1.7, the lantern room sits above the gallery.* — and the diagram itself floats to the nearest free slot after the sentence (the bottom of this column, the top of the next, or a band of the next page), while this sentence and the text after it flow on uninterrupted.

The running text is never broken at the reference point. Where the resource lands — the first free slot, or only a top or bottom slot; within a single column or across the full width — is governed by its **placement** (see [Placement](#placement) below), and by where you mention it: the search starts right after the reference.

### Block embed (optional, explicit inline placement)

Occasionally you want a resource to sit at an exact point in the flow rather than float. Opt out of floating by giving the resource `placement.position: "here"` and embedding it with `::resource{id="…"}` on its own line:

```md
Here is the floor plan we discussed.

::resource{id="lighthouse-diagram"}

The keeper's quarters occupy the eastern wing.
```

For a floated resource the `::resource` directive is unnecessary — the `:ref` already placed it, and a redundant `::resource` for the same id is simply treated as another reference, not a second copy. A `::resource` only renders the resource inline when its resolved placement is `"here"`. An inline resource keeps a line of space above it (the float gap), like a float would, unless the block before asks for more. In the running text it keeps the same space below it; then the text after it goes back onto the baseline grid, which can add up to a line more. A heading, a list, a box or another inline resource right after it shares that space with its own space above: the larger of the two applies, not both. (Up to postext 1.4 the space below was only what the grid snap left, anywhere from nothing to a line; `layout.inlineResourceGap: 'above'` keeps that rule, and books saved before 1.5 are read with it.) Inside a box (`:::callout`) the resource keeps the same space, a line of the box's own text above it and, with `'around'`, below it; at the top or the foot of the box the padding sets it off instead. (Up to postext 1.4 it sat right against the text of the box; `layout.inlineResourceGapInBoxes: false` keeps that, and books saved before 1.5 whose boxes embed a resource are read with it.) A `"here"` resource the text cites with `:ref` and no `::resource` embeds is on no page: the build reports it (`hereNotEmbedded`) at its first reference.

An inline resource whose placement also says `span: 'page'` (`placement: { position: 'here', span: 'page' }`) spans the page at the point of its directive in a layout of several columns, as a page-span box does: the columns above it close level (balanced, or the resource moves to the head of the next page when they cannot be), it is set across the content width with the same space above and below, and the text goes on in every column under it. On a one-column page it is set as before, at the full measure. A `span: 'column'` resource with `columns` set keeps to its column when it is embedded inline: the columns count only for floats. Since postext 1.19.

The `id` must match a resource defined in the Resources panel. The engine renders the resource (bitmap, SVG, or table) with its caption drawn underneath as a figure/table foot. The caption text is composed from the resource type's `captionPrefix`, the computed number, and the resource's own caption — e.g. **Figure 1.7. The original lighthouse plan.** Its typography is governed by [Configuration › Caption style](/en/docs/configuration-resources#caption-style): label and description share one typeface and size, while the label keeps independent bold/italic/colour settings; the gap above the caption defaults to `0.75em` and the alignment to left. The caption can instead sit above the resource (`captionStyle.position: 'above'`, globally or per resource type), optionally on a coloured bar. A resource may also carry a `note` — a short source line or credit, with the same inline formatting and `:ref` marks as the caption — set in a smaller run under the resource (under the caption when the caption is below, under the body when it is above) and styled through `captionStyle.note`.

**Line breaks in captions and notes.** A caption or a note is one paragraph set at the measure of its slot, and a newline typed in it is a space. To start a new line, write `\\` — the forced break of titles — or end the line with a backslash, Markdown's hard break: `¹ Measured at 20 °C. \\ ² Mean of three runs.` sets each footnote of a wide table on a line of its own. The line before a break keeps its natural width, never stretched to the measure, and two breaks in a row make no empty line (a no-break space between them does). In a table cell `\\` does what a newline does: it opens a new paragraph; the indentation of the line after it is kept, so two leading spaces still nest a list item. In the text itself the two backslashes are always a break, with no escape: to print them, set them in inline code, where backslashes print as written. In a link's destination they stay part of the URL, and in a directive's attributes (a `:ref`'s `text`) part of the value; inside a chip, which is set on one line, a break is a space. (Up to postext 1.4 the backslashes printed, and a caption or a note broke only where the measure ran out.)

Table resources draw their own grid, styled through [Configuration › Table style](/en/docs/configuration-resources#table-style): body and header cells have fully independent typography, the header background defaults to `#f0f0f0`, borders to `0.75pt`, and `cellPadding` to `0.375em` — any field left unset inherits from the body text. Column widths are part of the table itself: `TableModel.columnWidths` holds one relative weight per column (`[2, 1, 1]` gives the first column half the width); when unset, columns split the width equally. A table can also be set in a named variant: `table.styleId` picks one of the document's `tableStyles` (see [Configuration › Named table styles](/en/docs/configuration-resources#named-table-styles)), whose unset fields inherit `tableStyle`; an unknown or missing id keeps `tableStyle`.

A cell places its content with `TableCell.align` (`left`, `center`, `right`; list items stay flush left) and `TableCell.verticalAlign` (`top`, the default, `middle` or `bottom`). The vertical alignment moves the cell's whole content — the image and the text under it, as one unit — inside a cell taller than it: a row stretched by a longer neighbour, or the rows a `rowSpan` covers. It applies in every output (canvas, HTML, PDF), in rotated tables and in each slice of a table split across pages. Both are set per cell from the alignment buttons of the sandbox's table editor toolbar.

A table cell can also hold an image. `TableCell.image` names a bitmap or SVG resource by id (`{ "resourceId": "fig-arm", "width": 0.7 }`): the image is drawn inside the cell — never numbered, floated or captioned — fitted to the cell's inner width (or the fraction of it given by `width`, default `1`) with its aspect ratio kept, aligned like the cell's text, and any cell text runs under it. The row grows to hold it. In the sandbox's table editor the image button of the toolbar picks the resource for the active cell and a width field sets the fraction. An id that matches no image resource leaves the cell text-only.

A cell can carry its own fill. `TableCell.background` is a colour value (`{ "hex": "#c1dfd6", "model": "hex" }`, optionally linked to a document palette entry with `paletteId`) painted instead of the style's header or body background — a compatibility matrix shades its cells green, red and yellow this way. The sandbox's table editor sets it from the fill control of the toolbar. To key such fills, the caption, the note and any text block accept an inline **colour swatch**: `:swatch{color="#c1dfd6"}` (a hex, or the id of a palette entry — `:swatch{color="table-compatible"}`) sets a small square on the baseline, three quarters of the font size, filled with the colour and outlined in the text colour, so a note can read `:swatch{color="ok"}: compatible; :swatch{color="no"}: incompatible`. A colour that resolves to nothing draws an empty outline. The hex may carry an alpha channel (`#rrggbbaa`, `#rgba`), and `rgb()` / `rgba()` colours work too, so a translucent cell fill has a matching translucent key (see [Configuration › Transparency](/en/docs/configuration-fonts-colors-viewers#transparency)).

A map legend sets its keys at a size of its own, outlined and rounded, with room before the label: `:swatch{color="farmland" size=10pt border="0.75pt ink" radius=2pt gap=3pt}Farmland`. `size` is the side of the square (default 0.75 em); `border` is `none` or a width and a colour in either order (`1pt #333`, `ink 0.5pt`), and `borderWidth` and `borderColor` set one of the two on their own, over `border` (default an outline 6 % of the side in the text colour); `radius` rounds the corners, up to a circle; `gap` adds room after the square, part of the swatch, so the label can follow with no space (on its left in right-to-left text). Lengths take `pt`, `px`, `mm`, `cm`, `in`, `em` and `rem` (a bare number is in points; em is the size of the surrounding text), and the outline colour is a hex or a palette id like `color`. The line height does not change. The canvas, the HTML viewer, the PDF and the EPUB paint the same square (since postext 1.52).

SVG resources can additionally be recoloured for single-spot-colour printing via `diagramStyle.singleInk` (default `false`). When enabled, every colour in an SVG diagram is remapped to a luminance-mapped tint of `diagramStyle.inkColor` (which defaults to the main palette colour, `#295AA3`) — white maps to paper, black to the full ink — so figures reproduce faithfully when the document is printed with a single spot colour. See [Configuration › Diagram style](/en/docs/configuration-resources#diagram-style).

A malformed embed (missing or empty `id`, an unquoted or single-quoted `id`, attributes other than a composed page's `slot`) is not promoted to a resource block; it falls through to ordinary paragraph parsing and remains visible in the output. So does a well-formed embed line glued under a paragraph line without a blank line between them: it is read as part of that paragraph. Both raise a `malformedEmbed` warning — an **Embed set as text** entry in the sandbox's Checks panel.

Inline references are recognised inside any text block — paragraphs, headings, blockquotes, and list items — and may sit alongside bold, italic, inline code, and inline math.

#### Reference options

The `:ref` directive accepts three optional attributes, in any order. `style` selects how the computed label is rendered: `style="number"` prints the bare number (`1.7`), `style="full"` prints the type's full name plus the number (`Figure 1.7`), and when `style` is unset the type's `shortLabel` plus the number is used (`Fig. 1.7`). `case` changes the letter case of the label part only — `lower`, `upper` or `capitalize` — leaving the number untouched. `text` is a verbatim override that replaces any computed label and takes precedence over both `style` and `case`. If a `:ref` (or `::resource`) names an id with no matching resource, the label falls back to `?` (a `text=` label is printed instead, still with no number or link) and the sandbox raises an **unknown resource** warning. The engine records it as an `unknownResourceId` entry in the document's `contentWarnings`, with the reference's source range and page — also for a `:ref` inside a caption, a note or a table cell of a resource the text uses.

### First-reference numbering

A resource's number is assigned the **first time it is mentioned in reading order** — whether that first mention is a `::resource` block embed or an inline `:ref`. From then on, every reference to the same id prints that same number.

This means numbers follow the order the reader meets them, not the order resources were created in the panel:

- If you `:ref` a figure in the introduction and only embed it (`::resource`) two pages later, it still takes the introduction's number — the reference came first.
- Inserting a new reference *earlier* in the document automatically renumbers everything after it. There is no manual numbering to keep in sync.

Numbering is per resource type and respects each type's reset scope and counter format — see [Configuration › Resource types](/en/docs/configuration-resources#resource-types) for the template tokens (`{h1}`, `{n}`), `resetOn`, and `counterFormat`.

Only what the text references is counted: a resource that only a design draws (an image element of a chapter opener, say) gets no number and leaves the counter as it was. And `{h1}` counts every level-1 heading whose style is not `numbered: false`, even one whose `numberingTemplate` is empty, so an article whose only H1 is its title numbers its figures 1.1, 1.2… by default. [Configuration › What is numbered](/en/docs/configuration-resources#what-is-numbered) has both rules and the settings for Figure 1, 2….

It does so only when the float fits there within <code>layout.maxTopFraction</code> of the column (0.7 by default), when the earlier floats of its numbering sequence are already set, when no explicit break opens the page (a chapter's first page, a part, a <code>:::pagebreak</code>) and nothing spanning the page stands above the citing line, and when, with the float there, the citing line still lands on that page (or in the column the float heads); otherwise the float takes its usual slot. Turned floats, side-column floats and the rest of a table split across pages never move this way. Default: the type's <code>defaultPlacement</code>, then <code>layout.floatsAtCitingPage</code>. Since postext 1.25.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><code>pin</code></td>
      <td><code>&#123; page, column?, slot &#125;</code></td>
      <td>Not recommended (see <a href="#pins-are-not-recommended">Pins are not recommended</a>). A float set by hand, in the Resources panel or by moving it in the Sandbox canvas: <code>page</code> counts pages from the one that first cites it (<code>0</code> is that page, <code>3</code> at most), <code>column</code> the text column from 0 in reading order (any, when unset; a float across the page ignores it), <code>slot</code> is <code>"top"</code> or <code>"bottom"</code>. A pin is a preference. The float waits for that page and is offered that slot first; when the slot cannot hold it (too tall for the room left, taken by another float, or above its citation), it takes the usual slots of that page, and then goes on as any float. A pin for a page after the end of the chapter is dropped there, so the float and the ones after it in its numbering sequence are set before the chapter ends. Side-column floats, turned floats, plates and resources set in the text ignore it. Every pin the layout does not follow is reported as <code>floatPinMissed</code>, with its reason. Paged outputs only.</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

A float goes into the **first free slot after its first reference**, in reading order: the bottom of the column the reference sits in, then the top and the bottom of the next empty column of the same page, then the bands of the next page the flow opens (a page-span float takes the bottom of the page when every column still has room for it, else a band of the next page). A page that an inline figure or table opens counts too: a float waiting for that page takes its head, above the figure or table, when both fit on it; when they do not, the figure or table keeps the page and the float waits for the next one. Up to postext 1.4 such a page was skipped, and the float waited for the page after it even when both fitted. It is set at its size unless its `shrink` lets it scale to the room of a slot (see the table above), and it never lands before its reference. Floats of one numbering sequence appear in reference order: a figure that fits nowhere on a page holds up the figures behind it (a waiting table does not hold up a figure, nor the other way round), so figure 12 never appears before figure 11. A table that would otherwise wait is cut instead: offered the head of an empty column, it takes the rows that fit and continues in the next slot — the column beside it or the next page — with its header rows repeated (see `tableStyle.overflow`). A `top` or `auto` float with `citingPage` on may instead head the page (or the column) where its citing line lands, the text above the reference moving down under it: it is then painted above its reference on the same page, never on an earlier one, and still read after it in a tagged PDF and in the HTML. The engine settles this over a few extra passes and keeps it only while the citing line stays on that page.

Floats never escape their chapter: at a chapter opener (a heading level with `breakBefore` or `span: 'page'`), a `:::part`, a callout style with `floatBarrier: true` (the closing "key points" box of a chapter) and at the end of the document, every float still pending is placed first — in the page's free slots, or on pages opened ahead of the boundary. A float first cited by the opener itself (a chapter title that names its figure), or by the first block after a `:::part`, belongs to the new chapter: it lands after that line, like any other. A figure or table that asked for the head of a page may then take the foot of the chapter's closing page, under its balanced columns, rather than a page of its own. A `:::pagebreak` simply sends pending floats to the page that follows it.

Under a page-wide opener set over two or more text columns, the opener's own column counts as empty at its head while nothing follows the opener in it: a floated box fenced right after the opener, or a resource embedded there with `::resource`, takes the head of the first column, under the opener and level with the heads of the other columns (across several columns from it with `columns`), and the column's text starts under it. A float cited in running text still follows its citing line. Up to postext 1.24 the first column offered no slot there and such a float landed from the second column on; `layout.floatsUnderOpener: false` keeps that (see [Configuration › Layout](/en/docs/configuration-page-layout#layout)).

Side boxes (`span="side"`) keep to their chapter too. A box that the side column of its page cannot hold moves to the side column of the next page; if the chapter, or the document, ends first, each box still waiting is set in the side column of a page opened after the text, in the order of their fences, and a side figure cited after them stands behind them. Such a page holds no text, so the build reports every box and figure on it (`afterText`). A box taller than an empty side column is set anyway and reported (`calloutOverflow`), and anything that still finds no page is reported too (`unplaced`) rather than dropped. Up to postext 1.24 only the first of those pages was opened and the boxes left were lost without a warning.

Float bands are corrected against the baseline grid so the surrounding text keeps the page-wide vertical rhythm. A **top** band grows its bottom margin to the next grid line, so the text below the float stays aligned with the neighbouring columns and the facing page. A **bottom** float is anchored so its caption's last line shares a baseline with the last text line of the other columns (captionless content aligns its bottom edge to the last grid slot) — full pages therefore end at the same height across columns and across facing pages.

On the closing page of a chapter — and of the document — nothing follows the page-wide figures and tables set below the last band of text, so they move up to sit one float gap under it, stacked in their order, instead of leaving white between the text and a figure at the page foot. That holds for a `position: 'bottom'` float too. To keep them at the foot, as on every other page, set `layout.hugClosingFloats: false` (see [Configuration › Layout](/en/docs/configuration-page-layout#layout)). Pages with a side column never move them.

All three backends — the canvas preview, the HTML viewer, and the PDF output — render resources. In the HTML backend, image payloads live out-of-band, so the host supplies them through the `resourceImageUrl(fileId)` resolver option; when the resolver is missing (or returns nothing for a file), the resource renders as a neutral placeholder box so the layout stays stable. A video's own file comes through `resourceVideoUrl(fileId)` the same way (see [Videos](#videos)).

#### Where a float can land

"Never before its reference" counts from the line that cites the float: the float waits until that line is set, and takes the first free slot after it in reading order — the head of the page the line is set on comes before it. A side float (`span: 'side'`, in the float-only side column of a one-and-a-half layout) is the exception: it stacks in the side column beside the text that cites it, and may sit higher on the page than the citing line. For a figure cited on page 5:

- A chapter opener page follows the same rules: a page-span `auto` or `bottom` float cited in the opener's first paragraph can take that page's foot band, under its text; a `top` one opens the next page. To have the picture on the page that cites it, use `auto` or `bottom`, or draw it as an image element of the opener's design.
- Two column floats cited in the same paragraph take the next two slots: typically the first the foot of the citing column, the second the head of the next empty column — side by side on the page.
- In a single-column layout a page-span float is a column float, and the same rules hold: a `top` float cited on a page lands at the head of the next one.
- A paragraph that runs from one page onto the next counts from its citing line too. When the paragraph begins at the foot of page 5 and the reference falls on page 6 — or the whole paragraph moves to page 6, because not enough of its lines fit at the foot of page 5 — the reference is on page 6: a `top` float opens page 7, and an `auto` one takes the foot of page 6 when there is room for it. The same holds between the columns of a page, and for a box split across pages: a figure that its second part cites waits for that part.
- **Changed in postext 1.5.** Up to postext 1.4 a float was queued when the layout reached the paragraph that cites it, so the page such a paragraph ran onto (or moved to) could open with the float, above the line holding the reference. Such a figure now lands a page later, or at the foot of the page when it asks for `auto`; a page-count change can follow.

#### Pins are not recommended

Postext lays a book out by rules: each float takes the first free slot after its citation, within the limits its placement sets, and the whole book is laid out again when the text, the page size, the number of columns or the edition's language changes. A pin is tied to the geometry of one layout. It names a page counted from the citation and a column by its number, and both stop meaning the same thing once the text moves. Add a paragraph and the citation goes to another page; translate the book and the line breaks change; set an edition in one column and column 1 no longer exists. The pin then fails, and the layout reports it, or it puts the float somewhere that no longer serves the page. A pin is stored on the resource, so every edition and language of the book shares it.

The rules below keep a figure near its text in every edition:

- `position: "top"` or `"bottom"` (on the resource or its type) restricts the slots a float takes to heads or feet.
- `citingPage: true`, or `layout.floatsAtCitingPage` for the whole book, lets a top or automatic float head the page or the column where its citing line lands.
- A box with `keepTogether` keeps together what it holds, a picture embedded in it with `::resource` included; `floatBarrier` on a box style keeps the floats cited before it on its side.
- `shrink: "slot"` (or `"page"`) lets a picture scale down to the room its slot has, and a [safe area](#safe-area) lets the engine crop it to fit before it scales.

If a page still needs a figure moved, pin it as the last step, for one finished edition in one language, and check the Checks panel for `floatPinMissed` whenever the text or the design changes.

### Text wrap

A picture, a table or a box narrower than its column can stand at one side of it with the text running beside it, as in a magazine or a newsletter. Give its placement a `wrap` side and, if you like, a `width`:

```json
{ "id": "bed", "typeId": "figure", "kind": "svg", "caption": "The new planting bed.",
  "placement": { "position": "here", "wrap": "right", "width": 0.4 } }
```

- **Inline (`"here"`).** The resource sits at its side of the column, its top level with the top of the next line at the `::resource` line, and the paragraphs, quotations and list items that follow run beside it at the measure left over, then under it at the full measure. A paragraph that starts beside it and ends under it stays one paragraph: no break, no extra space at the picture's foot. Every line stays on the baseline grid; the room under the picture is its gap rounded up to whole grid lines.
- **A column float.** A `span: "column"` float one column wide, at the head (`"top"`, or `"auto"` at the head of a column) or the foot (`"bottom"`, `"auto"`) of a column, is placed in its band as any float; the column's first lines, or its last ones, then run beside it. On a page of two columns the float belongs to its column, and the other runs full.
- **A box.** A `:::callout` takes the same settings as attributes: `:::callout{wrap="right" width=0.4}` sets a pull quote or a sidebar where it occurs, with the text after it beside it, and `placement="top"` or `"bottom"` with `wrap` floats it to the head or foot of a column. A wrapped box is laid out whole and never splits.
- **What goes under it.** A heading, a display formula, a figure or table, a box and a poem do not run beside a wrapped item: they go under it, and the white space beside the item above them stays empty. Leaving the column (its foot, a `:::columnbreak`, a page break) ends the wrap there: the item belongs to its column. When the block right after an inline resource cannot run beside it, the resource takes its band, as without `wrap`.
- **Gap and limits.** `wrapGap` (else `layout.wrap.gap`, else one body line) separates the item, caption included, from the text beside it and from the text under it. Where the text beside it would be narrower than `layout.wrap.minTextWidth` (12 em of body text), or the item is shorter than `layout.wrap.minLinesBeside` lines (2), it takes its band whole and a `textWrap` warning says so. An inline one too tall for the room left in its column moves on to the next with its anchor, with the same warning.
- **Sides.** `left` and `right` are sides of the body flow, as for `align`: in a right-to-left book `left` is the sheet's right. `start` and `end` name the same sides.
- **Outputs.** The canvas, the HTML viewer, the PDF and the fixed-layout EPUB set the lines as they were laid out; a tagged PDF reads the picture or the box before the paragraph that runs beside it. A reflowable EPUB floats it to its side with CSS, at the same share of the text width.
- **Not yet.** Text on both sides of a picture, wrapping inside a box's own content, and wrapping in vertical text (the item keeps its band, with a warning). Columns with a wrapped item are left out of column balancing.

#### Contour wrap

A cut-out illustration (a figure on a transparent background, a drawn object, a map of an island) looks better when the text follows its outline than when it stops at the edge of its box. `"wrap": "contour"` does that: each line beside the picture is set as short as the picture's shape is wide at that line's height, plus a margin, and a line that passes clear of the shape takes the whole measure.

```json
{ "id": "pear", "typeId": "figure", "kind": "bitmap",
  "bitmap": { "fileId": "pear.png", "format": "png", "width": 600, "height": 800 },
  "placement": { "position": "here", "wrap": "contour", "align": "right", "width": 0.42 },
  "contour": { "threshold": 0.5, "margin": { "value": 2, "unit": "mm" } } }
```

- **Where the shape comes from.** A bitmap's shape is its alpha channel: the pixels at least `contour.threshold` opaque (0 to 1, default 0.5). A polygon in `contour.points`, at least three points in fractions of the whole picture from its top-left corner (`{ "x": 0.5, "y": 0 }` is the middle of its top edge), wins over the alpha channel and is the way to give an SVG a shape. `"alpha": false` ignores the alpha channel. A JPEG has no transparency, so without a polygon it runs round its box.
- **Margin.** `contour.margin` is the space kept between the shape and the text, at the sides and above and below it. Unset, it is the wrap gap (`placement.wrapGap`, then `layout.wrap.gap`, then one body line).
- **Side.** The picture stands at the side its `align` names (`"right"` or `"end"`; anything else is the left), and the lines run on the other side. Everything else is as for a side wrap: an inline embed or a one-column float, the same limits and the same `textWrap` warnings.
- **Crop and scale.** The shape follows the picture as it is set: scaled with it, and cut where a safe area crops it. A caption or a note under the picture keeps its box.
- **Right to left and CJK.** On a right-to-left page the shape turns with the page, as the picture is painted unturned at its mirrored place. Under a CJK character grid each line's inset is a whole number of characters, so the lines keep to the grid.
- **The trace.** The layout reads pictures by their size, never by their pixels, so the alpha channel travels with the resource: `contour.traced`, the shape in rows, which the host makes from the file with `traceResourceContour(resource, bytes, mime)` (the engine reads PNG itself, in the browser and in Node; another format goes through the browser's decoder). Reading a bundle traces every picture that asks for it, and the Sandbox traces a picture when its wrap, its file or its threshold changes. A bundle never stores the trace. A picture whose shape is not known (an SVG with no polygon, a bitmap with no transparency, or an untraced one) runs round its box, with a `textWrap` warning whose reason is `noContour`: give it a polygon, use a PNG with transparency, or read it through `readBundle` or the Sandbox so it is traced.
- **Frames.** A frame of a composed page that pushes text (`exclude: "push"`) takes `"wrap": "contour"` too: see [Composed pages › Frames that push text](/en/docs/composed-pages#frames-that-push-text).
- **Not yet.** Text on both sides of a shape that stands in the middle of the measure (the text takes the wider side), and polygon editing in the Sandbox.

### Bitmap size

A bitmap declares its pixels: `bitmap.width` and `bitmap.height` are the file's pixel counts, not a print size. How many of them go to an inch decides how large the picture is set before its slot caps it. There are three ways to size one:

- **Its pixels at the page's dpi** (the default). Without a resolution, one pixel of the picture is one pixel of the layout (`page.dpi`, 300 unless set): a 2400 px photograph is 8 in (203.2 mm) wide on a 300 dpi page, and twice that on a 150 dpi one.
- **A resolution.** `bitmap.resolution` is the picture's ppi at its natural print size, so its natural width is `width × page.dpi / resolution` on a page of any dpi: 2400 px at `resolution: 300` is 203.2 mm wide on a page laid out at 150 dpi or at 300. `layout.bitmapResolution` gives every bitmap without one of its own a resolution: a number (`300`), or `'file'`, the resolution its file states (`bitmap.fileResolution`: a PNG's `pHYs`, a JPEG's JFIF or EXIF density, a WebP's EXIF; for a picture imported from Word, the size Word printed it at). Cameras, screenshots and web exports often state 72 or 96 ppi when they know nothing of a print size, so under `'file'` those two count as unset and the picture keeps its pixels at the page's dpi; a 72 declared on the resource itself is taken as written. `'document'`, the default, is the first way.
- **A share of the column.** `placement.width` narrows a float or an inline embed to a fraction of its column (or page), and a picture larger than that share is scaled down to it, whatever its pixels (see [Placement](#placement)).

In every case the slot caps the picture, keeping its proportions, and a smaller picture is never enlarged. The same natural size applies to a picture in a table cell and to a design's `image` element with an `'auto'` size. The PDF embeds the file as it is, at its placed size. The [preflight](/en/docs/configuration-pdf-output#preflight) checks the resolution each picture prints at, and the Sandbox's [Resources panel](/en/docs/sandbox#resources-panel) shows it for each picture. Since postext 1.24.

The slot of a picture in the flow (`position: 'here'`, or a column float) is its column, and the default page has two columns (`layout.layoutType: 'double'`): a picture sized for the whole page comes out at about half its print size there. Set such a page in one column (`layout.layoutType: 'single'`), float the picture across the page (`placement: { position: 'top', span: 'page' }`) or put it in a [composed page](/en/docs/composed-pages)'s frame. When a bitmap with a print size (its own `resolution`, or a number in `layout.bitmapResolution`) is held at under half of it by a column narrower than the page, the layout reports `pictureBelowPrintSize`. Since postext 1.37.

```json
{ "id": "harbour", "typeId": "figure", "kind": "bitmap", "caption": "The harbour at dawn.",
  "bitmap": { "fileId": "harbour.jpg", "format": "jpeg", "width": 2400, "height": 1600, "resolution": 300 } }
```

`bitmapInfo(bytes)` (from `postext` and `postext/bundle`) reads a file's pixels and the resolution it states, `{ width, height, resolution?: { x, y, source } }`; a bundle opened under `'file'` fills `fileResolution` with it, and the Sandbox keeps it for every picture it uploads.

### Safe area

A bitmap or SVG resource can mark a **safe area**: the rectangle of the picture that holds what matters (a person and a strip of the landscape behind them, the plotted part of a chart) and is always shown. It is the resource's `safeArea`, four fractions (0–1) of the picture's intrinsic size measured from its top-left corner: `x` and `width` are fractions of its width, `y` and `height` of its height. The name says what has to be kept; a crop box would say where to cut.

```ts
const harbour: Resource = {
  id: 'harbour', typeId: 'figure', kind: 'bitmap', caption: 'The harbour at dawn.',
  createdAt: 0, updatedAt: 0,
  bitmap: { fileId: 'harbour.jpg', format: 'jpeg', width: 2400, height: 1600 },
  // the boats and the quay: 18–68 % of the width, 25–85 % of the height
  safeArea: { x: 0.18, y: 0.25, width: 0.5, height: 0.6 },
};
```

Only a picture with a safe area is ever cropped. The engine may then show it at any aspect ratio between the whole picture and the safe area: taller than the picture's own ratio by cropping the sides, at most down to the safe area's width, or shorter by cropping top and bottom, at most down to its height. The width of the figure never changes, only its height. What lies outside the safe area is cropped on both sides in proportion to the margins there, so a subject left of centre stays left of centre. Set 60 mm wide, the harbour above is 40 mm tall when whole, and can be shown anywhere from 24 mm tall (top and bottom cropped down to 60 % of the height) to 80 mm tall (the sides cropped down to half the width). Values past the picture's edges are clamped to it; an area with a value that is not a number or a side under 2 % of the picture, or one that covers the whole picture, is ignored, and so is the field on a table. Without a safe area a picture is always shown whole, as before.

The engine uses that room in four places:

- **The room left in a column.** An inline picture (`position: 'here'`) a little too tall for the room left in its column, including one whose caption would run past the foot of the page, is cropped within its safe area to stay there, instead of moving on to the next column or page and leaving this one short. So is a page-span embed (`span: 'page'`) a little too tall for the band left under an opener, keeping the widow minimum of text under it.
- **Pages shorter than the figure.** With `layout.fitFiguresToPage`, a picture too tall for the page is first cropped within its safe area, keeping its width, and only scaled down if it is still too tall (see [Configuration › Layout](/en/docs/configuration-page-layout#layout)).
- **Bands shorter than the figure.** A float whose `placement.shrink` scales it to the room of its slot is cropped within its safe area first, keeping its width, and only then scaled down; the `flexFigure` lever below never grows it past that room.
- **Column balancing.** A picture with a safe area set inline in a short column, or floated at the head or the foot of that column over that column alone, grows by whole baseline-grid lines to fill the column's empty lines. A taller picture leaves no visible hole, so this lever, `flexFigure`, is tried right after the box closing the column and before any space is added above headings or after lists. A float at the head of a column on a chapter's closing page or in a closing band does not grow, since those column heads stay level (see [Configuration › Column balancing](/en/docs/configuration-page-flow#column-balancing)).

The VDT records the crop on the resource block: `bodySource` is the part of the picture shown in `bodyRect`, in the same fractions (absent when the whole picture fills it), and `bodyFlex` gives the px the body could still shrink and grow and the px the levers already changed it by (`{ shrink, grow, delta }`). The canvas preview and the PDF clip to the body and draw the whole picture at its uncropped size; the HTML viewer and fixed-layout EPUB set the image with `object-fit: cover` and a matching `object-position`; a reflowable EPUB, which has no page to fill, shows the whole picture. A host that draws pictures itself can use the helpers `postext` exports: `resourceSafeArea` and `normalizeSafeArea` (the area as the engine reads it, or `undefined`), `safeAreaHeightRange` (the shortest and tallest body at a width), `safeAreaSource` (the part shown at a given height) and `uncroppedPictureBox` (where to draw the whole picture before clipping), with the type `ResourceSafeArea`. In the Sandbox the field is **Safe area** on a picture in the [Resources panel](/en/docs/sandbox#resources-panel).

Recipe Nº 116 in the Cookbook, [Photos that grow to fill a short column](/en/cookbook/photos-fill-short-columns), sets a two-column magazine feature twice, without and with safe areas, so you can compare the pages one beside the other.


### Manual crop

A bitmap, SVG or video poster can also be cropped by hand: `crop`, in the same fractions as the safe area, is the part of the picture the figure always shows, whatever the page. The figure takes the crop's ratio. The safe area, if any, is read inside the crop: the engine may cut further where the page needs it, never outside the crop, and `bodySource` is the composition of both. Mark the safe area inside the crop; the part of it outside the crop is never shown. Unset, the whole picture. The crop reaches every output: the canvas, the PDF, the HTML viewer, both EPUBs (a reflowable book sets the picture, or a video's poster and the film in its frame, in a box at the crop's ratio), the IDML and the formatted Word export, a picture in a table cell, the art of a comic panel (the panel's window is chosen inside the crop) and a design image that shows a resource. The semantic Word export inserts the whole picture, as it does with the safe area, and keeps the crop in the book. Comic anchors are checked against the safe area only (`comicAnchorOutsideSafeArea`): keep a speaker inside the crop too. In the Sandbox, *Crop* beside the safe area opens a frame to drag, nudge with the arrows and preview; an agent sets it through `sandbox_save_resource` (`marks.crop`).

```ts
const portrait: Resource = {
  /* … */
  bitmap: { fileId: 'portrait.jpg', format: 'jpeg', width: 3000, height: 2000 },
  // the middle half, top to bottom
  crop: { x: 0.25, y: 0, width: 0.5, height: 1 },
};
```

### Speaker anchors and avoid zones

A picture that is a comic panel's art can also say where its people are, for the lettering (see [Comics](/en/docs/document-format-directives#comics)). `anchors` lists one point per speaker and `avoid` the regions no balloon covers, both in fractions of the picture like the safe area:

```ts
const radio: Resource = {
  id: 'lh-radio', typeId: 'figure', kind: 'bitmap', createdAt: 0, updatedAt: 0,
  bitmap: { fileId: 'lh-radio.jpg', format: 'jpeg', width: 1000, height: 1000 },
  safeArea: { x: 0, y: 0.36, width: 0.62, height: 0.56 },
  anchors: [
    // the keeper: mouth, head (for thought balloons) and face
    { id: 'tomas', x: 0.345, y: 0.52, head: { x: 0.38, y: 0.44 }, face: { x: 0.29, y: 0.4, width: 0.16, height: 0.18 } },
  ],
  avoid: [{ x: 0, y: 0.44, width: 0.22, height: 0.26 }], // the radio
};
```

An anchor's `id` is the speaker key of the script (`tomas: …`), or `sfx` for the point the panel's sound effects gather at; `x` and `y` are the mouth a speech balloon's tail points at, `head` the point a thought balloon's bubbles point at (the mouth when unset) and `face` a rectangle no balloon covers. The engine maps every point through the panel's crop and mirror, so a tail stays on its speaker whatever shape the panel takes; an anchor outside the safe area raises `comicAnchorOutsideSafeArea`, because a crop may cut it off. A third list, `faces`, holds the faces of people who do not speak, as rectangles like those of `avoid`: no balloon covers them, where an avoid zone gives way when a panel has no other room. Anchors belong to the picture and serve every translation of the comic. Comic panels read them for their lettering. A figure's labels use the anchors too, and since postext 1.42 the `avoid` zones of a map or a diagram mark its symbols: a label that runs onto one raises `labelOverlap` (see [Composed pages](/en/docs/composed-pages-figures#labeloverlap)). In the Sandbox they are marked in the **Safe area & lettering** dialog of the [Resources panel](/en/docs/sandbox#resources-panel).


An anchor that a figure's label uses can also set the label away from its point, `labelAt: { x, y }` in fractions of the picture, and draw a line back to it, `leader: true` or a leader look; a connector of a composed page can end at the anchor's point with `anchor: '<id>'`. See [Configuration › Label fields of an anchor](/en/docs/configuration-composed-pages#label-fields-of-an-anchor). Since postext 1.48.

### Symbols in captions, notes and cells

A caption, a note or a table cell can hold a small symbol on its line: `:icon[alt]{glyph="⊙"}` sets a character (in the face `font="…"` names, else the text's), `:icon[alt]{resource="town-mark"}` a bitmap or SVG resource, and `:icon[alt]{line="dashed"}` a sample of a line, for a table that explains the lines of a map. `color="…"` tints it with a hex colour or a palette entry, and `size="…"` sets its height in ems (default `0.75`, a capital's height). The text in brackets is what screen readers and the tagged PDF read in its place; empty brackets make it decoration.

```ts
{ id: 'mapa-politico', typeId: 'figure', kind: 'bitmap',
  caption: 'Capitals :icon[capital]{glyph="⊙" font="Noto Sans Math"} and towns :icon[town]{resource="town-mark"} of Varenia' }
```

Symbols are read in captions, notes, table cells and figure labels (see [Composed pages › Leaders, legends and symbols](/en/docs/composed-pages-figures#leaders-legends-and-symbols)); in body text and headings the directive stays text in this release. Since postext 1.48.

### Videos

A **video** resource (`kind: 'video'`) is a YouTube or Vimeo video, or a video of your own: a file kept in the book (MP4, WebM) or one at a web address, a file on a server or an **HLS stream** (an `.m3u8` playlist). It is placed, captioned, floated and cited like a picture, and numbered in a sequence of its own: the built-in `video` type prints *Video 1.1*, *Video 1.2*… beside *Figure 1.1* and *Table 1.1* (see [Configuration › Resource types](/en/docs/configuration-resources#resource-types)). **The poster.** It is set at the measure of its slot (or the `placement.width` fraction of it), in its own ratio, whatever its pixel size: a screen-sized frame is enlarged rather than printed small. Like a bitmap it can carry a [safe area](#safe-area) and follow `placement.align`. A video without a poster prints a dark box in the video's ratio (16:9 when nothing says) with its overlays, and raises a `videoWithoutPoster` content warning. In the Sandbox the poster of a YouTube or Vimeo video is fetched from the platform when its address is entered, and a file's is a frame you pick in the video.

**The address.** The QR code and the PDF link open the video's page, `https://youtu.be/<id>` or `https://vimeo.com/<id>`, from `start`. A file has no page: they open its production address, which is also where a video with no file of its own plays from (an MP4 on a server, an HLS stream). Without one, print gets no QR code and no link (`videoWithoutUrl`), and an HTML or EPUB output that does not carry the file shows the poster. A YouTube or Vimeo address that names no video of that platform raises `videoUrlInvalid`; the poster is shown in place of a player.

**The players.** The player options (controls, download button, full screen, speed menu, picture in picture, casting, autoplay, a muted start, loop, preload, privacy-enhanced embeds) are set for the whole book in `videoStyle.player` and for one video in `video.player`; each player honours what it can (see [Configuration › Video style](/en/docs/configuration-resources#video-style)). The HTML backend takes the playable URL of a file through the `resourceVideoUrl(fileId)` option, as it takes pictures through `resourceImageUrl`, and falls back to the production address. Its `videos: { files, streams, hls }` option chooses between player and poster per source, over `videoStyle.html` (`hls`, a video played from an HLS address, follows `files` unless set); the EPUB writer uses it. A `<video>` that plays an HLS address carries `data-pt-hls`, so a host can give it a player in browsers without native HLS (Safari and recent Chrome play it as it is). In a right-to-left book the player and the poster's overlays are turned back like a picture, so the QR code reads.

**In the VDT.** The resource block of a video has `kind: 'video'`, its poster as `fileId` / `format` (drawn like a bitmap), and `video` (`VDTResourceVideo`): the `link` a reader is sent to, the YouTube or Vimeo `embedUrl` with the player options applied, a file's `fileId`, the `mimeType` of a video of your own (of its file or of its address alone, `application/vnd.apple.mpegurl` for HLS), the `start` / `end` range, the resolved `player`, `linkPoster`, `html`, and the overlays `playMark` and `qr` (the QR modules as rows of `'0'` / `'1'`), with rects relative to the body's top-left corner. A host drawing videos itself can use the helpers `postext` exports: `parseVideoUrl`, `videoWatchUrl`, `resourceVideoLink`, `videoEmbedUrl`, `videoEmbedAllow`, `videoElementAttributes`, `mediaFragment`, `videoMimeType`, `videoFormatOfUrl`, `resourceVideoFormat`, `isHlsMimeType`, `HLS_MIME_TYPE`, `youtubePosterUrls`, `encodeQr`, `layoutVideo`, `playMarkTriangle` and `qrModuleRuns`.

**In a bundle.** A `.postext` carries a file (`resources/<id>.mp4`) and the poster (`resources/<id>.poster.jpg`): the manifest entry names them in `file` and `poster`, gives the poster's size in `width` / `height`, and keeps the rest in `video` without file ids. `bundleVideoUrl(bundle)` is the `resourceVideoUrl` resolver over a bundle's files. A video played from an address alone carries no file: its entry has only the poster. In a bilingual bundle a language can give a video its own `poster` and `video` (another cut, at its own address) in `localized[…].resources`.

#### Videos on Folio's pages

In the [3D book](/en/docs/configuration-html-folio-epub#a-3d-book-postext-folio) a video of your own plays on the page it is printed on. A click on the poster starts it there, whatever the pointer does on the book (turning pages, orbiting the view or selecting text): the page's shader draws the video's frames over the poster, so the picture bends and curls with the paper while the leaf is turned, and it goes on playing during the turn. A click on a playing video pauses it, a click on a paused one resumes it, and Space or Enter does the same for the first video on the open spread. Starting another video stops the one playing (it goes back to its poster), and a video stops when the book comes to rest on a spread that no longer shows it, or at its `end`. The pointer turns into a hand over a video it can play; a drag that starts on one still turns the page or the view. A video set to play on its own (`video.player.autoplay`) starts by itself the first time the book comes to rest on its spread, once the book has settled (a host laying it out in steps included), and never again in that viewer.

A video that plays alongside the others (`video.player.exclusive: false`, since postext 1.18) changes both rules. Starting it stops only the exclusive videos, so several such videos play at once; starting an exclusive video stops it too. With `autoplay` it starts, muted, each time the book comes to rest on its spread, and stops when the spread is turned away: the silent loops (`loop`) of a page run together every time the reader turns to it.

- **What plays.** A file (the host turns its `fileId` into a URL with the `videoUrl` option, an object URL in the Sandbox) or, without one, its address: MP4, WebM or an HLS stream. An HLS stream plays through [hls.js](https://github.com/video-dev/hls.js) wherever the browser has Media Source Extensions (loaded on the first play; an optional peer dependency of `postext-folio`), capped at the variant nearest the picture's size on screen, so a picture a few hundred pixels tall never pulls a 4K variant; the browser's own HLS is the fallback. Its requests carry a `pt-cors` query of their own, so the browser's cache never hands them a copy another player fetched without CORS. YouTube and Vimeo play in iframes, which WebGL cannot draw: a click on their poster turns the page as anywhere else.
- **Cross-origin reads.** WebGL draws a video only when its server allows the read (CORS, `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`). A video that cannot be read stays a poster, and `onVideo` hears `error`.
- **Sound.** The click lets the video play with its sound; a browser that still refuses gets it muted. A video that starts on its own has its sound once the reader has clicked or typed on the page (turning to it counts); before that it starts muted, and a click on it unmutes it before the next one pauses it. `video.player.muted` starts it muted, `loop` plays it again, and `start` / `end` give the part that plays.
- **On every page.** The picture is drawn where the layout puts it: turned with a rotated resource, upright on a vertical page, and unmirrored in a right-to-left book, like the poster. A poster cropped within its [safe area](#safe-area) crops the video the same way.
- **Without WebGL2**, or with reduced motion, the video plays in an HTML `<video>` laid over the page.

```ts
const book = createFolioFromDocument(container, doc, {
  // An uploaded video's playable URL (an object URL over its bytes).
  videoUrl: (fileId) => urls.get(fileId),
  onVideo: ({ resourceId, state }) => console.log(resourceId, state), // playing | paused | stopped | error
});
book.stopVideo();
```

`videos: false` turns it off. `toggleVideoAt({ page, x, y })` does what a click there does, and `pageVideoSpots(page, doc)` lists where each video lies on a page, for a host that draws its own controls.
